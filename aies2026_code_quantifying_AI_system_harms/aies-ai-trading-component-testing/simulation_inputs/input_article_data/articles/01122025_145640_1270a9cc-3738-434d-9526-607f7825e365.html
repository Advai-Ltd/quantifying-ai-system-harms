Mortgages-Backed Securities Rally Intensifies as Refi Wave and Passive Demand Tighten Spreads

Agency MBS surged in early December as a fresh drop in mortgage rates and heavy TBA buying compressed spreads across the coupon stack, delivering one of the strongest windows for mortgage-backed paper since the mid‑2020s. The rally has attracted broad investor participation — from bank balance sheets to foreign accounts — and is reshaping relative-value conversations across fixed income, with analysts increasingly favoring MBS for yield and convexity-managed exposure.

Rachel Moreno, Senior Markets Correspondent in New York 01/12/2025 09:15:00

A pronounced rally in agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS) gathered momentum overnight as 30-year TBA prices climbed and option‑adjusted spreads (OAS) tightened sharply. Dealers reported accelerated buying in 2.5%–4.0% coupon classes, with the 30-year conventional stack tightening roughly 18 basis points over the past week versus U.S. Treasuries. The move followed a rapid fall in conforming mortgage rates, which have retraced after a late‑autumn spike, reigniting refinance economics for a meaningful cohort of borrowers.

Market participants cited a mix of technical and fundamental drivers: active TBA demand from passthrough-focused mutual funds and ETFs, renewed hedge-fund participation in convexity-driven trades, and larger-than-expected allocations from foreign accounts seeking yield in a volatile sovereign bond market. Primary dealers noted a pickup in net long positions and said that agency issuance cleared more easily than anticipated, with mean reoffering concessions narrower, signaling strong bid-side liquidity.

Concrete market metrics illustrate the bullish setup. Aggregate weekly TBA volumes expanded to an estimated $72 billion, up 12% week-on-week, while the 30-year fixed mortgage rate fell to a simulated 4.15% from 4.45% two weeks prior — a move that lifted projected prepayment speeds on refinance-sensitive cohorts to roughly 160–190 PSA depending on vintage. Agency OAS for top-of-the-stack coupons compressed to approximately +42 bps, narrowing versus Treasuries and outperforming similarly rated corporate credit on a total-return basis.

Analysts who track the sector point to an improved risk/reward for structured agency paper. "With the bulk of near-term policy uncertainty reflected in front-end yields, MBS offers an attractive carry-to-risk profile — particularly in coupon classes where prepayment sensitivity is manageable," said Daniel Kwon, Head of Mortgage Research at Crestmont Analytics. Kwon highlighted that active managers are selectively lengthening duration in 3.0%–3.5% coupons while using TBA basis trades to hedge convexity exposure.

Institutional appetite has been further amplified by technical relief: mortgage servicing rights (MSR) hedging flows have eased relative to earlier quarters as originations stabilized, reducing forced selling pressure. Banks and insurance companies have redeployed cash into agency passthroughs and CMOs with bullet-like structures that preserve spread pickup without large negative convexity profiles. Looking ahead, strategists expect continued inflows if mortgage rates remain range-bound or decline modestly, but caution that a rapid, sustained drop in rates would elevate prepayment risk and shorten durations, capping upside for longer coupons.

Key developments

  • Agency OAS tightens ~18 bps week-on-week, with top-of-stack coupons trading near +42 bps to Treasuries.
  • 30-year fixed mortgage rate falls to ~4.15%, lifting refinance economics and increasing projected PSA speeds to 160–190 for refinance-sensitive vintages.
  • TBA weekly trading volumes expand to an estimated $72 billion as mutual funds, ETFs and foreign buyers increase allocations to agency MBS.

""The supply/demand dynamic has flipped in favor of MBS: lower rates and strong technical demand are delivering outsized returns relative to duration-adjusted benchmarks. We’re constructive on agency coupon exposure but are selectively guarding against prepayment convexity with targeted hedges,""

Daniel Kwon, Head of Mortgage Research, Crestmont Analytics
