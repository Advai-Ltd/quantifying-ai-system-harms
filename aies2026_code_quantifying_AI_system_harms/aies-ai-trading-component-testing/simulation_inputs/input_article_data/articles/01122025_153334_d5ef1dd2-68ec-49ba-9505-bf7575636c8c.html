Mortgage-Backed Securities Tumble as Fed Signals Extended Higher Rates, Prepayment Risks Mount

Mortgage-backed securities suffered their steepest decline in eight months as Federal Reserve officials indicated interest rates would remain elevated longer than anticipated, triggering widespread selling across agency and non-agency MBS markets. Spreads widened dramatically while prepayment speeds slowed to multi-year lows, raising concerns about duration extension risk and deteriorating collateral performance amid a stagnating housing market.

Michael Chen and Patricia Rodriguez in New York 15/03/2024 14:32:18

Mortgage-backed securities markets experienced a sharp selloff Wednesday as investors recalibrated expectations following hawkish commentary from Federal Reserve officials, with the Bloomberg U.S. MBS Index declining 1.8% in its worst single-day performance since July. Agency MBS spreads widened 12 basis points to Treasuries, reaching levels not seen since the regional banking crisis of early 2023, while trading volumes surged to $285 billion as institutional investors rushed to reduce exposure ahead of quarter-end.

The selloff intensified concerns about duration extension risk, with prepayment speeds on Fannie Mae 30-year 6% coupons dropping to just 4.2 CPR (Constant Prepayment Rate) in February, down from 6.8 CPR three months earlier. This dramatic slowdown means borrowers are refinancing at the slowest pace in two decades, forcing MBS investors to hold securities significantly longer than anticipated and extending portfolio durations at precisely the wrong time in the rate cycle. Goldman Sachs strategists now estimate the effective duration of the agency MBS market has expanded to 6.2 years, up from 4.1 years just six months ago.

Non-agency residential mortgage-backed securities fared even worse, with credit spreads on recent vintage prime jumbo deals widening 28 basis points as liquidity evaporated. The ICE Non-Agency RMBS Index fell 2.4%, with particularly acute pressure on securities backed by adjustable-rate mortgages and interest-only loans. Dealers reported virtually no bid for legacy non-agency paper from the 2005-2007 vintages, with some tranches declining 3-5 points in price. Market participants attributed the deterioration to mounting concerns about borrower stress as ARM resets loom and home price appreciation stalls in key metropolitan markets.

The commercial mortgage-backed securities market mirrored the residential downturn, with the CMBX Series 15 BBB- index widening 45 basis points to 485 basis points over SOFR. Particular weakness emerged in office-heavy CMBS tranches, where delinquency rates climbed to 6.7% in February from 5.2% in January, according to Trepp data. Special servicer transfers accelerated, with $12.8 billion in commercial real estate loans moved to special servicing during the month, the highest level since the pandemic. Analysts at Morgan Stanley downgraded their outlook for the sector, citing deteriorating fundamentals and tightening lending standards that are preventing troubled borrowers from refinancing maturing debt.

The Federal Reserve's own MBS holdings added another layer of complexity to the market dynamics. The central bank continues its quantitative tightening program, allowing up to $35 billion in agency MBS to roll off its balance sheet monthly through principal paydowns. However, with prepayment speeds at historic lows, actual runoff has averaged just $18 billion per month, frustrating the Fed's balance sheet normalization efforts while simultaneously depriving the market of a crucial price-insensitive buyer. The Fed's MBS portfolio still stands at $2.38 trillion, down only $340 billion from its peak despite nearly two years of quantitative tightening.

Institutional investors are now reassessing their MBS allocations amid the deteriorating technical and fundamental backdrop. Insurance companies, which hold approximately $520 billion in MBS, face potential capital charges as risk-based capital requirements tighten and mark-to-market losses accumulate. Pension funds have begun rotating out of MBS into shorter-duration corporate bonds, seeking to reduce interest rate sensitivity. The massive repricing in MBS valuations has also triggered margin calls for leveraged investors, with several smaller hedge funds reportedly facing redemptions after suffering double-digit losses in their fixed-income portfolios this quarter.

Key developments

  • Bloomberg U.S. MBS Index declined 1.8% in worst single-day performance since July, with agency MBS spreads widening 12 basis points to Treasuries
  • Prepayment speeds on Fannie Mae 30-year 6% coupons plummeted to 4.2 CPR in February, causing effective duration of agency MBS market to expand to 6.2 years from 4.1 years six months ago
  • Non-agency RMBS credit spreads widened 28 basis points on prime jumbo deals as liquidity dried up, with ICE Non-Agency RMBS Index falling 2.4%
  • CMBS delinquency rates surged to 6.7% in February from 5.2% in January, with $12.8 billion in commercial loans transferred to special servicing
  • Federal Reserve's MBS portfolio runoff averaging just $18 billion monthly versus $35 billion cap due to historic low prepayment speeds

"The combination of widening spreads, extension risk, and deteriorating credit metrics creates a perfect storm for MBS investors. We're advising clients to significantly reduce agency MBS exposure and avoid non-agency paper entirely until we see stabilization in both the housing market and the Fed's policy trajectory. This sell-off has further to run."

Jennifer Waldron, Chief Fixed Income Strategist at Thornhill Capital Management
