Global Government Bonds Rally as Dovish Central Bank Signals and Strong Demand Push Yields Lower

Government bonds across major markets rallied sharply on Monday as dovish central bank commentary, cooling inflation signals and robust primary market demand drove a wave of buy-side appetite for duration. Benchmark yields fell to multi-week lows, ETF inflows and strong auction cover ratios reinforced the move, prompting strategists to upgrade duration positions and project further downside for long-end yields in the near term.

Charlotte Beaumont in London 01/12/2025 09:30:00

Global sovereign bonds advanced decisively in early trading as investors interpreted recent central bank commentary and softer inflation indicators as confirmation that peak policy rates may have passed. The US 10-year Treasury yield fell 17 basis points to 3.38%, the German 10-year Bund dropped 19 basis points to 1.72%, and the UK 10-year gilt declined 24 basis points to 3.86%. The move sent government bond prices higher across Europe, North America and Asia, with duration once again the dominant trade for fixed income desks.

Market participants pointed to a confluence of factors behind the rally. Minutes from recent policy meetings and public remarks from senior officials in the Fed and ECB were interpreted as incrementally dovish, while headline inflation prints for the month signalled a further easing in goods-driven price pressures. Primary market dynamics amplified the rally: the US Treasury's 10-year auction drew a bid-to-cover of 2.85, with a notable increase in indirect bidding, and sovereign ETF products saw approximately $5.1 billion of inflows on the day, according to exchange-traded fund flow trackers.

Analysts highlighted compression in term premium and tightening swap spreads as additional technical drivers. Harper & Cole Research estimated the US term premium fell roughly 10 basis points intraday, and investment-grade swap spreads narrowed by about 6 basis points as hedge costs declined. Corporate credit reacted in sympathy: global investment-grade spreads tightened roughly 8 basis points, reflecting a repricing of duration risk and improved liquidity. Portfolio managers at Eclipse Asset Management reported a surge in client demand for 7- and 10-year duration as a tactical hedge against slowing growth expectations.

Market structure developments reinforced the bullish picture. With central banks pausing their pace of rate hikes and signalling a more patient approach, long-dated sovereign paper outperformed the short end, leading to a mild steepening of the yield curve in several markets. Real yields compressed and inflation breakevens eased, suggesting the market is pricing a longer window of accommodative policy than previously anticipated. Strategists at Stonebridge Capital upgraded duration weights in multi-asset mandates and flagged 10-year Treasuries as the preferred defensive allocation in a late-cycle environment.

Looking ahead, the consensus among bullish bond strategists is that continued moderation in inflation and strong demand from both official and private investors will keep downward pressure on long-term yields. Stonebridge Capital now models aBASE case of US 10-year yields reaching the low 3.20% area by the end of the quarter, contingent on further benign inflation data and stable auction outcomes. Risks remain — notably persistent services inflation or a sudden shift in fiscal issuance — but for now the market signal is clear: duration is in favour.

Key developments

  • Benchmark yields fall sharply: US 10-year down 17 bps to 3.38%, German 10-year down 19 bps to 1.72%, UK 10-year down 24 bps to 3.86% — multi-week lows across core markets.
  • Strong primary demand: US 10-year auction records a 2.85 bid-to-cover with elevated indirect participation; sovereign ETFs see approximately $5.1 billion in inflows.
  • Technical compression: estimated 10 bp drop in term premium and tightening of investment-grade spreads by c.8 bps, supporting a bullish duration trade.

"We see this as an extended opportunity to add duration. The recent data and central bank rhetoric have materially lowered the probability of further policy tightening, and with term premia compressing there's room for long-end yields to move lower in the weeks ahead."

Amara Singh, Head of Rates Research, Stonebridge Capital
