MBS Market Holds Steady as Supply and Prepayment Signals Offset Soft Demand

Mortgage-backed securities traded in a narrow range Thursday as muted TBA volumes and mixed prepayment data left spreads and yields largely unchanged. Market participants signaled a neutral stance ahead of next week's economic calendar, with supply expectations and modest changes in prepayment speeds balancing technical demand from agencies and banks.

Evelyn Cortez in New York 01/12/2025 09:15:00

Trading in agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS) was characterized by limited directional conviction on Thursday, with the 30-year fixed-rate MBS coupon stack seeing mid-single digit basis point moves intraday. The benchmark 30-year term (Fannie Mae 3.5% current-coupon proxy) finished largely flat, yielding about 3.40% after a 1-3bp intra-day range. The TBA (to-be-announced) market recorded below-average volumes, and bid/offer spreads held near their multi-week averages, reflecting an absence of new macro catalysts.

Supply-side dynamics remain a central theme for portfolio managers. Issuance from the agency seller-set continued at a steady clip, with Ginnie Mae pools coming to market alongside regular Fannie and Freddie activity. Dealers noted institutional demand has been steady but unspectacular, with banks and balance-sheet investors buying selectively for carry rather than taking duration bets. The prospect of steady mortgage originations at current rate levels suggested manageable new collateral flow rather than any surge that would decisively widen spreads.

Prepayment behavior provided mixed signals: MBA-modelled prepay speeds (PSA) ticked up modestly to an estimated 130 PSA for the 30-year cohort, driven by a small uptick in refinance inquiries at the margin, while seasoned vintages showed slower-than-expected runout. Analysts emphasized that modest increases in prepayment speeds have been largely anticipated and remain within modelled ranges, limiting mark-to-market volatility for coupon stacks priced for neutral prepayment risk.

Positioning in the space reflected tactical rebalancing. Hedge funds and long-duration managers pared back exposure to long-coupon 30yr pools, citing diminished convexity benefits relative to spread risk, while mortgage-specific desks increased allocations to 15-year coupons and seasoned two-step pools for relative value. Spread metrics remained range-bound: agency OAS (option-adjusted spread) versus comparable-duration Treasuries was around 40-55bp across conventional coupons, a level that market participants described as 'workable' but not compelling for aggressive buying.

Looking ahead, participants flagged a quiet near-term macro calendar as a reason for the lack of conviction. Attention will shift to next week's housing starts, existing-home sales, and the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation indicators — all data that could nudge MBS yield curves and swap spreads. In the absence of a material macro surprise, traders expect the market to remain in a holding pattern, with technical flows and prepayment updates driving localized moves rather than broad directional trends.

Key developments

  • TBA volumes remained below the 30-day average, contributing to rangebound price action for agency MBS.
  • Modelled prepayment speeds edged to ~130 PSA for recent 30-year cohorts, a modest rise that market participants view as within expected tolerances.
  • Agency OAS versus Treasuries held in the 40-55bp range, keeping spreads neutral and limiting impetus for aggressive portfolio rotation.

"With issuance and prepayment trends moving in opposite directions, the market has settled into a neutral equilibrium—it's a technical story right now rather than a macro-driven repricing."

Daniel Mercer, Head of Mortgage Strategy, Halcyon Fixed Income Partners
