Mortgage-Backed Securities Rally as Fed Signals Dovish Pivot, Spreads Tighten to Three-Year Lows

Agency mortgage-backed securities posted their strongest weekly performance in eighteen months as Federal Reserve commentary sparked a powerful rally across fixed-income markets, with MBS spreads compressing to levels not seen since early 2021. Robust institutional demand and improved prepayment dynamics are driving renewed investor appetite for the sector, with analysts projecting continued outperformance through year-end as rate volatility subsides and housing market stability returns.

Katherine Morrison and David Chen in New York 15/11/2024 14:32:17

Mortgage-backed securities are experiencing a dramatic resurgence as shifting Federal Reserve policy expectations and stabilizing housing metrics fuel the strongest rally in the sector since spring 2023. The Bloomberg US MBS Index returned 2.8% over the past five trading sessions, outpacing both corporate bonds and Treasuries, while option-adjusted spreads on agency MBS tightened 12 basis points to just 118 basis points over comparable-duration government securities. Trading volumes surged to $287 billion for the week, the highest level since March, as asset managers and regional banks aggressively added exposure to the sector.

The rally has been particularly pronounced in current-coupon securities, with Fannie Mae 5.5% and 6.0% coupons climbing more than three points as convexity profiles improved dramatically following the Fed's dovish shift. Prepayment speeds have decelerated to their slowest pace in four years, with the Mortgage Bankers Association reporting the constant prepayment rate fell to 4.2% in October, down from 6.8% at the start of the year. This extension in duration has paradoxically made MBS more attractive to liability-driven investors seeking stable cash flows, reversing months of negative convexity concerns that had kept institutional buyers on the sidelines.

Major Wall Street dealers report that foreign central banks and sovereign wealth funds have returned as significant buyers after largely abandoning the sector during the 2022-2023 rate hiking cycle. Japanese institutional investors alone purchased an estimated $18 billion in agency MBS during October, the largest monthly total since 2019, attracted by yields exceeding 6% on a hedged basis. Domestic money managers have similarly increased allocations, with mutual fund holdings of MBS rising by $34 billion over the past six weeks according to Federal Reserve flow of funds data, the sharpest increase since the pandemic-era buying surge.

Housing market fundamentals are providing additional tailwinds for MBS performance. The S&P CoreLogic Case-Shiller National Home Price Index showed prices rising 4.2% year-over-year in September, while inventory levels have stabilized and mortgage delinquency rates remain near historic lows at just 3.1%. Credit performance across both agency and non-agency MBS pools continues to exceed underwriting expectations, with loss severities on defaulted loans running 40% below historical averages due to substantial home equity cushions. These positive credit trends have compressed risk premiums and attracted crossover buyers from the corporate credit space seeking superior risk-adjusted returns.

The technical backdrop for MBS has also improved markedly. The Federal Reserve's quantitative tightening program has slowed its pace of MBS runoff, with monthly reductions now capped at $35 billion versus the $60 billion ceiling that prevailed through mid-2024. This reduced supply pressure, combined with seasonal patterns that typically favor MBS in the fourth quarter due to slower mortgage origination volumes, has created a favorable supply-demand dynamic. Dealer inventories have normalized to $1.8 billion, down from the elevated $4.2 billion levels seen during the spring volatility, indicating healthy market clearing and reduced financing costs for market makers.

Looking forward, strategists across the Street have turned increasingly constructive on MBS relative value. Goldman Sachs upgraded agency MBS to overweight last week, projecting another 8-12 basis points of spread tightening over the next quarter as volatility continues to decline and Fed rate cuts progress. Morgan Stanley's securitized products team raised their year-end price targets for production coupons, citing improved carry profiles and positive convexity positioning. With real money accounts still underweight MBS relative to historical benchmarks and technical factors remaining supportive, the sector appears well-positioned for continued outperformance even as broader fixed-income markets face uncertainty around fiscal policy and inflation trajectories.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads tightened 12 basis points to three-year lows of 118 basis points over Treasuries, with the Bloomberg US MBS Index returning 2.8% in five trading sessions
  • Foreign institutional demand surged with Japanese investors purchasing $18 billion in October alone, while domestic mutual fund holdings increased by $34 billion over six weeks
  • Prepayment speeds decelerated to 4.2% CPR in October, the slowest pace in four years, dramatically improving convexity profiles and extending duration characteristics
  • Major investment banks including Goldman Sachs upgraded MBS to overweight, projecting an additional 8-12 basis points of spread compression through year-end

"The MBS sector is experiencing a perfect storm of positive catalysts that we haven't seen aligned in years. Between Fed policy normalization, stable housing fundamentals, and dramatically improved prepayment dynamics, we're looking at one of the most compelling risk-adjusted opportunities in fixed income today. Institutional accounts that remained underweight are now scrambling to rebuild positions."

Thomas Reinhardt, Head of Securitized Products Strategy at Barrington Capital Markets
