Mortgage-Backed Securities Face Sharp Selloff as Fed Signals Extended Higher Rates, Prepayment Risks Mount

Mortgage-backed securities markets tumbled sharply in Wednesday trading as Federal Reserve officials signaled rates will remain elevated longer than previously anticipated, triggering widespread selloffs across agency and non-agency MBS sectors. Spreads widened to their highest levels in fourteen months while prepayment concerns and deteriorating housing affordability metrics compound pressure on the $12 trillion market, prompting institutional investors to reduce exposure amid growing concerns over credit quality and duration risk.

Sarah Mitchell and Robert Chen in New York 15/11/2024 16:42:33

Mortgage-backed securities suffered their worst single-day decline in over a year on Wednesday, with the Bloomberg U.S. MBS Index falling 1.8% as investor sentiment soured following hawkish commentary from Federal Reserve Governor Michelle Bowman. Agency MBS spreads widened 12 basis points relative to Treasury benchmarks, reaching levels not seen since September 2023, while trading volumes surged to $285 billion—nearly double the daily average—as institutional investors rushed to reduce portfolio exposure.

The selloff intensified concerns about the structural challenges facing the MBS market as mortgage rates climbed back above 7.5% for 30-year fixed loans, effectively freezing refinancing activity and extending the duration profile of existing securities. Analysts at Morgan Stanley warned that the combination of elevated rates, slowing home price appreciation, and deteriorating borrower credit metrics could trigger a repricing across the entire mortgage credit spectrum, particularly impacting lower-rated tranches of non-agency residential mortgage-backed securities.

Prepayment speeds have collapsed to their slowest pace since 2000, with the Mortgage Bankers Association reporting the constant prepayment rate fell to just 4.2% in October, down from a peak of 47% during the 2020-2021 refinancing boom. This dramatic slowdown has left MBS investors locked into lower-yielding securities while simultaneously exposing them to heightened extension risk as the effective duration of their holdings expands well beyond initial modeling assumptions. Portfolio managers at Wellington Asset Management estimate that average durations on current-coupon agency MBS have extended to 6.8 years, up from 4.2 years just eighteen months ago.

The deterioration has been particularly acute in the non-agency sector, where credit spreads on BBB-rated RMBS tranches have blown out by 145 basis points over the past six weeks. Fitch Ratings placed 47 non-agency MBS tranches on negative watch this week, citing rising delinquency rates among borrowers with debt-to-income ratios above 45% and increased loss severity assumptions on properties in overheated markets. The rating agency projects cumulative losses on 2022-2023 vintage non-QM loans could reach 3.8%, substantially higher than initial expectations of 2.1%.

Foreign demand for agency MBS has evaporated as international investors retreated from dollar-denominated assets amid currency hedging costs that have rendered yields uncompetitive. Data from the Federal Reserve's custody holdings show foreign central banks reduced their agency MBS positions by $42 billion in the third quarter, marking the largest quarterly decline since 2013. Japanese institutional investors, traditionally among the largest offshore buyers, have shifted allocations toward Japanese government bonds following the Bank of Japan's policy normalization, removing a critical source of MBS demand.

Market participants are now bracing for potential forced selling as leverage-dependent hedge fund strategies face mounting margin calls. Several quantitative funds employing MBS basis trades have begun unwinding positions after losses exceeded risk parameters, according to prime brokerage sources. The deleveraging could accelerate if spreads continue widening, potentially triggering a feedback loop that further depresses valuations. Treasury Department officials have reportedly begun monitoring the situation for systemic risk implications, though no formal interventions have been announced.

The outlook for the sector remains clouded by uncertainty over the Federal Reserve's balance sheet normalization program, with the central bank still allowing up to $35 billion in agency MBS to roll off monthly. Some strategists argue the Fed should slow or halt MBS runoff to prevent disorderly market conditions, though policymakers have shown little appetite for adjusting the program. With the Fed holding approximately $2.3 trillion in agency MBS—down from a peak of $2.7 trillion—the continued quantitative tightening represents a structural headwind that could pressure valuations for quarters to come.

Key developments

  • Bloomberg U.S. MBS Index declined 1.8% in single session, worst performance since October 2023, as agency MBS spreads widened 12 basis points to 14-month highs
  • Prepayment speeds collapsed to 4.2% constant prepayment rate, slowest since 2000, extending average MBS durations to 6.8 years and dramatically increasing extension risk exposure
  • Fitch Ratings placed 47 non-agency RMBS tranches on negative watch with BBB-rated credit spreads widening 145 basis points on deteriorating borrower credit quality
  • Foreign investors withdrew $42 billion from agency MBS holdings in Q3, largest quarterly decline in decade, eliminating critical source of market demand
  • Hedge fund deleveraging accelerates as MBS basis trades hit risk limits, raising concerns about potential forced selling and systemic contagion

"The MBS market is facing a perfect storm of negative catalysts that we haven't seen converge simultaneously in over a decade. With the Fed maintaining restrictive policy, prepayment speeds at generational lows, and credit quality deteriorating across non-agency sectors, we're advising clients to materially reduce duration and credit exposure until we see clear signs of stabilization."

David Patterson, Head of Fixed Income Strategy at Wellington Asset Management
