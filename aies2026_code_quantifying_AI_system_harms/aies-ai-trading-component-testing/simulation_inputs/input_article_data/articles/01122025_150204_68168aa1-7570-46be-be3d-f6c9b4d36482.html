Sharp Sell-Off in MBS as Rates Spike, TBA Market Sees Deep Losses and Spreads Widen

Mortgage-backed securities (MBS) endured a pronounced downturn on Monday as a renewed repricing of interest-rate expectations pushed yields higher, TBAs weaker and agency spreads significantly wider. Traders point to sticky inflation prints, a more hawkish Federal Reserve outlook and a surge in agency issuance — alongside proposed guarantee-fee changes — as drivers that have turned investor positioning sharply bearish across the sector.

Eleanor Park in New York 01/12/2025 14:22:00

A broad-based liquidation in the MBS complex accelerated on Monday, with To-Be-Announced (TBA) prices tumbling and benchmark current-coupon yields climbing sharply. The 30-year current-coupon passed-through yield jumped roughly 65 basis points on the week to near 5.30%, while 30-year conforming TBAs lost about 1.75 points in early trading, reflecting one of the heaviest one-week moves this quarter.

Option-adjusted spreads (OAS) on agency passthroughs widened materially — moving from roughly 80 basis points over Treasuries last week to about 140 basis points mid-week — as dealers re-priced convexity risk and longer-duration hedges. The 10-year Treasury spiked to approximately 4.20%, amplifying duration extension across longer-coupon stacks and prompting a re-evaluation of carry-versus-risk profiles in vanilla agency paper.

Market participants cited a mix of macro and micro drivers. Sticky core inflation prints and accompanying commentary from Fed officials reinforced a higher-for-longer rate narrative, while secondary drivers included an unexpected pickup in agency issuance — estimated at $45 billion last week by desk tallies — and a newly disclosed proposal from a government-run guarantor to raise guarantee fees by 10 basis points, which dealers said exacerbated selling pressure on Ginnie and other agency strips.

Flow dynamics intensified the move. Mutual and ETF funds registered net outflows of approximately $4.2 billion from MBS-linked products over the past five trading days, according to market flow data compiled by Riverton Analytics. Hedge funds and select prop desks increased TBA short exposure and purchased duration-buying protection via longer-dated swaps, a trade that further steepened hedge ratios and pressured outright prices in the most liquid coupons.

The real-economy implications of the sell-off are already visible in the mortgage market. The average quoted 30-year conforming mortgage rate moved up to around 7.05%, shrinking refinance demand and further depressing prepayment speeds (PSA) — industry trackers show CPRs declining to near 60 PSA on seasoned pools. For originators, the combination of higher funding costs and slower pipelines has revived margin compression conversations and halted a nascent uptick in loan volume.

Credit-sensitive corners of the securitized housing market are under particular strain. Non-agency RMBS dealers reported widening credit spreads and re-pricing of mezzanine tranches as investors flee duration and opt for liquidity. 'This is a classic case of a convexity shock meeting an issuance wave,' said a senior strategist at Arcadia Securities, adding that positioning remains deeply stretched on the short side and that a further leg up in rates could provoke additional forced selling from ill-equipped long holders.

Key developments

  • TBAs plunged roughly 1.75 points week-over-week; 30-year current-coupon yield up ~65 bps to near 5.30%.
  • Agency OAS widened from ~80 bps to ~140 bps as dealers re-priced convexity and duration risk.
  • Ginnie Mae proposal to raise guarantee fees by 10 bps announced; mutual fund MBS outflows ~ $4.2bn last five trading days.

"We're seeing a convergence of adverse forces — higher real rates, heavier-than-expected issuance and policy changes — that leave agency MBS vulnerable to an extended drawdown. Inventory is plentiful and natural buyers are scarce; the technicals are decisively bearish."

Marcus Hale, Head of Fixed Income Strategy, Hayward Capital
