Mortgage-Backed Securities Market Holds Steady as Fed Policy Uncertainty Balances Housing Data

The MBS market traded within a narrow range this week as investors weighed mixed housing sector indicators against ongoing uncertainty over Federal Reserve monetary policy direction. Agency MBS spreads remained relatively unchanged while trading volumes tracked near seasonal averages, reflecting a wait-and-see approach among institutional buyers as the market digests economic data ahead of upcoming policy meetings.

Sarah Mitchell and Daniel Park in New York 15/03/2024 14:32:17

The mortgage-backed securities market maintained equilibrium through mid-week trading sessions as market participants continued to assess the interplay between stabilizing housing market fundamentals and the Federal Reserve's monetary policy stance. The Bloomberg Barclays MBS Index registered a modest 0.12% gain for the week, while current coupon Ginnie Mae 30-year securities traded at 101-08, virtually unchanged from the previous week's close. Trading desk activity reflected measured positioning rather than directional conviction.

Agency MBS spreads to Treasury securities held within a tight 5-basis-point band throughout the period, with the current coupon spread settling at 162 basis points over the 10-year Treasury note. Prepayment speeds remained consistent with seasonal expectations, coming in at 9.2 CPR for 30-year conventional pools, representing neither acceleration nor deceleration from recent trends. The stability in prepayment metrics has allowed portfolio managers to maintain duration targets without significant rebalancing requirements.

Housing market data released this week presented a mixed picture that neither significantly boosted nor pressured MBS valuations. Existing home sales edged up 1.1% month-over-month to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 4.15 million units, while mortgage application volumes for purchase activity remained flat week-over-week. The average 30-year conforming mortgage rate held at 6.87%, according to Fredman Home Loan Mortgage Corporation data, contributing to the steady state in refinancing activity which represents approximately 28% of total origination volume.

Institutional demand for agency MBS remained consistent with recent patterns, as asset managers continued their methodical accumulation strategies. Insurance companies and pension funds maintained their typical buying programs, with reported purchases concentrated in seasoned 5.5% and 6.0% coupon pools that offer favorable convexity profiles. Bank portfolio buying was described as steady but not aggressive, with regional institutions focusing on shorter-duration specified pools to manage interest rate risk exposure. Foreign central bank participation through the Federal Reserve's custody holdings showed net additions of $2.3 billion for the week, in line with the three-month average.

The issuance pipeline for new mortgage originations continues to support market balance, with primary-secondary spread relationships remaining within historical norms. Production economics for lenders have stabilized at levels that neither incentivize aggressive origination pushes nor create capacity constraints. Warehouse lines at major correspondent lenders reported utilization rates between 65-70%, indicating normal flow business without inventory buildups or shortages. Servicing values for newly originated loans have held relatively constant, with base servicing strips for conventional 30-year mortgages trading at 88-92 basis points depending on credit quality and geographic concentration.

Looking ahead, market participants expect the current equilibrium to persist absent significant shifts in either Fed policy communications or housing sector fundamentals. Upcoming economic releases including the employment report and inflation data will be closely monitored for implications on the central bank's policy trajectory. Technical factors including settlement patterns and month-end rebalancing flows are expected to have minimal impact given the current depth of market liquidity. Volatility measures for MBS options remain subdued, with implied volatility for at-the-money swaptions trading near the lower end of the 12-month range, suggesting limited expectations for dramatic price movements in the near term.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads held steady at 162 basis points over 10-year Treasuries, trading within a 5-basis-point range for the week
  • Prepayment speeds registered at 9.2 CPR for 30-year conventional pools, consistent with seasonal expectations and recent trends
  • Institutional buying remained balanced with insurance companies and pension funds maintaining regular purchase programs focused on 5.5-6.0% coupon securities
  • The 30-year conforming mortgage rate held at 6.87%, supporting stable origination volumes and refinancing activity at 28% of total production

"The MBS market is in a holding pattern as investors await clearer signals on both Fed policy direction and housing market trajectory. We're seeing consistent two-way flow and adequate liquidity, but conviction levels remain muted. Portfolio managers are comfortable at current spread levels but aren't aggressively adding duration exposure given the uncertainty around the next phase of monetary policy."

Robert Chen, Head of Mortgage Strategy at Bridgewater Capital Advisors
