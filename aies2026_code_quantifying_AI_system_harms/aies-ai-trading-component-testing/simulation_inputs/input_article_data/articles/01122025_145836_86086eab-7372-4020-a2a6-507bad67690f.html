Agency MBS Rally Intensifies as Technicals and Lower Supply Fuel Bull Run

A broad-based rally in agency mortgage-backed securities accelerated this week as falling Treasury yields, reduced mortgage origination supply and renewed institutional demand tightened spreads and lifted prices across the curve. Market participants say technical dynamics and stabilizing prepayment expectations underpin a bullish outlook for MBS into the new quarter.

Rachel M. Turner, Senior Markets Correspondent in New York 01/12/2025 14:30:00

Agency mortgage-backed securities surged in secondary trading Monday, extending a three-week advance that has become one of the more pronounced rallies in the fixed-income complex this year. Traders pointed to a sharp drop in 10-year Treasury yields — which fell to 3.45% from 3.70% at the start of the week — and a steady stream of demand from domestic banks, life insurers and offshore accounts as the primary engines of the move. Fannie Mae 30-year 2.5% coupons led the charge, gaining roughly 28 ticks as bid-side interest pushed prices to multimonth highs.

Market structure factors helped amplify the rally. Mortgage origination volumes have moderated, with industry estimates showing a roughly 22% year-over-year decline in new production, reducing fresh pass-through supply. At the same time, a softer-than-expected inflation print and dovish forward guidance from the central bank lowered investors' terminal-rate expectations, supporting duration assets and narrowing MBS/Treasury spreads. The option-adjusted spread (OAS) on benchmark agency 30-year pools tightened by about 12 basis points to near 34 bps, according to exchange data.

Flow data underlines the breadth of demand. MBS-focused ETFs and mutual funds recorded net inflows of approximately $2.3 billion over the past five trading days, while weekly primary and secondary trading volumes rose an estimated 18% week-on-week. Wall-street dealers reported stronger participation from community and regional banks looking to lock in yield with agency coupons, and from pension funds rotating into MBS for higher running income and stable cash flow characteristics.

Technical metrics have begun to look more favorable to MBS holders: prepayment speeds (PSA) have cooled to roughly 80 PSA from above-100 PSA levels earlier in the year, reducing refinancing-related convexity shock. That moderation — combined with a shrinking new-issue calendar — is helping to compress the term premium on longer-dated passthroughs. Effective duration on a benchmark 30-year coupon pool is now estimated near 3.1 years, giving portfolio managers a clearer risk-reward trade-off as they extend duration in search of carry.

Analysts have responded to the move by revising positioning recommendations. Whitestone Asset Management raised its tactical stance to overweight agency 15- and 30-year coupons, citing 'a favorable confluence of supply dislocation and persistent investor demand.' Meanwhile, Harcourt Capital's senior MBS strategist noted that current valuations are attractive relative to historical norms, particularly on the 5- to 7-year window where extension risk remains contained. Several shops also highlighted growing appetite for structured MBS tranches (IO/PO and short-coupon CMOs) as investors seek incremental pickup while managing prepayment exposure.

The bullish MBS backdrop has tangible implications for mortgage markets and broader credit. Lower MBS yields are contributing to a modest decline in retail mortgage rates, with the secondary-market component of the 30-year fixed rate moving down roughly 10–15 basis points over the past week. Market participants caution that a sudden shift in inflation surprises or a liquidity event could reintroduce volatility, but for now the technical squeeze and steady demand argue for continued momentum in agency MBS through the upcoming quarter.

Key developments

  • Benchmark 10-year Treasury yield dropped to 3.45%, prompting MBS prices to rally and OAS to tighten ~12 bps to near 34 bps on 30-year agency coupons.
  • Net inflows into MBS-focused ETFs and funds totaled an estimated $2.3 billion over the past five trading days while secondary trading volumes rose ~18% week-on-week.
  • Mortgage origination supply has eased (approx. 22% YoY decline), reducing new-issue pressure and supporting higher prices and compressed spreads across agency passthroughs.

""The technical backdrop is exceptionally constructive right now — constrained supply, softer prepayment speeds and robust bid-side demand are creating a clear bull market in agency MBS. Tactical overweights in 15- and 30-year coupons make sense as carry improves and spread compression continues,""

Eleanor Briggs, Senior MBS Strategist, Harcourt Capital
