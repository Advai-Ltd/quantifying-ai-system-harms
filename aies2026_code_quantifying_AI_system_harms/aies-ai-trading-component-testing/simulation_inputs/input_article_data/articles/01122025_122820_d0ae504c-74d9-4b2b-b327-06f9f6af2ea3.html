Equities Rally Extends to Record Highs as Tech, Industrials and Financials Power Broad-Based Upswing

Global equity markets pushed higher, with major benchmarks closing at fresh records after a wave of upbeat earnings, dovish central bank signals and renewed investor demand for cyclicals and growth. Strong breadth, sustained ETF inflows and rising buyback activity underpin a bullish case for equities even as valuations tick higher.

Harper Lin, Senior Markets Correspondent in New York 01/12/2025 09:15:00

U.S. equities extended a month-long advance on Monday, with the S&P 500 rising 1.6% to finish at 5,010, the Nasdaq 100 climbing 2.4% to 17,950 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average up 1.2% to 39,850 — all closing at fresh intraday and record levels. Trading breadth was strong: advancing issues outnumbered decliners by better than 4-to-1 on the NYSE, while the number of new 52-week highs exceeded 1,100, signaling broad participation beyond headline tech names.

Sector rotation accompanied the rally. Semiconductors and software led gains — the SOX-equivalent semiconductor index jumped 3.8% — while industrials and financials climbed 2.7% and 2.1% respectively as investors re-priced cyclical recovery prospects. Defensive sectors lagged; utilities fell 0.4% as risk-on flows favored growth and cyclicals. IMPLIED volatility collapsed, with the VIX sliding 14% to 11.8, reflecting reduced option-costs for market exposure.

Market drivers were clear: an above-consensus earnings season, softening core inflation prints in recent weeks and a more dovish tilt in central bank communications. Notable corporate beats included AetherCloud, whose fiscal Q4 revenue beat by 12% and whose cloud bookings growth accelerated to 42% year-over-year; Riverton Steel warned last quarter but reported better-than-expected margin recovery on stronger downstream demand; and Atlas Bank cited improving net interest margins and upgraded its capital return plan. Institutional flows reinforced the move — equity ETFs recorded an estimated $18.2 billion net inflow across global funds last week — while corporate buyback announcements accelerated, totaling roughly $92 billion in commitments for the quarter.

Analysts responded by lifting forward estimates and price targets. At Meridian Capital Markets, analysts raised S&P 500 12-month EPS projections by 2.4% following the latest results and contemporaneous macro data, nudging the 12-month forward P/E to 18.4 from 17.9 a month earlier. Technical indicators corroborated fundamentals: the market’s advance-decline line remained firmly positive and short interest across large-cap growth names declined, indicating unwinding bearish positioning. Strategists highlight improving revenue momentum, margin expansion in select sectors and accelerating buybacks as supportive structurally bullish signals.

Despite the constructive backdrop, strategists caution on near-term risk. Tail risks include an unexpected inflation rebound, geopolitical flare-ups that could disrupt supply chains, or a sharper-than-expected rotation that leaves stretched growth names exposed. For now, the recommended positioning is overweight quality cyclicals, selective growth with durable cashflow conversion and a defensive overlay via low-cost options or diversified bond ladders to manage potential volatility spikes.

Key developments

  • Major benchmarks closed at record highs: S&P 500 +1.6% to 5,010; Nasdaq 100 +2.4% to 17,950; Dow +1.2% to 39,850 — breadth led by semiconductors and industrials.
  • Net inflows into equity ETFs totaled an estimated $18.2 billion last week while corporate buyback announcements surged to roughly $92 billion for the quarter, providing demand support for equities.
  • Earnings season surprises: AetherCloud beat revenue and bookings estimates and raised guidance; several banks reported expanding net interest margins, prompting analysts to upgrade earnings forecasts and price targets.

""The combination of accelerating earnings revisions, persistent buyback activity and continued capital reallocations into cyclicals suggests this is more than a short-term melt-up — it's a sustained regime where risk assets can outperform for the next several quarters, provided inflation remains subdued,""

Maya Rodriguez, Head of U.S. Equity Strategy, Meridian Capital Markets
