MBS Spreads Hit 18-Month High on Persistent Rate Fears and Liquidity Crunch

Mortgages Backed Securities (MBS) valuations are under severe pressure as spreads over comparable Treasuries have widened dramatically, signaling deep investor concern over duration risk and housing market stability. The current volatility, exacerbated by diminishing liquidity and sustained quantitative tightening (QT), has created a challenging environment for fixed income portfolios reliant on these instruments.

Eleanor Vance, CFA in New York 15/05/2024 09:45:00

Agency MBS spreads, particularly for the widely traded Ginnie Mae and Fannie Mae 30-year current coupons, have blown out by nearly 18 basis points (bps) over the past week, pushing them to levels not consistently seen since late 2022. This rapid decompression is fundamentally driven by the market's acceptance of a 'higher-for-longer' interest rate outlook, which increases the extension risk inherent in long-duration mortgage paper.

The core issue facing MBS holders is the pronounced slowdown in prepayment speeds (known as Conditional Prepayment Rate or CPR). With average mortgage rates hovering well above 6.5%, fewer homeowners are refinancing, meaning the expected duration of MBS bonds is lengthening significantly. This extension makes existing bond prices acutely sensitive to rising yields, driving investors to sell positions to manage portfolio duration targets, amplifying the downward price movement.

Liquidity concerns are compounding the issue. Dealer balance sheets are showing signs of stress as they are forced to hold higher inventories amid sluggish demand from traditional buyers, including mREITs and pension funds. Analysts at Keystone Analytics report that bid/ask spreads are noticeably wider, indicating price discovery issues and difficulty executing large block trades without significant slippage.

Furthermore, while Agency MBS carry an implicit government guarantee against principal loss, the broader market sentiment is being weighed down by creeping credit risks in the non-agency sector. A mild increase in delinquencies, particularly in newly originated non-Qualified Mortgage (Non-QM) pools, has raised systemic caution regarding housing market stability, creating an overarching bearish shadow over the entire securitized credit landscape.

The Federal Reserve's ongoing balance sheet runoff (QT) remains a structural headwind. As the Fed allows its portfolio of Treasury and MBS holdings to mature without reinvesting, a massive and historically reliable buyer is absent from the market, forcing the private sector to absorb supply at increasingly discounted prices. This lack of a structural floor buyer ensures continued price volatility.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads widened by approximately 18 bps over the past week, signaling severe duration concerns.
  • Prepayment speeds (CPR) have slowed significantly, exacerbating extension risk across long-dated MBS pools.
  • Dealer inventories have reportedly surged, leading to reduced liquidity and widening bid/ask spreads in the secondary market.

"We are facing a perfect storm of duration extension risk and structural liquidity removal. The market is struggling to properly price the impact of slow prepayments combined with persistent QT pressure. Until interest rate volatility subsides, we anticipate further price decay in conventional MBS paper."

Marcus Holt, Head of Fixed Income Strategy at Apex Capital Management
