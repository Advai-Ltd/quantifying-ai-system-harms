Agency MBS Spreads Tighten Sharply as Institutional Demand Overwhelms Supply

Strong institutional inflows and resilient housing fundamentals are driving a bullish resurgence in the Agency MBS market, with spreads tightening significantly against benchmark Treasuries, signaling optimism about credit quality and duration stability despite recent rate volatility. The sector is now exhibiting relative value attractiveness not seen since early 2022.

Julian Vance, CFA in New York 16/07/2024 10:35:00

The Agency Mortgages Backed Securities (MBS) market delivered its strongest performance quarter-to-date relative to comparable duration Treasuries, propelled by heavy buying in the To Be Announced (TBA) market. Current coupon 30-year Fannie Mae paper saw spreads tighten by nearly 15 basis points (bps) in the first two weeks of July, reflecting a robust appetite from money managers and official sector accounts.

This tightening is primarily attributed to a confluence of favorable technical factors. Pension funds, insurance carriers, and other liability-driven investors are aggressively seeking high-quality, government-guaranteed paper to meet duration matching requirements. The stable, albeit high, level of underlying mortgage rates has effectively capped refinancing activity, keeping Prepayment Speed Assumptions (PSA) well below 100, thereby minimizing extension risk—a critical factor attracting long-term capital.

Analysts at Zenith Securities note that foreign official accounts have also significantly increased their allocation to US agency debt, viewing MBS as a compelling relative-value play compared to low-yielding European sovereign bonds. This global hunt for yield, combined with the implicit government guarantee backing Agency MBS, has provided a powerful bid floor, absorbing new issuance efficiently.

Furthermore, underlying credit quality remains exceptionally robust. Data released by the GSEs (Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac) last week showed that serious delinquency rates are holding near historic lows. This strong performance of the collateral pool reduces tail risk perception among investors and reinforces the high credit quality of the underlying assets, further supporting the bullish narrative.

The market consensus suggests that while overall interest rate volatility persists, the technical strength within the MBS sector should allow spreads to continue tightening. Many desks are now targeting the current coupon spread to compress further, possibly reaching the 45 bps level, which would represent a significant rally from the 70 bps range observed earlier this year.

Should the Federal Reserve maintain its current neutral stance and long-end Treasury yields stabilize further, the risk-adjusted return profile of Agency MBS is projected to materially outperform corporate and high-grade indices through the remainder of the third quarter.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads tighten 15 bps month-to-date, marking the strongest period of relative performance this year.
  • Prepayment speeds remain low (PSA < 100), ensuring stable duration profiles critical for institutional asset matching.
  • Increased allocation from foreign central banks and domestic pension funds creates powerful technical tailwinds, signaling persistent high demand.

"The structural dynamics of low prepay speeds coupled with stabilizing Treasury yields provide an extremely attractive entry point for MBS. We see spreads hitting levels we haven't witnessed since 2021, driven purely by technical strength and predictable cash flows that asset managers desperately need."

Eleanor Hayes, Head of Fixed Income Strategy, Olympus Capital Management
