Bullish Run in Agency MBS Accelerates as Spreads Tighten and Flows Surge

Agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS) rallied this week as investor demand outpaced supply, driven by sustained Fed reinvestment guidance, softer-than-expected inflation data and renewed real-money interest. The move tightened spreads across the coupon stack, lifted TBA prices and prompted above-average inflows into MBS ETFs and mutual funds, reinforcing a constructive outlook for the asset class.

Samuel V. Hart in New York 01/12/2025 14:15:30

Agency MBS posted strong gains across the curve on Monday and extended momentum through the week as yields fell and spread measures contracted sharply. The FNMA 30-year 2.5% coupon tightened roughly 20 basis points week-over-week versus the 10-year Treasury, leaving the MBS-Treasury spread down to about 60 basis points from near 85 bps at the start of November. TBA (To-Be-Announced) prices firmed, with the most traded 30-year coupons up 60–90 ticks on a broad rally that market participants described as ``risk-on for duration.''

Market technicians and portfolio managers pointed to a trifecta of supportive drivers: the Federal Reserve's reiterated guidance to reinvest principal paydowns from its agency MBS book, softer CPI prints that tempered near-term rate volatility, and a pickup in real-money demand from insurance and pension funds seeking long-duration spread. Primary dealer data showed $5.4bn in net new institutional buying of agency 30-year coupons in the latest session, while TBA roll specialness tightened, indicating stronger cash demand relative to supply.

Flows into securitized-product vehicles have been robust. MBS-focused ETFs recorded net inflows of approximately $3.2bn in the past five trading days, according to market estimates compiled by Centurion Analytics, while several mutual funds and bank trading desks increased allocation to 15- and 30-year agency pools. Hedge funds, meanwhile, added leverage selectively on cheap-to-deliver rails and coupon stack arbitrage opportunities. Larkspur Capital reported that agency 2.0–3.5% coupons saw the most aggressive coverage as investors chased incremental spread compression with manageable convexity exposure.

Technicals remain favorable for long-duration MBS despite a modest pick-up in prepayment expectations. The market-implied 30-year single-family CPR (Conditional Prepayment Rate) edged up from 6.2% to 7.4% month-over-month, but analysts noted that the move has been priced-in and is unlikely to derail the rally given the depth of demand and the Fed's reinvestment policy. Supply-side constraints are also supportive: origination volumes have been stable but not excessive, and agency issuance calendars signal limited incremental borrower-driven supply through year-end.

Analysts say the risk-reward profile for agency MBS has improved relative to other fixed-income sectors. Shorter-duration agencies have underperformed somewhat, but the 30-year stack's yield pick-up versus comparable-duration Treasuries remains attractive to long-term investors. ``We view current valuations as a favorable entry point for long-duration, high-quality agency MBS — the structural support from reinvestments and institutional demand suggests further spread compression is likely,'' said Evelyn Martin, head of securitized products at Larkspur Capital. Strategists continue to watch macro prints and prepayment data closely, but the prevailing consensus in the market is cautiously bullish heading into the final month of the quarter.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS-Treasury spreads tightened to ~60 bps for 30-year coupons from ~85 bps one month ago, reflecting strong investor demand.
  • Estimated net inflows into MBS-focused ETFs and mutual funds reached ~$3.2bn over the past five trading days, boosting secondary market liquidity and TBA specialness.
  • Federal Reserve reaffirmed reinvestment of principal payments into agency MBS, underpinning durable technical support for the sector.

""Valuations have moved to a point where long-duration agency MBS offer a compelling combination of spread carry and convexity protection — with Fed reinvestment as a backstop, we see room for further spread tightening into year-end,""

Evelyn Martin, Head of Securitized Products, Larkspur Capital
