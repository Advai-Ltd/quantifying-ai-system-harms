Treasury Bonds Rally as Fed Signals Peak in Rate Cycle, Yields Drop to Nine-Month Lows

Government bonds are experiencing their strongest rally in over a year as investors pile into Treasuries following dovish signals from the Federal Reserve and growing expectations of rate cuts in 2024. The 10-year Treasury yield has dropped 45 basis points over the past month to 3.82%, while demand at recent auctions has surged to multi-year highs, reflecting a fundamental shift in fixed-income market sentiment and renewed appetite for sovereign debt.

Catherine Morrison and David Chen in New York/Washington 15/11/2024 14:32:17

Government bonds are enjoying a powerful resurgence as fixed-income investors return to sovereign debt in force, driven by mounting evidence that the Federal Reserve's aggressive tightening campaign has reached its conclusion. The benchmark 10-year Treasury yield has plummeted from 4.27% to 3.82% over the past four weeks, marking the most significant rally since November 2023, while longer-dated 30-year bonds have seen yields compress by 52 basis points to 4.15%. The sharp decline in yields, which move inversely to bond prices, has generated substantial capital gains for investors who maintained positions through the volatility of the past two years.

Demand dynamics have shifted dramatically across the Treasury market, with recent auction results demonstrating exceptional investor appetite. The Treasury Department's $24 billion 30-year bond auction last week attracted a bid-to-cover ratio of 2.67, the highest since February 2021, while indirect bidders—a proxy for foreign central bank demand—took down 72% of the offering. Primary dealers were left holding just 11.2% of the issuance, indicating robust participation from real money accounts. The strong auction performance comes as institutional investors, pension funds, and insurance companies aggressively reposition portfolios toward duration in anticipation of a more accommodative monetary policy environment.

Market participants are increasingly confident that the Federal Reserve has concluded its rate hiking cycle, with futures markets now pricing in a 78% probability of at least three quarter-point rate cuts by the end of 2024. Recent economic data showing moderating inflation pressures—core PCE inflation declined to 2.7% year-over-year in October—combined with softening labor market conditions have reinforced expectations for policy easing. Fed Chair Jerome Powell's recent comments emphasizing the central bank's dual mandate and acknowledging progress on inflation have been interpreted as laying the groundwork for a pivot toward rate reductions, a scenario highly supportive for government bond valuations.

International demand for U.S. Treasuries has rebounded sharply after a period of relative weakness, with foreign holdings increasing by $89 billion in September according to the latest Treasury International Capital data. Japanese investors have been particularly active buyers, with commercial banks and life insurance companies attracted by attractive hedged yields exceeding 5% when currency costs are factored in. Chinese holdings stabilized at $778 billion after several months of net selling, while new buyers from the Middle East and emerging markets have entered the Treasury market seeking safe-haven assets amid geopolitical uncertainties and attractive real yields following the inflation normalization.

Technical factors are also supporting the government bond rally, with short covering by hedge funds and systematic trend-following strategies adding momentum to price gains. Commodity Trading Advisors (CTAs) have flipped from net short to net long positions in Treasury futures over the past three weeks, with positioning data from the CFTC showing leveraged funds now hold approximately $42 billion in net long positions across the curve. This represents a dramatic reversal from the heavily short positioning that prevailed through much of 2023 and early 2024, when consensus views favored higher-for-longer interest rates.

Credit strategists are advising clients to extend duration and lock in elevated yields before the anticipated rate cutting cycle drives yields substantially lower. Goldman Sachs Asset Management recently upgraded its outlook on Treasuries to overweight, projecting the 10-year yield could test 3.25% by mid-2025 if the Fed delivers the rate cuts currently priced into markets. Morgan Stanley's fixed-income team has recommended a barbell strategy combining 2-year and 30-year positions to capitalize on curve steepening dynamics, while JPMorgan's rates desk suggests investors can achieve attractive total returns of 8-10% on long-duration Treasuries over the next twelve months if the base case scenario of Fed easing materializes as expected.

Key developments

  • 10-year Treasury yield drops 45 basis points to 3.82%, the lowest level since February 2024, generating substantial capital appreciation for bondholders
  • Treasury auction demand surges with 30-year bond bid-to-cover ratio reaching 2.67, the strongest showing in nearly four years
  • Fed funds futures now price 78% probability of at least three rate cuts in 2024 as inflation moderates to 2.7% year-over-year
  • Foreign holdings of U.S. Treasuries increase $89 billion in September with renewed Japanese and Middle Eastern buying
  • Hedge funds reverse positioning from net short to $42 billion net long in Treasury futures as technical momentum shifts bullish

"The government bond market is experiencing a fundamental repricing as investors recognize we've reached the peak in this rate cycle. With inflation normalizing and the Fed likely to begin easing in the first half of next year, Treasuries offer compelling value at current levels with the potential for significant total returns as yields compress further."

Sarah Brennan, Head of Fixed Income Strategy at Vanguard Asset Management
