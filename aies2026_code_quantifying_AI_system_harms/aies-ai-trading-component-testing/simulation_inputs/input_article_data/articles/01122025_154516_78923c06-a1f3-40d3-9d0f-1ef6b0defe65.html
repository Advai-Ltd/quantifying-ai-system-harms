Agency MBS Spreads Stabilize as Market Digests Mixed Housing Data

The agency Mortgages Backed Securities (MBS) market registered a period of consolidation this week, with spreads holding within established ranges relative to benchmark Treasuries. Traders noted moderate volume as the market digested mixed signals regarding housing inventory and employment data, suggesting investors are maintaining a cautious, wait-and-see approach.

Eleanor Vance, Fixed Income Correspondent in New York, Fixed Income Desk 25/07/2024 14:35:00

Trading in the To-Be-Announced (TBA) market remained relatively subdued throughout the trading session, reflecting the lack of major directional conviction among institutional investors. Fannie Mae 5.0 percent current coupon instruments saw spreads remain firmly anchored near the +115 basis point level over the 10-year swap rate, a range that has defined price action for the past two weeks. This stability suggests that prevailing market risks—primarily duration risk and incremental supply from the Federal Reserve's balance sheet runoff—are currently priced appropriately.

A key factor contributing to the neutral outlook is the predictable behavior of prepayment speeds. The latest reporting showed the Conditional Prepayment Rate Average (CPRA) tracking slightly slower than market consensus estimates, registering at 8.2% annual rate. High prevailing mortgage rates continue to dampen refinancing activity substantially, effectively minimizing extension risk for higher coupon securities, thus stabilizing their valuation and demand.

While the broader fixed income complex experienced mild volatility driven by evolving expectations for future rate cuts, the relative insulation of the MBS sector provided a buffer. Analysts at Citadel Prime noted that robust demand from money managers needing yield enhancement, paired with manageable supply from agency issuances (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac), has prevented any significant widening pressure.

However, structural challenges remain. The Federal Reserve continues its quantitative tightening (QT) program, allowing up to $35 billion in MBS to run off its balance sheet monthly. While this process is passive, it represents a steady supply overhang that caps potential spread tightening. Market participants are keenly watching upcoming speeches from Fed officials for any deviation in the expected QT timeline or pace, which could shift the current equilibrium.

The overall tone reflects a market waiting for a macro catalyst, either a significant acceleration in inflation data or a marked downturn in housing metrics, to force a directional move. Absent such signals, liquidity remains adequate, and relative value trading strategies dominate, reinforcing the tight spread environment.

Mortgage application volumes, a leading indicator for future origination supply, showed only a marginal decline last week, suggesting the pipeline for new supply will remain consistent but not overwhelming. This balance between supply and sustained investor demand is crucial to maintaining the current neutral stance in the secondary market.

Key developments

  • FNCL 5.0 spreads ended the week largely unchanged, settling near +115 basis points over the 10-year swap rate, signaling equilibrium.
  • Recent prepayment speeds (CPRA) tracked marginally slower than consensus estimates, stabilizing security cash flows and minimizing extension risk.
  • The ongoing, passive $35 billion monthly runoff by the Federal Reserve continues to impose moderate supply pressure, preventing significant spread tightening.

"The current pricing levels in the TBA market appear to fairly reflect the inherent duration risk and the backdrop of steady, albeit modest, Fed runoff. We see spreads tightly anchored until a definitive shift in either monetary policy expectations or significant housing market contraction occurs."

David Chen, Head of Fixed Income Strategy, Olympus Capital Management
