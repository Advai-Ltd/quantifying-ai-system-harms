Agency Spreads Widen Significantly as Extension Risk Hits MBS Sector Hard

The Mortgage-Backed Securities (MBS) sector is under acute pressure, with spreads over benchmark Treasuries reaching multi-year highs as ongoing interest rate volatility increases duration and extension risk. Agency MBS, typically seen as a low-risk segment, are experiencing significant price declines as investors grapple with reduced liquidity and mounting fears that sustained high rates will keep homeowners from refinancing, locking in lower prepayment speeds and extending bond maturities far beyond initial projections.

Julian Vance in New York 09/11/2024 10:45:00

MBS spreads continue their relentless march wider, signaling profound stress in the fixed income market's second-largest component. The current coupon spread on 30-year agency MBS (Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac) has ballooned to approximately 180 basis points over the 10-year Treasury, marking levels not consistently seen outside of severe economic dislocations. This aggressive widening is primarily attributed to a sharp repricing of extension risk.

Extension risk, the threat that the effective maturity of the bond extends beyond expectations due to low prepayment rates, has become the dominant bearish narrative. With mortgage rates hovering near 8%, refinancing activity has ground to a halt. Data released by the Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA) shows prepayment speeds (PSRs) registering near decade lows across a vast swathe of outstanding MBS pools, particularly those carrying coupons below 5%. Investors are thus forced to hold low-yielding debt for much longer, depressing market prices.

Compounding the duration pain is the significant structural shift in market participants. The Federal Reserve continues its quantitative tightening (QT) program, removing a crucial anchor buyer from the market, while regional banks and REITs, traditionally large holders of agency MBS, are showing dwindling demand due to liquidity concerns and balance sheet constraints. This withdrawal of major buyers has exacerbated volatility and constrained market depth significantly.

Dealer desks report that liquidity in the TBA (To Be Announced) market is deeply constrained. Bid-ask spreads for standard agency production have expanded, reflecting a widespread reluctance among dealers to take on inventory risk in an environment where interest rate forecasts remain highly uncertain. This lack of smooth trading function further penalizes existing holders and raises the hurdle rate for new investors.

Furthermore, while agency MBS benefit from government backing, the underlying fundamentals of the housing market are deteriorating. Although credit quality remains robust for now, high rates are freezing transaction volumes, and any future economic slowdown or uptick in unemployment poses a latent threat to forbearance and delinquency rates, which could indirectly pressure agency guarantees and, more acutely, non-agency private label MBS (PLMBS) segments.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS current coupon spread widened 15 basis points (bps) last week, hitting 180 bps over the 10-year Treasury yield.
  • Prepayment speeds (PSRs) registered 5-year lows across most Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac pools due to sustained high mortgage rates.
  • Market depth for MBS liquidity has tightened dramatically following reduced demand from the Federal Reserve and commercial banks.

"The market is fundamentally mispricing the duration risk currently embedded in legacy low-coupon MBS. We believe the Federal Reserve’s higher-for-longer stance means sustained pain for these assets, leading to further downside revisions in their fair value models as extension timelines are baked in."

Cassandra Hale, Head of Fixed Income Strategy at Atlas Capital Management
