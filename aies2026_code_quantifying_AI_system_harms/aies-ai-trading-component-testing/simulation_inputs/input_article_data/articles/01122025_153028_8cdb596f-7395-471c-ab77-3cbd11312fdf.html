Mortgage-Backed Securities Rally as Fed Signals End to Rate Hikes, Spreads Tighten to 18-Month Lows

Mortgage-backed securities are experiencing their strongest performance since early 2022, with agency MBS spreads tightening to 18-month lows as investors pile into the sector on expectations of Federal Reserve rate cuts and improving housing market fundamentals. The Bloomberg US MBS Index has gained 3.2% this month, outperforming Treasuries, while prepayment risks decline and technical factors support continued strength in the asset class.

Sarah Chen and Michael Rodriguez in New York 15/03/2024 14:32:18

Mortgage-backed securities are staging a powerful rally as shifting Federal Reserve policy expectations and stabilizing housing market conditions drive investor demand to levels not seen since before the central bank's aggressive rate-hiking campaign began. Agency MBS spreads have compressed to just 142 basis points over comparable Treasuries, the tightest level since September 2022, while trading volumes have surged 47% above the three-month average as portfolio managers rotate into the sector.

The Bloomberg US MBS Index has delivered total returns of 3.2% so far this month, significantly outpacing the 2.1% gain in the broader Treasury market and marking the strongest monthly performance for the asset class in nearly two years. Fannie Mae 30-year 5.5% coupons, the most actively traded securities in the market, have rallied more than two points to trade at 101-16, while Ginnie Mae securities have posted even stronger gains on robust demand from banks and real estate investment trusts seeking stable cash flows.

Market participants cite multiple tailwinds supporting the bullish momentum in MBS. Primary among them is the Federal Reserve's pivot away from rate increases, with futures markets now pricing in three quarter-point cuts by year-end. This dovish shift has dramatically reduced extension risk—the concern that homeowners will be locked into higher-rate mortgages for longer periods—making MBS duration characteristics more attractive to institutional investors. Additionally, mortgage origination volumes have declined 22% year-over-year, creating a supply-constrained environment that is providing technical support for prices.

The improving technical backdrop is complemented by strengthening housing market fundamentals that are boosting confidence in the underlying collateral. Home prices have stabilized after last year's correction, with the S&P CoreLogic Case-Shiller National Home Price Index posting its fourth consecutive monthly gain. Mortgage delinquency rates remain near historic lows at just 3.1%, while credit enhancement levels on new issuance continue to provide robust protection for investors. Furthermore, the return of foreign central banks as significant buyers of agency MBS has added an important demand component that had been absent during the Fed's quantitative tightening program.

Bank portfolio managers, who had been net sellers of MBS throughout 2023, have reversed course and added $18.3 billion in agency MBS holdings over the past two months, according to Federal Reserve data. This shift reflects improved liability management as deposit costs stabilize and banks seek to deploy excess liquidity into higher-yielding assets with implicit government backing. Money managers are also increasing allocations, with mutual funds and exchange-traded funds tracking fixed-income benchmarks adding MBS exposure to capture the attractive risk-adjusted returns relative to corporate credit and municipal bonds.

Analysts expect the positive momentum to continue as seasonal factors turn favorable and the Fed potentially begins purchasing MBS again to manage its balance sheet runoff. Prepayment speeds, which had accelerated earlier this year and pressured returns, have moderated to more manageable levels as the refinancing wave subsides. With specified pool premiums expanding and pay-up valuations for prepayment-protected securities reaching their highest levels since 2021, sophisticated investors are finding ample opportunities to generate alpha through security selection and structural positioning. The combination of central bank support, technical strength, and improving fundamentals has created what many market participants view as the most compelling opportunity in agency MBS since the pandemic-era disruptions of 2020.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads tightened to 142 basis points over Treasuries, the narrowest level in 18 months, as investor demand surges on Fed dovish pivot
  • Bloomberg US MBS Index returns 3.2% month-to-date, outperforming Treasuries by 110 basis points in strongest rally since early 2022
  • Banks reversed selling trend and added $18.3 billion in MBS holdings over past two months as deposit costs stabilize and yield advantage improves
  • Mortgage delinquency rates hold at 3.1% near historic lows while home prices post fourth consecutive monthly gain, strengthening credit fundamentals
  • Foreign central banks return as significant buyers and specified pool pay-ups reach highest levels since 2021, providing technical support

"The convergence of Fed policy expectations, supply constraints, and renewed institutional demand has created a perfect storm for MBS outperformance. We're seeing the most constructive technical and fundamental backdrop for agency mortgage securities in over two years, and we expect spreads to grind tighter as the Fed moves closer to cutting rates."

Jennifer Park, Head of Mortgage Strategy at Cornerstone Capital Markets
