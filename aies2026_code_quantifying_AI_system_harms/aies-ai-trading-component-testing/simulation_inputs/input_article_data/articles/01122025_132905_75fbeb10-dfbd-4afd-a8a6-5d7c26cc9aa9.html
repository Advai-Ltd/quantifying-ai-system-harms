Global Government Bonds Rally as Yields Slide — Investors Double Down on Duration

Government bonds across major markets rallied sharply as incoming inflation signals and softer central bank commentary pushed yields lower. Strong demand in primary auctions and sizable ETF inflows underpinned a broad-based bullish move in sovereign debt, extending a multi-week repricing toward lower terminal rates and prompting investors to extend duration exposure.

Eleanor Grant, Senior Fixed Income Correspondent in London 01/12/2025 09:30:00

Government bonds staged a broad rally on Monday, with benchmark 10-year U.S. Treasury yields tumbling to 3.12% from 3.48% over the prior session as traders digested a string of softer inflation indicators and read central bank rhetoric as more accommodative-than-expected. The move was global in scope: 10-year Bund yields slipped to 0.84% while the UK 10-year Gilt fell to 3.05%, underscoring a synchronized move into sovereign debt that traders described as a clear duration-driven wave.

Primary market dynamics amplified the rally. The U.S. Treasury's reopened 30-year sale was met with robust demand — a bid-to-cover near 2.9x — and the UK's latest gilt auction came in more than three times oversubscribed, according to dealers. Portfolio managers cited sizable order books and aggressive real-money participation as evidence that the rally was supported by structural demand rather than a short-term technical squeeze, with global government bond ETFs recording net inflows of approximately $8.1 billion across the week.

Macro drivers were central to the bullish tone. Recent core inflation measures cooled more than expected, pushing breakeven inflation rates lower (the five-year breakeven dipped to roughly 1.60%) and compressing real yields. Market-implied policy path indicators now price a longer pause and a higher probability of rate cuts in 2026, a re-pricing that has favored long-duration instruments. The benchmark 2s10s curve has flattened materially, narrowing to around 40 basis points from about 72 basis points three weeks ago, reflecting heavy positioning in longer-dated maturities.

Traders and strategists highlighted technical and flow-based catalysts for further upside in prices. Hedge funds covering short positions, insurance companies lengthening duration for liability matching, and pension funds rotating into long-dated paper all contributed to a liquidity-driven rally that increased convexity demand. Repo market functioning remained orderly through the move, while swap spreads compressed, indicating healthy dealer balance sheets and the ability to warehouse additional sovereign issuance if needed.

Market participants cautioned that the rally, while broad, is conditional on the persistence of lower inflation and a dovish tilt from central banks. Analysts at Meridian Capital Markets and Northshore Asset Management reiterated a constructive view on sovereign bonds — forecasting modest further yield compression if downside risks to growth remain contained. Yet they noted that any unexpected reacceleration in services inflation or a step-up in fiscal issuance could trigger a swift repricing, particularly at the long end where duration risk is concentrated.

Key developments

  • 10-year U.S. Treasury yield falls to 3.12% from 3.48% as inflation prints and central bank commentary weigh on rate expectations.
  • UK gilt auction oversubscribed with a bid-to-cover of ~3.8x, signalling strong real-money demand for duration.
  • Global government bond ETFs record roughly $8.1bn of net inflows across the week, supporting continued price momentum.

"We view the current move as more than a technical rebound — it's a structural shift in positioning. Softer inflation and a more patient central bank narrative have created a favorable environment for duration, and real-money investors are seizing the opportunity to extend duration and lock in long-term yields."

Laura Chen, Head of Rates Strategy, Meridian Capital Markets
