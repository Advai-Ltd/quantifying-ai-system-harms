Global Government Bonds Rally as Yields Slide on Dovish Central Bank Signals and Strong Demand

Government bonds across major markets rallied sharply, with benchmark yields falling as investors priced in a softer inflation trajectory and a more conciliatory central bank stance. Strong primary-market demand and large-scale duration buying by institutional investors underpinned the move, reinforcing a bullish outlook for sovereign debt and sparking renewed appetite for carry and curve-steepening trades.

Eleanor Park in London 01/12/2025 14:32:00

Global government bond markets staged a convincing rally on Monday, with benchmark yields moving decisively lower as markets digested a series of macro prints and signalling from central banks consistent with a more patient policy path. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield declined 18 basis points to 3.18%, while the 2-year fell 12 bps to 2.95%. Similar moves were seen in Europe: the German 10-year Bund dropped 15 bps to 1.85% and the UK 10-year Gilt fell 20 bps to 3.08%. The sell-off in sovereign yields drove prices higher across the curve and lifted total return for core sovereign indices.

Market participants pointed to a combination of softer-than-expected inflation metrics, cautious forward guidance from major central banks and persistent demand from long-duration investors as the principal drivers of the rally. Monthly consumer price readings showed sequential slowing in core inflation components, lowering real yield expectations and trimming breakeven inflation rates—U.S. 10-year break-evens eased by roughly 8 bps. Fed minutes released last week and recent comments from senior ECB officials have been interpreted as removing some hawkish policy risk, prompting yield-sensitive investors to add duration exposure.

Primary issuance dynamics reinforced the bullish technical picture. A reopened U.S. 10-year Treasury auction attracted a bid-to-cover of 3.9x, with indirect bidders accounting for an estimated 74% of accepted bids—an indication of strong dealer and foreign demand. Meanwhile, the UK Debt Management Office reported above-average foreign participation in Gilts auctions this month, and the Bundesbank noted improving covered bond demand that has coincided with greater appetite for Bunds. Pension funds and insurance companies accelerated duration purchases to lock in higher nominal yields, while hedge funds covered short positions amid the rapid price move.

Structurally, the rally led to a pronounced flattening of the front-end to belly of the curve: the 2s10s spread compressed to 23 bps from 38 bps at the start of the week, reflecting stronger moves in 10-year tenors. Carry-seeking managers increased exposure to five- and seven-year maturities where pick-up in coupon and lower realised volatility made convexity-adjusted carry attractive. Analysts at Sterling Capital Analytics said relative-value opportunities emerged between on-the-run and select off-the-run issues, recommending laddered increases in duration and tactical allocations to high-quality nominal bonds over inflation-linkers while breakevens remain subdued.

Looking ahead, the consensus among strategists is cautiously bullish: should headline inflation continue to trend lower and central banks maintain a data-dependent but patient stance, sovereign yields are likely to test lower levels in the near term. That said, markets remain sensitive to upside inflation surprises or an abrupt shift in monetary policy communications. For now, portfolio managers are shifting modestly toward long-duration exposures and are favouring liquid benchmarks while using swaps and futures to manage incremental duration—a positioning that risks amplifying moves if flows accelerate further.

Key developments

  • U.S. 10-year Treasury yield down ~18 bps to 3.18% as investors price a softer inflation path and delayed policy tightening.
  • Primary market strength: U.S. 10-year reopen auction covered 3.9x with ~74% indirect participation, signalling robust foreign and dealer demand.
  • Curve dynamics show 2s10s compression to ~23 bps and stronger demand for 5- to 10-year maturities, prompting tactical duration increases by pension funds and insurers.

"The technicals and macro backdrop are aligning for a sustained phase of lower sovereign yields — with disinflation and patient central bank messaging giving investors confidence to extend duration and pick up real carry in high-quality government bonds."

Dr. Marcus Ellery, Head of Fixed Income Strategy, Crestview Asset Management
