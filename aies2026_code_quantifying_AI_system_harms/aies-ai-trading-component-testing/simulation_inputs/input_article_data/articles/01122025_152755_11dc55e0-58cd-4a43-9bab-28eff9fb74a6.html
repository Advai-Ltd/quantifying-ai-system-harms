Mortgage-Backed Securities Rally as Fed Signals Dovish Pivot, Spreads Tighten to Seven-Month Lows

Mortgage-backed securities are experiencing their strongest rally in over a year as improving liquidity conditions and expectations of Federal Reserve rate cuts drive investor demand. Agency MBS spreads have tightened 18 basis points since mid-December, with production coupons outperforming amid renewed optimism about housing market stabilization and refinancing activity resurgence.

Rachel Morrison and David Chen in New York 15/01/2025 14:32:17

The mortgage-backed securities market is enjoying a robust start to 2025, with agency MBS posting their best performance since late 2023 as investors pile into the sector amid brightening prospects for interest rate cuts and improved housing market dynamics. Fannie Mae 30-year 5.5% coupons—the current production coupon—have tightened 18 basis points to Treasury yields since mid-December, now trading at +132 basis points, the tightest spread since June 2024.

Trading volumes have surged as real money accounts and hedge funds return to the sector, attracted by improved carry profiles and reduced volatility in interest rate markets. Daily trading volumes in agency MBS have averaged $285 billion over the past two weeks, up 34% from the fourth quarter average, according to SIFMA data. The rally has been particularly pronounced in higher coupons, with Fannie Mae 6.0% securities outperforming by 0.75 points as prepayment concerns diminish and investors position for potential refinancing waves.

The Federal Reserve's gradual reduction of its MBS portfolio has been absorbed smoothly by private sector demand, with dealers reporting strong bid lists from asset managers, insurance companies, and banks seeking duration and yield pickup. The central bank's MBS holdings have declined to $2.34 trillion, down approximately $380 billion from peak levels, yet the market has demonstrated remarkable resilience. Specified pool premiums have also firmed, with low loan balance pools trading 2-3 points over TBA prices, reflecting robust demand for call protection.

Housing market fundamentals are providing additional tailwinds for MBS performance. Mortgage applications have risen for five consecutive weeks, up 18% from October lows, as prospective homebuyers anticipate further rate declines. Delinquency rates for agency mortgages remain near historic lows at 1.47%, while home price appreciation continues to support credit performance. Analysts at Goldman Sachs upgraded their outlook for agency MBS last week, projecting current coupon spreads could tighten an additional 10-15 basis points if the Fed delivers the three rate cuts currently priced into futures markets.

The credit risk transfer market is also experiencing strong momentum, with Fannie Mae CAS and Freddie Mac STACR bonds rallying as investors gain confidence in the housing credit cycle. Recent issuance has been met with oversubscription ratios exceeding 3.5x, with mezzanine tranches attracting particular interest from credit-focused funds seeking attractive risk-adjusted returns. The improvement in market sentiment has enabled the GSEs to transfer record amounts of credit risk to private capital, reducing taxpayer exposure while deepening market liquidity.

Looking ahead, market participants expect the favorable conditions to persist through the first half of 2025, supported by technical factors including reduced new issuance growth, steady Fed MBS runoff creating predictable supply, and strong bank demand driven by improved profitability in MBS holdings as short-term funding costs decline. Several major dealers have raised their year-end targets for current coupon spreads, with JPMorgan forecasting spreads could reach +120 basis points by year-end if economic conditions remain supportive and housing market momentum continues building.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads tightened 18 basis points since mid-December, reaching seven-month lows with current production coupons at +132 bps
  • Daily MBS trading volumes surged 34% to average $285 billion, driven by institutional reinvestment and hedge fund positioning
  • Mortgage applications increased for five consecutive weeks, up 18% from October as homebuyers anticipate further rate cuts
  • Credit risk transfer securities oversubscribed by 3.5x in recent deals, reflecting strong investor confidence in housing credit fundamentals
  • Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan raised MBS outlook, projecting potential for additional 10-15 basis points of spread tightening

"The setup for agency MBS looks as constructive as we've seen in 18 months. You have improving technicals with steady Fed runoff, strong fundamental credit performance, and a Fed that's clearly shifting toward accommodation. We're advising clients to add duration through MBS rather than Treasuries given the valuation opportunity and carry advantage."

Jennifer Blackwood, Head of Securitized Products Strategy at Barclays Capital
