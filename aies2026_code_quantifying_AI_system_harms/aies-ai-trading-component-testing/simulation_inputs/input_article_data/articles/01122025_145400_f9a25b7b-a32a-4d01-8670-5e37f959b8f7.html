Government Bond Markets Hold in Narrow Ranges as Mixed Data Keeps Yields Muted

Global government bond yields traded in a tight band on Monday as mixed economic data and steady central bank rhetoric left investors without a clear directional cue. Treasuries, gilts and bunds saw modest moves as supply expectations and technical positioning — rather than fresh macro surprises — shaped intraday flows.

Clara M. Donovan, Senior Markets Correspondent in London 01/12/2025 09:27:00

Government bonds across major markets traded with little conviction on Monday, reflecting a broader risk-on/risk-off equilibrium that has characterized the asset class in recent weeks. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield was quoted around 4.05% (down ~2 basis points on the day), the UK 10-year gilt hovered near 3.45% (flat), and the German 10-year bund edged up to 2.05% (up ~1 basis point). Trading volumes were moderate as investors awaited a calendar of scheduled auctions and upcoming data releases.

Market participants said the tone was shaped more by supply dynamics and position-squaring than by a shift in the macro outlook. Mixed U.S. data released last week — with consumer-price measures showing moderation while payrolls remained resilient — left rate expectations broadly unchanged; futures continue to price the Fed's path as data-dependent with no decisive move priced for the December meeting. Real-money accounts and liability-driven investors remained active buyers at the curve belly, supporting the 5- to 7-year sector.

Technicals were a central theme: the Treasury's upcoming supply profile and dealer inventory constraints limited outright directional bets. The Treasury is scheduled to reopen the 10-year note with an indicative offering size near $40 billion next week, while gilt issuance is expected to be steady as the UK Debt Management Office progresses with its financing remit. In the euro area, limited net bund issuance and ongoing ECB reinvestments have continued to cap volatility in core yields.

Curve behavior was subdued but notable in its subtle shifts. The U.S. 2s10s spread tightened by roughly 3 basis points intraday, reflecting modest demand for front-end protection and continued buying in the belly. Inflation breakevens (10-year) were near 2.15%, little changed from Friday, suggesting the market's inflation compensation remained anchored. Swap spreads were broadly unchanged, and repo market strains were absent, supporting a constructive liquidity backdrop for new issuance.

Analysts said the next set of macro prints and calendar events — including later-week employment data, upcoming central bank minutes and scheduled sovereign auctions — will likely provide the next directional impetus. In the absence of a clear macro surprise, the market is expected to remain range-bound, with trading driven by supply rotations and cross-asset flows rather than a re-pricing of terminal policy rates.

Key developments

  • Major yields traded in narrow ranges: U.S. 10-year ~4.05% (-2 bps), UK 10-year ~3.45% (flat), Germany 10-year ~2.05% (+1 bp).
  • Treasury scheduled to reopen a $40bn 10-year issue next week; supply expectations underpin belly demand but cap volatility.
  • 10-year breakevens steady at ~2.15%; swap spreads and repo conditions showed no material stress, keeping technicals supportive but neutral.

"With mixed macro prints and a steady supply calendar, markets are asking fewer directional questions and more technical ones — expect range-bound trading until a clear macro or policy signal emerges."

Evelyn Park, Rates Strategist, Meridian Capital Markets
