MBS Market Holds Steady as Modest Rate Moves Leave Agency Spreads Largely Unchanged

Mortgages backed securities traded in a narrow range on Monday as modest Treasury yield moves and steady issuance expectations left agency spreads largely intact. Market participants described the tone as neutral, with technical factors such as prepayment speeds and dealer inventory driving short-term flows rather than a clear directional shift.

Evelyn Hart, Senior Markets Correspondent in New York 01/12/2025 14:30:00

The agency MBS market exhibited little directional conviction on Monday, with 30-year TBA coupons and current-coupon pools trading in a tight band as investors digested modest moves in the Treasury curve. The 10-year Treasury yield rebounded slightly to 4.12 percent after a morning dip, leaving typical agency 30-year TBAs roughly flat versus Friday, while 15-year product showed marginal outperformance on supply dynamics.

Price and spread activity remained subdued. Fannie Mae 30-year current-coupon pools around 3.5 percent changed hands near $101.38, and 15-year 3.0 percent coupons were quoted roughly $99.75. Option-adjusted spreads on agency MBS hovered close to 55 basis points over Treasuries, a move of only a few basis points from last week. Traders noted daily trading volumes were average, with dealer inventories adequate to meet TBA delivery demand and no significant dealer-induced volatility.

Technical drivers continued to shape day-to-day execution. Market participants pointed to a modest uptick in conditional prepayment speeds, with bulk servicing reports implying PSA profiles edging from 110 to around 125 in some vintage cohorts. That uptick supported slight investor preference for lower-coupon, shorter-duration coupons in the near term. Mortgage rates remained steady, limiting a large-scale refinance impulse and keeping prepayment uncertainty in a moderate range.

Investor flows reflected a neutral appetite for duration risk. Core fixed-income accounts and some regional banks added small MBS positions for carry, while hedge funds opportunistically traded front-end convexity trades around recent volatility. Ginnie Mae issuance penciled in for the week was estimated at roughly 11.5 billion dollars, a level described by strategists as manageable versus dealer absorption capacity and unlikely to meaningfully shift spreads absent a change in rates or prepayment expectations.

Looking ahead, market participants emphasized that headlines from the macro calendar and incremental data on refinance activity will likely dictate short-term direction. Analysts at Arbor Ridge Securities said the market is in a holding pattern, with the most likely scenario being continued narrow trading unless the 10-year Treasury breaks a key technical level. For now, the prevailing view is neutral: limited upside for price and limited downside for spreads absent a clear macro catalyst.

Key developments

  • Agency 30-year TBAs traded in a narrow range; option-adjusted spreads near 55 basis points, changing only a few basis points since Friday.
  • Conditional prepayment speeds showed a modest rise with recent servicing data implying PSA speeds drifting toward 120-130 in certain vintages, supporting demand for lower-coupon paper.
  • Ginnie Mae issuance this week estimated at approximately $11.5 billion, judged by dealers as absorbable without materially shifting market technicals.

"We see the MBS complex in a constructive but neutral posture right now. Technicals are dominating short-term flow and issuance looks manageable, so trades are being priced around carry and convexity rather than a directional bet on rates."

Jordan Mills, Head of MBS Trading, HarborPoint Capital Markets
