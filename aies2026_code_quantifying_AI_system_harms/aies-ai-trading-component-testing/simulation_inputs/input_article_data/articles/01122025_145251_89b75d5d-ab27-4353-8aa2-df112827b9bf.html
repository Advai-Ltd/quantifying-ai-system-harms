Government bond yields hold steady as markets digest steady issuance and mixed macro signals

Global government bond markets traded with little conviction as investors weighed steady issuance and mixed economic data. Tenors across core markets showed modest moves, leaving yields and break-even inflation measures broadly unchanged in a neutral session that reflected balanced demand and supply dynamics rather than a decisive macro pivot.

Emily Carter, Senior Markets Correspondent in London 01/12/2025 09:15:00

Government bond markets opened the week in a largely neutral tone, with core yields moving in a narrow range as traders digested a mix of supply schedules and softer-than-expected regional activity data. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield hovered around 3.85%, up roughly 2 basis points on the day, while the 2-year traded near 4.25%, leaving the 2s10s curve little changed and displaying mild flattening relative to last week.

Primary market activity was a focal point but failed to spur strong directional conviction. A regular-size Treasury auction for 30-year debt cleared at a modestly tighter stop-out yield than two previous auctions, a sign of steady demand, while the Treasury's issuance calendar for the quarter remained broadly in line with expectations. In the U.K., gilt auctions saw bid-to-cover ratios in line with averages, and the Debt Management Office indicated issuance plans consistent with previously communicated guidance.

Market internals suggested positioning, rather than fresh macro news, was dictating moves. Real yields edged slightly higher as break-even inflation rates were largely unchanged: U.S. 10-year breakevens traded near 2.3%, and the five-year five-year forward breakeven remained anchored around 2.4%. Cash–futures basis and repo spreads were stable overnight, pointing to no acute liquidity stress in core bond markets.

Cross-market dynamics were similarly muted. German Bunds remained roughly flat with the 10-year at about 2.45%, while Japanese 10-year yields stayed tightly pinned near the policy band as the BoJ maintained a cautious tone. Currency moves were modest and did not materially influence sovereign curves: the dollar index was marginally firmer, but not enough to trigger broad re-pricing in fixed income.

Analysts described the session as one of consolidation. Portfolio managers told reporters they were monitoring upcoming data and central bank speak rather than shifting duration aggressively. With inflation expectations and growth indicators sending mixed signals, many investors favored tactical, short-duration positions and selective credit exposure rather than a large directional bet in government bonds.

Key developments

  • U.S. 10-year Treasury yield near 3.85%, up about 2 basis points on the day, reflecting a narrow trading range.
  • 30-year Treasury auction clears with modestly tighter stop-out yield; primary market demand described as steady and in line with issuance expectations.
  • Inflation breakevens and short-term real yields largely unchanged, contributing to neutral positioning across core sovereign curves.

"The market looks to be in a consolidation phase — investors are balancing steady supply against mixed macro signals and prefer to wait for clearer guidance from data and central banks before taking larger duration bets."

Mark Reynolds, Head of Fixed Income Research, Northbridge Advisors
