Yield Shock Hits Agency MBS: Spreads Widen to Multi-Year Highs Amid Liquidity Concerns

The Mortgage-Backed Securities (MBS) market is facing intense bearish pressure, driven by aggressive monetary tightening and subsequent duration risk concerns. Agency MBS spreads relative to benchmark Treasuries have widened significantly, reaching levels not seen since the financial crisis volatility of 2020, as investors grapple with uncertain prepayment speeds and shrinking central bank support, signaling a difficult environment ahead for mortgage finance.

Julian Vance in New York 08/07/2024 14:35:10

Fixed income traders are bracing for continued volatility in the Mortgage-Backed Securities sector, as spreads on 30-year Fannie Mae (FNCL) current coupon paper widened another 15 basis points (bps) last week. This recent decompression means the average spread for Agency MBS is now tracking more than 150 basis points over the 10-year Treasury note, well outside the historical average and signaling reduced investor demand for duration and heightened basis risk.

The primary driver of the bearish sentiment remains the hawkish stance of the Federal Reserve. Persistent inflation pressures are forcing the central bank to maintain higher-for-longer policy, leading to significant negative convexity challenges for MBS holders. The uncertainty surrounding the terminal rate has exacerbated volatility, making it increasingly difficult for institutions to accurately hedge their exposure against unexpected shifts in prepayments, which are already subdued due to elevated mortgage rates.

Technical selling pressure is compounding the issue. The ongoing Quantitative Tightening (QT) program continues to drain liquidity, forcing the market to absorb supply that was historically monopolized by the Fed’s balance sheet. Analysts estimate that the withdrawal of central bank support, coupled with reduced foreign investor appetite seeking higher yields elsewhere, has created a structural supply imbalance that is weighing heavily on pricing, particularly for less liquid TBA contracts.

Furthermore, while Agency MBS carries minimal credit risk, the underlying housing market stresses cannot be ignored. Rising affordability concerns and slowing home price appreciation in key metropolitan areas raise long-term concerns about default probabilities, especially in lower-coupon pools that are now trading at deep discounts. This perception of latent risk, even if remote, is contributing to the pervasive bearishness and driving institutional capital towards safer, less complex instruments.

Market participants anticipate that the spread widening may not have peaked. Should economic data continue to signal resilience, prompting further tightening expectations, MBS valuations are poised for additional deterioration. The market is currently demanding higher compensation to hold mortgage paper, reflecting deep skepticism about short-term stabilization or a timely easing cycle from the central bank.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads tracked 20 bps wider last week, pushing the 30-year current coupon spread over 10-year Treasuries to 15-year historical extremes (excluding March 2020 volatility).
  • MBS volatility index (MBVX) surged 18% month-over-month, reflecting surging hedging costs and complexity due to interest rate uncertainty.
  • Net flows into dedicated MBS funds turned negative for the fourth consecutive week, indicating continued institutional and retail divestment.

"We are seeing significant technical strain in the front end of the stack. The market is struggling to absorb the combined impact of QT runoff and persistent duration risk. Until the Fed signals a definitive pivot, pricing in the MBS sector remains vulnerable to further decompression."

Eleanor Chen, Head of Fixed Income Strategy, Citadel Global Capital
