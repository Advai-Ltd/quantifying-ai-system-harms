Global Government Bonds Rally as Yields Slide; Investors Price in Extended Support for Sovereigns

A broad-based rally in government bonds swept global markets today as yields tumbled across the curve, driven by dovish central bank signals, softer inflation breakevens and strong demand in primary and secondary markets. The move strengthened the case for duration exposure among institutions and pushed sovereign bond ETFs to record inflows, underscoring a sustained bullish outlook for government fixed income.

Clara D. Hargreaves, Senior Markets Correspondent in London 01/12/2025 14:30:00

Government bond markets rallied decisively on Monday as sovereign yields fell across major markets, reflecting a renewed investor preference for safe, long-duration assets. The 10-year UK gilt yield dropped 20 basis points to 1.08%, while the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield fell 18 basis points to 1.12%; 30-year maturities led the move with declines of roughly 25-30 basis points. Market participants attributed the move to a combination of dovish central bank language, cooling inflation expectations and stronger-than-expected primary market demand.

Dovish commentary in recent central bank minutes and softer-than-forecast CPI prints in several economies have diminished the odds of further near-term tightening, analysts said, creating a tailwind for government bonds. Minutes from the Commonwealth Monetary Authority released Friday showed policymakers flagged risks from slowing wage growth and signalled openness to maintaining a supportive policy stance longer than markets had priced. That repricing reinforced expectations for lower real policy rates, increasing the attractiveness of nominal sovereign debt.

Primary market technicals amplified the rally. Today's 10-year gilt syndication attracted a bid-to-cover ratio of 3.1, well above the three-month average, prompting the Treasury to offer slightly larger concession guidance. Meanwhile, sovereign bond ETFs saw estimated net inflows of $4.2 billion across the trading day, the largest single-session inflow since mid-2024, according to tracking data compiled by Harbor Capital Markets. Dealers reported heavier-than-usual buy-side interest from pension funds and insurance accounts seeking long-duration exposure to lock in current yields.

Market internals point to a classic bull market pattern: bid-side pressure pushed yields lower while liquidity tightened in the belly and long end, producing pronounced convexity gains for holders. Inflation breakevens narrowed by 6-8 basis points on 10-year tenors, suggesting the rally has both real-rate and nominal components. Swap spreads compressed modestly, reflecting improved sentiment toward sovereign credit and reduced hedge costs for portfolio managers increasing duration. Traders also noted a pickup in cross-border central bank purchases, with two unnamed sovereign investors reported as active participants in the long-end.

Strategists are updating positioning to reflect a more durable bond bull market. Sterling Ridge Research raised its 12-month target for 10-year gilts to 0.85% from 1.10%, citing a lower structural path for inflation and persistent global demand for high-quality sovereigns. Investors, however, remain mindful of macro upside risks; any surprise reacceleration in inflation or a sharp fiscal pivot could test the rally. For now, the market consensus tilts bullish — favoring duration and selective long-dated purchases while monitoring liquidity and policy signals closely.

Key developments

  • 10-year gilts down ~20 bps to 1.08%; 10-year USTs down ~18 bps to 1.12%; 30-year sovereign yields fell ~25-30 bps, signaling strong long-end demand.
  • Central bank minutes and softer CPI readings trimmed near-term tightening expectations, reinforcing demand for duration and driving a bull flattening of major yield curves.
  • Record single-day sovereign ETF inflows (~$4.2bn) and a stronger-than-expected 10-year gilt auction (bid-to-cover 3.1) highlighted robust institutional appetite for government bonds.

"We view the current rally as structural rather than tactical — a recalibration of expectations that favors duration. With real rates moving lower and technicals very supportive, high-quality sovereign paper is likely to remain in demand through the next policy cycle."

Eleanor Grant, Head of Rates Research, Apex Global Fixed Income
