U.S. Mortgage-Backed Securities Hold Narrow Range as Prepayment Signals and Policy Outlook Keep Investors Cautious

Mortgage-backed securities traded in a tight range on Monday as investor attention centered on steady prepayment trends and a largely unchanged Federal Reserve outlook. Agency MBS saw modest spread movement while flows and issuance remained balanced, leaving the market neither overtly bearish nor bullish.

Claire Donovan in New York 01/12/2025 10:15:00

U.S. agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS) held a narrow trading range in early U.S. hours as dealers and portfolio managers digested mixed technicals and a steady macro backdrop. Benchmark 30-year FNMA coupons were essentially flat on the session, with the option-adjusted spread (OAS) on 30-year conventional MBS around +72 basis points versus Treasuries, little changed from Friday's close. The market tone was neutral as participants weighed modest demand from yield-seeking accounts against continued attention to prepayment metrics.

Across the stack, 15-year coupons outperformed slightly on a duration-adjusted basis, with an OAS near +44 basis points. Average yields on agency 30-year coupons sat near 4.30% intraday, while the 10-year Treasury yield traded around 3.58%, keeping MBS spreads comfortably inside the multi-month trading band. Dealers flagged the effective duration of commonly traded 30-year coupons at roughly 4.9 years, with convexity measures remaining muted given prevailing coupon mix and prepayment expectations.

Prepayment dynamics remained a central focus for investors. Market-wide single-month CPR (constant prepayment rate) was reported near 8% and broadly in line with consensus, with seasoned 30-year loans showing modest sensitivity to recent mortgage-rate drift. Originations and refinance activity continue to be constrained by mortgage rates that have stabilized but remain above pandemic lows, limiting upside prepayment risk for the time being and supporting neutral positioning from bookrunners and investors reliant on extension protection.

On the supply and demand front, agency issuance was moderate over the past week, with roughly $12 billion of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac pools brought to market and Ginnie Mae participation steady in the buyer base. ETF flows into agency MBS products were modestly positive, estimated at about $250 million net inflows on Friday, reflecting ongoing allocation from liability-driven and mortgage-focused funds. Repo and financing markets were orderly, and dealers noted no material disruption in the delivery-of-cash pipeline that would force outsized spread moves.

Market participants described the near-term outlook as balanced. With the Federal Reserve communication steady and macro indicators showing gradual cooling in housing activity, investors are positioning for a low-volatility regime in which spread moves are likely to be tactical rather than structural. Portfolio managers said the most likely near-term drivers will be incremental changes in prepayment data, coupon-specific issuance, and technical adjustments around quarter-end balance-sheet management.

Key developments

  • Option-adjusted spreads on 30-year conventional agency MBS remained around +72 bps, reflecting a stable trading band.
  • Market-wide single-month CPR printed near 8%, broadly in line with expectations and limiting dramatic prepayment-driven moves.
  • Primary issuance moderate at roughly $12 billion across agencies while MBS ETFs saw modest net inflows (~$250m), keeping supply-demand balanced.

""The MBS complex is in a holding pattern: prepayments are neither accelerating nor collapsing, and supply is digestible. That keeps spreads rangebound and encourages measured, not aggressive, positioning,""

Jonah Reed, Head of Mortgage Strategy, Harbor Capital Markets
