Agency MBS Spreads Tighten Amid Robust Investor Demand, Signaling Sector Resilience

Agency MBS demonstrated significant strength this week, with spreads tightening by 5 basis points (bps) against the benchmark 10-year Treasury yield, driven by robust institutional demand and stabilizing prepayment speeds. Analysts point to improving underlying housing market fundamentals and anticipated shifts in Federal Reserve purchasing behavior contributing to the sector's positive momentum, suggesting sustained performance above expectations.

Elias Vance, Senior Fixed Income Correspondent in New York 18/07/2024 10:35:00

The Agency Mortgages Backed Securities (MBS) market saw a powerful rally over the past trading sessions, defying earlier concerns about volatility. The average spread on current coupon 30-year Fannie Mae TBA narrowed significantly to T+85 bps, marking the tightest level recorded since the start of the second quarter. This robust movement is largely attributed to sustained global fixed-income flows seeking higher quality, yield-enhanced assets outside of standard government debt.

Investor appetite, particularly from sovereign wealth funds and domestic pension systems, remains remarkably high for high-quality Agency issuance. This strong demand floor is efficiently absorbing new supply, preventing the typical supply-related spread widening that often follows periods of heightened mortgage originations. Furthermore, recent housing data confirms that serious delinquency rates remain near historic lows, providing structural reassurance regarding the credit quality of the underlying collateral pools.

Crucially, prepayment speeds (CPR) have stabilized, addressing a major overhang that previously pressured MBS valuations and convexity profiles. While benchmark interest rates have moderated slightly, the substantial 'lock-in' effect from legacy mortgages originated at significantly lower rates continues to suppress widespread refinancing activity. This resulting stability in duration is highly attractive to buy-and-hold institutional investors focused on reliable cash flow streams.

Market participants are increasingly factoring in the Federal Reserve's predictable approach to balance sheet management, which signals a stable technical environment for Agency MBS holdings. While the Fed is no longer a primary purchaser, the absence of aggressive selling or accelerated run-off programs removes a key source of spread volatility. This predictability allows investors to focus on fundamental value rather than technical positioning.

Analysts at Zenith Financial Group project that continued economic stability and moderating inflation expectations will further support the MBS rally. Their latest report indicates that the current spread environment offers compelling risk-adjusted returns compared to investment-grade corporate debt, suggesting sustained inflows into the sector through the second half of the year, potentially driving spreads below the T+80 bps threshold.

Beyond Agency paper, the non-Agency MBS sector, particularly deals backed by high-quality prime jumbo collateral, is also benefiting from improved sentiment. Falling credit risk premiums, combined with strong consumer balance sheets, are attracting capital that might otherwise be allocated to higher-risk corporate debt, reinforcing the overall bullish outlook for structured credit products tied to housing.

Key developments

  • Fannie Mae TBA current coupon spreads tighten to T+85 bps, the lowest point this quarter, signaling strong institutional buying interest.
  • Prepayment speeds (CPR) have stabilized, enhancing duration predictability and improving the convexity attractiveness of the securities.
  • Underlying housing market fundamentals remain solid, with historically low delinquency rates supporting the robust credit profile of the MBS collateral.

"We are seeing a perfect confluence of technical stability and fundamental strength. The current spread environment offers compelling value for risk-adjusted returns, and the stability in prepayments removes the biggest wildcard. This is a sector poised for continued relative outperformance within fixed income."

Dr. Serena Joshi, Head of Fixed Income Strategy at Stellar Capital Group
