Agency MBS Spreads Stabilize Amid Subdued Prepayment Activity and Steady Supply

The Agency Mortgages Backed Securities (MBS) market is currently range-bound, exhibiting technical equilibrium as steady institutional demand offsets manageable primary supply. Spreads are holding firm, reflecting a balance between persistent inflation concerns and stable duration profiles driven by historically low refinancing speeds.

Marcus A. Sterling in New York, NY 15/11/2024 10:45:00

Agency MBS spreads have demonstrated remarkable stability over the past quarter, with the widely traded FNCL (Fannie Mae) 30-year current coupon hovering within a narrow 5 basis point band relative to benchmark Treasuries. This stability signals a balanced technical environment where both risk and reward appear contained for the intermediate term.

A key driver of this neutral performance is the suppressed state of refinancing activity. With the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate consistently holding above 6.5%, conditional prepayment rates (CPRs) across most conventional cohorts remain in the low single digits. This lack of rapid principal return minimizes extension risk for investors, providing a foundation for tighter duration targeting and technical stability.

On the supply side, new issuance from Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac has been steady but manageable, primarily driven by purchase money mortgages rather than refinancing flows. This predictable supply is being efficiently absorbed by bank portfolios fulfilling regulatory requirements and large institutional fixed-income funds seeking high-quality, government-guaranteed credit exposure.

Market participants are closely watching the Federal Reserve’s ongoing quantitative tightening (QT) program. While the Fed continues to shed MBS from its balance sheet, the pace has been largely priced in by the market. Analysts suggest that unless there is a material acceleration in QT or a sharp, unexpected shift in the yield curve, the current spread environment is likely to persist through year-end.

Basis risk—the chance that MBS yields move out of sync with Treasuries—remains a low-level concern, but strong credit metrics in the underlying housing market are providing a robust counterweight. While housing price growth has decelerated, high home equity levels and low default rates in agency collateral continue to underpin investor confidence in the security structure.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads (FNCL current coupon) traded within 5bps range for the third consecutive month, reflecting technical stability.
  • Conditional Prepayment Rates (CPRs) for high-coupon pools remain historically low, reducing duration uncertainty.
  • Demand from money managers and bank treasuries remains robust, balancing steady purchase-driven issuance supply.

"The current MBS landscape reflects a stalemate between predictable supply dynamics and restrained investor appetite. We see limited potential for significant tightening or widening in the near term unless core inflation surprises materially or the Fed alters its QT schedule dramatically."

Elara Vance, Head of Fixed Income Strategy, Capstone Analytics
