Corporate Bond Market Slides as Spreads Widen and Liquidity Dries Up, Signaling Rising Credit Stress

A sharp repricing in corporate credit has sent investment-grade and high-yield spreads markedly wider, driven by slowing issuance, heavy ETF outflows and a string of downgrades. Market participants warn that a prolonged period of elevated yields and thinner liquidity could materially raise borrowing costs for corporates and pressure weaker credits into distress.

Claire Montoya, Senior Fixed-Income Correspondent in London 01/12/2025 09:15:00

The corporate bond market entered a pronounced risk-off phase this week as macro uncertainty and a wave of negative credit headlines drove a broad-based selloff. Investment-grade option-adjusted spreads widened to roughly 160 basis points — up from 120 bps a month ago — while the high-yield spread ballooned to near 510 bps, according to aggregate TRACE-style secondary market data compiled by Atlas Research. Benchmark 10-year corporate yields climbed sharply, with the Barclays-like corporate index yield moving above 4.4%, amplifying mark-to-market losses for long-duration portfolios.

Liquidity in secondary markets deteriorated as dealers stepped back, and block trade execution costs rose materially. Bid-ask spreads for single-name IG benchmarks have increased by 40-60% versus October averages, and TRACE-equivalent turnover fell 22% week-over-week. Market makers cited higher funding costs and tighter balance sheet constraints after a combination of rising Treasury yields and regulatory capital pressures. The illiquidity premium is now being priced into new issuance, forcing borrowers to either delay tap issuance windows or pay noticeably wider coupons.

Issuance has slowed dramatically: primary market volume for corporate bonds in November dropped 36% from the same period last year, with several large-cap borrowers pulling syndicated deals. According to data from Crestline Capital Markets, deal concessions averaged 18 bps above fair value for IG borrowers, while lower-rated issuers are facing concessions north of 120 bps. The retreat in the primary market coincides with estimated ETF outflows of $3.2 billion from corporate bond funds over the past ten trading days, exacerbating supply-demand imbalances in the secondary market.

Credit fundamentals that were stable earlier in the year are showing signs of strain. Pinnacle Ratings issued multiple downgrades this month, moving three issuers in the energy and healthcare sectors into speculative-grade territory after earnings misses and weaker free cash flow projections. Distressed exchanges and covenant breaches have risen in frequency, and distressed debt desks report an uptick in transfer pricing for credits trading under 70 cents on the dollar. Default probability models run by Verdant Analytics now forecast a 12-month default rate for speculative-grade credits near 3.2%, up from 2.1% two months ago under the same macro assumptions.

Analysts warn that the combination of rising funding costs, tightening liquidity and weaker corporate earnings creates a feedback loop that could sustain bearish conditions. Portfolio managers are increasing cash buffers and shortening duration, while bank treasuries hedge exposure and reduce repo collateralization of lower-rated bonds. Market strategists at Atlas Investment Bank have revised their base case to reflect a protracted period of higher spreads and light issuance, noting potential knock-on effects for corporate refinancing activity in the coming quarters.

Key developments

  • Investment-grade option-adjusted spreads widened to ~160 bps from 120 bps month-over-month, driven by risk-off flows and higher Treasury yields.
  • Primary corporate issuance down 36% year-on-year amid higher issuance concessions and an estimated $3.2bn of ETF outflows from corporate bond funds in the past ten trading days.
  • Multiple downgrades from Pinnacle Ratings pushed several issuers into speculative-grade; 12-month default probability for HY credits revised up to 3.2% by Verdant Analytics.

"We are seeing a classic liquidity shock compounded by fundamentals that are beginning to fray — borrowers are being repriced and pockets of the market that relied on cheap, covenant-lite financing are now particularly vulnerable."

Dr. Marcus Hale, Head of Credit Strategy, Atlas Investment Bank
