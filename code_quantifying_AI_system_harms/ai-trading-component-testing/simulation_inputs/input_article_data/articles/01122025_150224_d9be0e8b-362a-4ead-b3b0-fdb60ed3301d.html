MBS Market Reels as Rising Rates and Spread Widening Trigger Broad-Based Selling

Mortgage-backed securities plunged across the stack as a renewed rise in Treasury yields, hotter-than-expected economic data and increased concerns over credit stress in non-agency pools drove investors to the exits. Agency TBAs and lower-coupon coupons led the decline while non-agency RMBS saw outsized spread widening, signaling a sharper repricing of duration and credit risk in the mortgage complex.

Eleanor Voss in New York 01/12/2025 14:30:00

The U.S. mortgage-backed securities market experienced a pronounced sell-off on Monday, with agency TBAs and non-agency tranches both under pressure after the 10-year Treasury yield jumped 28 basis points to 4.45%. Agency 30-year FNMA TBAs for delivery widened roughly 20-30 basis points versus comparable Treasuries and fell nearly 1.4 points in price on a mark-to-market basis. Traders blamed a combination of stronger payroll and consumer spending prints over the last week alongside hawkish commentary from Fed officials for refreshing rate risk that amplifies MBS convexity losses.

Lower-coupon agency coupons bore the brunt of the move as duration extension hit MBS desks: 2.5% and 3.0% 30-year coupons lost between 1.5 and 2.2 points as market-implied prepayment speeds (PSA) slowed to multi-month lows. Slower CPR assumptions extended the effective duration on legacy coupons, pushing dealers to aggressively hedge by selling longer-dated agency paper into thinner liquidity. The TBA market saw volatility spike with bid-ask spreads widening materially during the session, complicating position management for both hedge funds and bank balance sheets.

Non-agency RMBS and IO/PO structures displayed even more acute strain. Option-adjusted spreads (OAS) on seasoned non-agency 30-year pools widened about 110-140 basis points on the day, reflecting both repricing for higher rates and rising credit concerns in lower FICO, high LTV cohorts. Ginnie Mae lending pools also showed early signs of stress: delinquencies in some servicer buckets ticked up in recent internal performance feeds and pushed risk premia wider for Ginnie paper, particularly in veteran and FHA-heavy vintages.

Market participants highlighted that the sell-off was exacerbated by positioning and liquidity dynamics: levered hedge funds and duration-short strategies rushed to unwind, creating a feedback loop that magnified moves in the most rate-sensitive coupons. Dealers reported conga-line selling into thin bid depth for 15- and 30-year fixed-rate pools, while structured product desks saw outsized flow into higher-coupon and seasoned collateral as investors sought more carry amid volatility. The net effect: weighted-average life (WAL) on many portfolios lengthened and mark-to-market losses mounted across institutional MBS holdings.

Analysts warned the pain may not be short-lived. With the 30-year fixed mortgage rate climbing to an average national rate of 7.25%—up from 6.65% three weeks ago—home purchase demand and refinance activity are likely to remain suppressed, keeping prepayment models subdued and duration risk elevated. Credit-watch desks at regional servicers indicated heightened watchlists on subprime-sourced securities, and several shop-level stress tests showed materially higher loss severities under an extended high-rate scenario. For portfolio managers the calculus has shifted from convexity hedging to liquidity and credit preservation.

Strategic implications are clear: expect further spread dispersion between agency and non-agency paper, a preference for shorter WALs and higher coupons among investors seeking yield with less extension risk, and increased reliance on buy-side liquidity buffers. Unless incoming data materially cools inflationary pressures or the Treasury curve normalizes, the MBS complex is likely to continue pricing in a more conservative, bearish baseline—one where rates stay higher for longer and mortgage collateral underperforms vs. prior consensus.

Key developments

  • Agency TBAs widened 20-30 bps versus Treasuries as 10-year yield jumped 28 bps to 4.45%, triggering heavy selling in lower-coupon pools.
  • Non-agency RMBS option-adjusted spreads widened 110-140 bps on rising credit concerns and thinner secondary-market liquidity.
  • National 30-year fixed mortgage rate climbed to ~7.25%, depressing refinance volumes and lowering market-implied prepayment speeds (PSA), lengthening effective durations.

"The MBS complex is undergoing a rapid repricing for both duration and credit — it’s no longer just about convexity hedges; balance sheets are being re-sorted to account for sustained higher rates and elevated credit risk in lower-quality cohorts."

Sarah K. Donovan, Head of MBS Strategy, BlackHarbor Capital
