Equities Rally Broadens as Earnings Beats and Buybacks Fuel Bullish Momentum

U.S. equities extended gains in a broad-based rally as a string of upbeat corporate results, healthy buyback announcements and continued ETF inflows lifted benchmarks. Investors rotated into cyclical stocks and selective growth names after analysts revised earnings estimates higher, sending the S&P 500 to fresh multi-week highs and underpinning a constructive backdrop for risk assets.

Evelyn Park, Senior Markets Correspondent in New York 01/12/2025 14:32:00

U.S. equity markets advanced across the board on Monday, with breadth supporting an extension of this month’s rally. The S&P 500 climbed 1.6% to 5,120.34, the Nasdaq Composite rose 2.1% to 15,980.27 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 1.0% to 41,230.12. Market breadth was strong — advancing issues outnumbered decliners by roughly 3-to-1 — while the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) fell 12% to 13.4, signaling eased near-term risk sentiment. Market-cap weighted gains were complemented by meaningful participation from mid- and small-cap names, pushing total U.S. equity market capitalization up an estimated $1.1 trillion on the session.

The rally was underpinned by a batch of better-than-expected corporate results and forward guidance that together prompted multiple analyst upgrades. Semiconductor designer Atlas Semiconductor (ATSX) reported Q4 adjusted EPS of $1.42 versus consensus $1.05 and revenue growth of 18% year-over-year, triggering an 8.7% jump in its shares and lifting the broader chip complex. Greenline Energy (GLNE) surprised with an accelerated offshore contract schedule and a higher margin outlook, while Crescent Retail (CRST) beat on same-store sales and raised its full-year comp forecast. Across the S&P 500, net earnings revisions turned positive for the first time in three months, and consensus 2026 EPS growth for the index was revised up to 10.2% from 8.1% a fortnight ago.

Investors also cheered the prospect of continued capital returns. Corporate buyback announcements for the quarter totaled approximately $120 billion, according to market estimates, and multiple large-cap firms confirmed accelerated repurchase programs and higher dividend targets. ETF flow data showed sustained demand for equities — U.S. equity ETFs recorded net inflows of roughly $6.4 billion on the day — with notable allocations into cyclicals and financials as investors rotated from defensives into economically sensitive sectors.

Macro signals were supportive but nuanced. The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield rose modestly to 3.95% (up 6 basis points), while the 2-year yield ticked slightly lower to 4.10% (down 1 basis point), a configuration that has kept the yield curve relatively flat but not inverted. That environment helped financials and industrials outperform as the market priced in steady nominal yields alongside resilient growth. Valuation multiples expanded modestly: the forward P/E for the S&P 500 rose to 18.5 from 18.2 last week, a re-rating analysts attributed largely to upward EPS revisions rather than speculative leverage.

Strategists described the move as a quality rally. ‘‘We’re observing constructive breadth, meaningful earnings resilience and return-of-capital activity — a combination that supports a higher equity risk premium,’’ said a senior strategist at a major asset manager. Technicals reinforced the bullish view: the S&P 500 cleared both its 50- and 200-day moving averages with improving momentum indicators, offering a favorable technical setup into key macro releases scheduled next week, including CPI and the Fed minutes. Still, market participants cautioned that geopolitical developments and any surprise uptick in inflation could test the current optimism.

Key developments

  • S&P 500 up 1.6% to 5,120.34, Nasdaq Composite gains 2.1% as breadth improves and VIX falls to 13.4.
  • Corporate catalysts: Atlas Semiconductor and Crescent Retail beat expectations; consensus 2026 EPS growth for S&P 500 revised up to 10.2%.
  • Equity demand strong: approximate $6.4bn in U.S. equity ETF inflows and $120bn in announced buybacks bolster liquidity and shareholder returns.

"We’re seeing a classic late-cycle re-rating where earnings upgrades, robust buybacks and steady macro prints are driving multiple expansion. Given the breadth of participation and improving fundamentals, we remain overweight U.S. equities into year-end."

Dana Mercer, Head of U.S. Equity Strategy, Meridian Capital Partners
