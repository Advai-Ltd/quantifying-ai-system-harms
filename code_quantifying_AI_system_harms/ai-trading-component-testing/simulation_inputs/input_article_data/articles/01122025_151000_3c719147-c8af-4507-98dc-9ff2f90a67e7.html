Global Equity Markets Face Sharp Selloff as Recession Fears Mount and Valuations Come Under Pressure

Major stock indices posted their steepest weekly decline in over a year as mounting recession concerns, deteriorating corporate earnings outlooks, and elevated valuations triggered widespread selling across global equity markets. The S&P 500 plunged 4.2% while technology stocks bore the brunt of the downturn, with analysts warning that further downside remains as economic indicators continue to weaken and profit margins face compression.

Sarah Mitchell and David Chen in New York 15/03/2024 16:45:32

Global equity markets experienced a dramatic selloff this week as investors fled risky assets amid growing evidence that major economies are sliding toward recession. The S&P 500 dropped 4.2% to close at 4,856 points, its worst weekly performance since October 2022, while the technology-heavy Nasdaq Composite tumbled 5.8%. European markets fared little better, with the Stoxx 600 declining 3.9% and Germany's DAX falling 4.1%. Trading volumes surged to their highest levels in six months as hedge funds and institutional investors aggressively reduced equity exposure.

The selloff was triggered by a confluence of negative factors including weaker-than-expected manufacturing data, rising unemployment claims, and a series of profit warnings from bellwether corporations. Consumer discretionary and technology sectors led the decline, falling 6.3% and 5.8% respectively, as investors reassessed lofty valuations in the context of deteriorating economic fundamentals. The fear gauge, as measured by the VIX volatility index, spiked 47% to reach 28.4, its highest level since the banking crisis of March 2023, signaling heightened market anxiety and expectations for continued turbulence.

Corporate earnings revisions have turned decidedly negative, with analysts at major investment banks cutting their S&P 500 earnings per share estimates for 2024 by an average of 8% over the past month. Margin pressures are intensifying as companies face stubbornly high labor costs, weakening pricing power, and slowing demand across key sectors. Forward price-to-earnings ratios, which had climbed to 19.8 times during the January rally, now appear stretched given the darkening economic backdrop. Goldman Sachs strategists downgraded U.S. equities to underweight this week, citing overvaluation and deteriorating earnings momentum.

The technology sector's decline has been particularly pronounced, with the so-called 'Magnificent Seven' stocks losing a combined $1.2 trillion in market capitalization this week. Semiconductor manufacturers have been hit especially hard following disappointing guidance from industry leader Apex Semiconductor, which warned of inventory buildup and weakening demand from both consumer electronics and data center customers. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged 7.9%, its worst weekly loss in two years, as investors questioned whether the artificial intelligence boom has been overhyped and whether chip stocks can justify their premium valuations.

Emerging market equities have also come under severe pressure, with the MSCI Emerging Markets Index declining 5.4% as investors withdrew capital from riskier assets. Chinese stocks listed in Hong Kong fell to their lowest levels in four months despite government efforts to stabilize markets, while Indian equities retreated from record highs as foreign institutional investors turned net sellers for the first time in six months. The broad-based nature of the selloff has raised concerns about potential forced selling from systematic trading strategies and volatility-targeting funds, which could exacerbate downward momentum.

Looking ahead, market participants are bracing for further volatility as the corporate earnings season approaches and economic data is expected to continue deteriorating. Technical analysts note that the S&P 500 has broken below key support levels at the 200-day moving average, opening the door to a potential test of the October 2023 lows around 4,200. Options market positioning shows unprecedented levels of bearish sentiment, with put-to-call ratios reaching extremes last seen during the 2022 bear market. Fund managers are raising cash levels and implementing defensive strategies, with many questioning whether the two-year equity bull market has definitively ended.

Key developments

  • S&P 500 plunges 4.2% in worst weekly performance since October 2022, with Nasdaq declining 5.8% as technology stocks lead broader market selloff
  • VIX volatility index surges 47% to 28.4, reaching highest level since March 2023 banking crisis as investor anxiety intensifies
  • Analysts slash S&P 500 earnings estimates by average of 8% as corporate profit warnings accelerate and margin pressures mount
  • Magnificent Seven technology stocks lose combined $1.2 trillion in market value with semiconductor sector falling 7.9%
  • Goldman Sachs downgrades U.S. equities to underweight citing overvaluation and deteriorating earnings momentum amid recession fears

"We're seeing a fundamental reassessment of equity valuations as the reality of slowing growth and margin compression sets in. The rally earlier this year was built on overly optimistic assumptions about earnings resilience and Fed policy. With forward P/E multiples still elevated and economic indicators deteriorating rapidly, we believe equities have further downside ahead, potentially 15-20% from current levels before reaching attractive entry points."

Rebecca Harrison, Chief Equity Strategist at Thornbridge Capital Management
