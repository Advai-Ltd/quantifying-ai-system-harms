MBS Markets Slide as Rising Treasury Yields and Seller Flood Trigger Broad-Based Bearish Repricing

Mortgage-backed securities tumbled across agency and non‑agency buckets as a sudden climb in Treasury yields, heavy TBA selling and worsening credit signals forced investors to reprice duration and credit risk. The move widened spreads, pushed convexity‑sensitive paper sharply lower and intensified pressure on originators and balance‑sheet providers, signaling a tougher near‑term outlook for the housing finance complex.

Eleanor K. Price, Senior Fixed‑Income Correspondent in New York 01/12/2025 14:32:00

Mortgage‑backed securities across agency and non‑agency markets sank on Monday as a jump in intermediate and long‑dated Treasury yields triggered forced selling and a rapid repricing of duration risk. The 10‑year Treasury yield climbed to 4.72% (up ~18 basis points intraday), prompting Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac 30‑year coupon TBAs to fall roughly 1.5–2.0 points. Dealers reported FNMA 3.5% coupons widening to about 155 basis points over Treasuries, a move of nearly 20 basis points from last week, while benchmark pass‑through prices slid as convexity costs accelerated.

Market participants cited a confluence of drivers behind the bearish move: stronger than expected economic data that dented hopes for near‑term Fed easing, a resurgence in net mortgage supply as originators stepped back and shed pipeline hedges, and technical dislocations from balance‑sheet constrained banks and regional dealers reducing risk appetite. The combination amplified hedging flows and produced outsized negative mark‑to‑market hits for long MBS positions, particularly in lower coupon and seasoned pools where extension risk is acute.

Credit‑sensitive non‑agency pools felt even more acute pressure. Traders said seasoned jumbo and private‑label pass‑throughs widened 35–50 basis points versus comparable agency paper as 60+ day mortgage delinquencies ticked higher — reported at roughly 3.1% for certain non‑prime vintages in early December — and investors demanded steeper compensation for credit and liquidity risk. Heightened volatility has also caused funding costs for non‑agency servicers and mortgage REITs to spike, worsening a feedback loop of seller supply into the secondary market.

Analysts pointed to slower prepayment expectations as an additional strain on prices. Beacon Mortgage Analytics estimates that CPR for 30‑year fixed pools has dropped to roughly 6% from near 10% six months ago; slower prepayments extend the effective duration of MBS and exacerbate losses in a rising yield environment. Traders noted that the combination of lower CPR and negative convexity is forcing some long‑duration funds to trim positions to reduce drawdown risk, feeding further selling pressure in TBAs and specified pools.

The immediate market impact extended to mortgage origination economics: primary rates rose with the secondary market stress — the average 30‑year conforming rate moved above 7.10% intraday — effectively stalling refinance demand and compressing margins for lenders that must hedge pipeline exposure. Several large originators and hedge desks reported stepping away from forward commitments, increasing downside risk for issuance and amplifying liquidity strains in the TBA market if volatility persists.

Looking ahead, strategists warned the MBS complex may remain vulnerable until either Treasury yields stabilize or dealer inventory returns to healthier levels. ‘‘We view the current move as a technical‑heavy repricing with an overlay of fundamental deterioration; unless supply dynamics change or the Fed signal materially shifts, downside pressure on both agency and non‑agency MBS is likely to continue,” said a senior strategist. For investors, the tradeoff is raw carry versus heightened duration and credit risk — a dynamic that will favor nimble, conviction‑driven positioning and tighter risk management in the near term.

Key developments

  • Agency TBA prices fell 1.5–2 points as 10‑year Treasury yields rose to 4.72%, driving FNMA 3.5% coupon spreads wider by ~20 bps versus Treasuries.
  • Non‑agency and jumbo pools widened 35–50 bps amid rising 60+ day delinquencies (~3.1% in some vintages) and higher funding costs for servicers and REITs.
  • Prepayment speeds (CPR) projected to slow to ~6% from ~10% six months ago, increasing extension risk and negative convexity losses for long‑duration MBS holders.

""We see this as a structural re‑price — not just a temporary dislocation. Higher rates, slowing prepayments and constrained dealer inventories are combining to push both agency and non‑agency MBS into bear territory; investors should assume volatility and wider spreads are the base case until supply and funding dynamics normalize.""

Daniel R. Hayes, Head of Mortgage Strategy, Harborview Securities
