Treasury Yields Surge to 16-Year Highs as Bond Sell-Off Intensifies Amid Inflation Fears

Government bonds suffered their steepest losses in over a decade as benchmark 10-year Treasury yields climbed above 5.2%, driven by persistent inflation concerns and mounting fiscal deficits that are forcing investors to demand higher returns. The broad-based sell-off has wiped out $340 billion in bond market value this month alone, with central bank officials signaling rates may remain elevated for longer than previously anticipated.

Sarah Mitchell and David Chen in New York 15/11/2024 14:32:17

Government bond markets extended their brutal sell-off Thursday, with the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield jumping 18 basis points to 5.24%, its highest level since July 2007. The 30-year bond yield climbed to 5.51%, while the 2-year note touched 5.15%, inverting the yield curve further and signaling deep pessimism about near-term economic prospects. Trading volumes surged to $712 billion, the highest single-day total in eight months, as institutional investors rushed to dump holdings amid deteriorating technical conditions.

The carnage in fixed-income markets reflects growing alarm over the Federal Reserve's inability to bring inflation back to its 2% target despite 18 months of restrictive monetary policy. Core PCE inflation printed at 3.7% last month, well above expectations, while wage growth accelerated to 4.8% annually. Market participants now price in a 73% probability that the Fed will maintain its benchmark rate at current levels through the second quarter of 2025, abandoning previous expectations for rate cuts that had buoyed bond prices earlier this year.

Compounding the bearish sentiment, the Congressional Budget Office released revised fiscal projections showing the federal deficit reaching $2.3 trillion for fiscal year 2024, significantly above the $1.7 trillion forecast made just six months ago. The ballooning supply of government debt has overwhelmed demand, with foreign buyers notably absent from recent Treasury auctions. China reduced its holdings by $31 billion in September, marking the seventh consecutive month of net selling, while Japan trimmed positions by $18 billion. The reduced foreign appetite has pushed the term premium—the extra yield investors demand for holding longer-dated bonds—to its highest level since 2011.

European government bonds mirrored the U.S. downturn, with German 10-year Bund yields rising 14 basis points to 3.12% and UK Gilts climbing to 4.87%. The synchronised global sell-off suggests structural concerns rather than country-specific issues are driving the bearish momentum. Portfolio managers at major pension funds and insurance companies face mounting mark-to-market losses, with the Bloomberg Global Aggregate Bond Index down 8.3% year-to-date, on pace for its worst annual performance since the index's inception in 1990.

Technical indicators paint an increasingly dire picture for bond bulls. The 10-year Treasury has broken through multiple resistance levels, with the 50-day moving average crossing decisively below the 200-day average—a so-called death cross that technical analysts view as confirmation of a sustained downtrend. Momentum indicators show the most oversold conditions since March 2020, yet attempted rallies have been met with aggressive selling. Options markets show elevated demand for put options on bond ETFs, with the TLT long-dated Treasury fund seeing put-call ratios reach 2.1-to-1.

The deteriorating bond market conditions are beginning to ripple through the broader financial system. Mortgage rates have climbed back above 8% for 30-year fixed loans, crushing refinancing activity and new home sales. Corporate borrowing costs have spiked, with investment-grade credit spreads widening by 47 basis points this month as companies delay planned bond issuances. Regional banks with large unrealized losses on their bond portfolios face renewed scrutiny, with the KBW Bank Index falling 6.2% this week on concerns about balance sheet pressures. Market veterans warn that without a meaningful catalyst—either a significant growth slowdown or unexpected inflation deceleration—the bond market rout shows little sign of abating.

Key developments

  • 10-year Treasury yields surge to 5.24%, highest since 2007, as inflation remains stubbornly elevated at 3.7%
  • Federal deficit projections revised upward to $2.3 trillion, overwhelming bond market demand as foreign buyers retreat
  • Global bond sell-off erases $340 billion in value this month, with Bloomberg Aggregate Index on track for worst year on record
  • Technical breakdown confirmed as 10-year yield crosses death cross threshold, signaling extended bearish momentum ahead

"We're witnessing a fundamental repricing of sovereign risk that could persist for years. The combination of structural inflation, unsustainable fiscal trajectories, and the Fed's credibility crisis creates a perfect storm for government bonds. Investors who thought 4.5% yields were attractive six weeks ago are now staring at 5%+ and wondering if even that's enough compensation for the risks ahead."

Gregory Hartman, Chief Fixed Income Strategist at Blackstone Capital Management
