MBS Rout Deepens as Yields Spike and TBA Liquidity Dries Up, Investors Warn of Prolonged Losses

A broad sell-off in mortgage-backed securities intensified as agency yields jumped and TBA prices plunged, widening spreads and exposing portfolios to duration and liquidity stress. Market participants say the combination of rising Treasury yields, reduced Fed support and deteriorating private-label credit metrics points to further downside for MBS valuations and mounting mark-to-market losses for leveraged holders.

Eleanor Park and Marcus Chen in New York 01/12/2025 09:15:00

A sharp move higher in benchmark Treasury yields on Monday triggered one of the largest single-day markdowns in mortgage-backed securities this quarter, with the 30-year agency MBS yield jumping roughly 24 basis points to 4.21%. The Fannie Mae 30-year 4.0% TBA coupon fell more than 1.5 points to 97-16 (97.50), and generic 30-year TBAs traded through the low 97s, as bid-side liquidity evaporated during the worst of the session.

Spread dynamics have turned notably bearish: nominal spreads of agency MBS to Treasury widened to roughly 118 basis points from around 106 bps last week, while non-agency and private-label RMBS underperformed, with spread moves in the 40–60 bps range on select mezzanine tranches. Market-makers cited a buildup of forced selling in hedge funds and bank portfolios as higher funding costs and margin calls pushed stop-losses through thin TBA books.

Investors flagged several structural drivers compounding the sell-off. The Federal Reserve's pace of balance sheet runoff and the tapering of emergency MBS reinvestments have removed a key buyer from the tape, dealers reported. At the same time, continuing upward pressure on nominal Treasury yields amplified negative convexity and extension risk for coupon-bearing pass-throughs; market-implied option-adjusted spreads (OAS) for seasoned 30-year coupons widened materially, increasing the cost of hedging for many holders.

Credit performance has begun to show early signs of strain in the private-label segment. Servicer reports and proprietary surveillance data from Ridgeway Analytics show 60-day delinquencies on select non-agency prime jumbo pools rose 90 basis points month-over-month, while Ginnie Mae issuance dropped an estimated 18% sequentially as originations stalled. Traders warned that deteriorating credit metrics in lower-quality pockets of RMBS could force valuation re-rates and tighten bid-offer spreads further.

Market participants also pointed to supply-side pressure. Liquidity-starved originators are increasingly selling seasoned pools to meet funding needs after mortgage application volumes plunged; banks, facing elevated capital charges and funding costs, have reduced MBS holdings by an estimated 4% week-over-week, according to dealer surveys. The confluence of forced secondary supply and a thinner bid list has amplified price moves, particularly in the most rate-sensitive coupons.

Analysts on the desk expect continued volatility and additional spread widening absent a quick technical rebound. Several sell-side strategists forecast another 15–35 basis points of spread widening across select agency coupons if Treasury yields hold above current levels, warning that negative convexity losses could deepen for leveraged institutions and make conventional basis trades uneconomical in the near term.

Key developments

  • 30-year agency MBS yields climbed ~24 bps to 4.21%, pushing FNMA 30yr 4.0% TBA down to 97-16 and widening spreads to Treasuries to ~118 bps.
  • Private-label RMBS underperformed: spreads widened 40–60 bps while 60-day delinquencies on select non-agency pools rose approx. 90 bps m/m; Ginnie Mae issuance estimated down 18% sequentially.
  • Liquidity drain in the TBA market and the Fed's reduced MBS reinvestment have heightened extension and convexity risk, prompting dealers and hedge funds to cut positions; banks reported a ~4% reduction in MBS holdings week-over-week.

"We see the current episode as a technical-driven correction that will morph into a valuation reset unless either Treasury yields retrace or dealer balance sheets rebuild quickly. Expect another 15–30 basis points of spread widening on vulnerable coupons as hedging costs rise and liquidity remains shallow."

Oliver Reed, Head of Structured Products Research, Ridgeway Analytics
