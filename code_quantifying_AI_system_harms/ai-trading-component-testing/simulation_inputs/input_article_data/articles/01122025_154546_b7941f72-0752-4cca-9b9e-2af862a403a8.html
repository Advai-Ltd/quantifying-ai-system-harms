MBS Spreads Hold Tight Amid Stable Prepayment Activity and Measured Fed Influence

The Mortgage-Backed Securities market is currently positioned in a state of relative equilibrium, characterized by persistent institutional demand counterbalancing moderate rate volatility. Agency MBS spreads have remained range-bound, suggesting that the market has efficiently priced in current economic outlooks, leading to predictable performance metrics and subdued directional risk.

Eleanor Vance in New York 15/05/2024 10:45:00

The broader MBS sector, particularly Agency Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac securities, has seen spreads stabilize over the past month, trading tightly against benchmark treasuries. The stability indicates that despite the Federal Reserve's ongoing quantitative tightening (QT), private market liquidity is robust enough to absorb supply without significant disruption. Trading volumes in the To-Be-Announced (TBA) market have been consistent, reflective of steady, though not excessive, underlying mortgage origination activity.

A key factor contributing to the neutral sentiment is the balanced behavior of prepayment speeds. Current 30-year conventional mortgage rates remain high enough to suppress large-scale refinancing waves, keeping prepayment speeds (measured by the Public Securities Association, or PSA) well within consensus forecasts. This predictability in speeds allows asset managers to maintain tighter control over duration risk, mitigating the sudden mark-to-market adjustments often seen during volatile periods.

Investors are focusing on the 'sweet spot' of the coupon stack, with 5.5% and 6.0% Agency coupons seeing consistent interest due to their attractive yield profile relative to their duration characteristics. While yields on lower coupons have compressed slightly due to duration extension fears being priced in, the overall market remains driven by yield curve positioning rather than credit concerns, which remain minimal for Agency paper.

In the non-Agency sector, performance is similarly measured. While spreads for seasoned credit-risk transfer (CRT) transactions have tightened marginally, new issuance remains highly selective. The market is distinguishing clearly between deals with robust underlying collateral and those lacking sufficient credit enhancement, suggesting a cautious, risk-averse approach from institutional buyers rather than an aggressive reach for yield.

Market participants generally agree that the path forward hinges on inflation data and subsequent Fed policy moves. Should the Fed maintain its current 'higher for longer' stance, the current stable environment is likely to persist. Any unexpected deviation, however, could quickly introduce volatility in duration and prepayment expectations, shifting the neutral outlook.

Furthermore, technical factors such as portfolio rebalancing and reinvestment flows from maturing bonds are providing a steady bid floor, preventing any major sell-offs. This technical support underpins the current tight trading ranges observed across the Agency MBS landscape.

Key developments

  • GNMA 5.5% coupons have traded within a narrow 5 basis point range against comparable duration Treasuries over the last trading period.
  • Primary mortgage rates exhibit sufficient volatility to prevent widespread refinancing, maintaining moderate and predictable prepayment speeds (PSA).
  • Institutional demand remains robust for higher-coupon Agency paper, balancing supply from Federal Reserve quantitative tightening.

"The MBS sector is currently exhibiting a healthy equilibrium. While we monitor macroeconomic indicators closely for shifts in interest rate expectations, the current supply/demand dynamics suggest spreads are likely to remain anchored near recent levels for the foreseeable future, barring a major inflation or employment surprise."

Dr. Jeremy Holt, Head of Fixed Income Strategy, Aegis Capital Management
