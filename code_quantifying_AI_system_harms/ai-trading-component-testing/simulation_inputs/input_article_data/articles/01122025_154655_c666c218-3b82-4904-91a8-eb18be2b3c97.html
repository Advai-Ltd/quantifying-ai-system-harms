MBS Spreads Widen to Multi-Year Highs Amid Persistent Rate Volatility and Softening Housing Market

The Mortgage-Backed Securities (MBS) market is grappling with sustained bearish pressure, driven by the Federal Reserve's 'higher for longer' interest rate narrative. Agency MBS spreads have significantly widened against benchmark Treasuries, signaling poor relative valuation and increased duration risk. Institutional investors are pulling back, anticipating further price deterioration as the housing sector shows signs of cooling under the strain of 7%+ mortgage rates.

Evelyn Reed in New York 18/10/2024 10:45:00

The recent sell-off in the Treasury market has cascaded directly into the MBS sector, pushing yields higher and prices lower across the coupon stack. Agency MBS, typically considered a cornerstone of fixed income portfolios due to the implicit government guarantee, is underperforming significantly. The benchmark Fannie Mae (FNMA) 5.5% coupon spread widened by an additional 14 basis points over the past week, reaching levels not seen since the peak rate hike cycle of the previous year.

Market participants are primarily concerned with duration risk. Unlike periods of low rates where prepayment risk (negative convexity) dominated, the current environment sees investors heavily penalized by the extended duration of these assets. With mortgage rates hovering near 7.5%, refinancing activity has been nearly eliminated, meaning the underlying mortgages are remaining outstanding for longer periods than anticipated, locking in low asset prices.

Liquidity has become a serious issue. Major dealers are reducing inventory exposure, citing elevated hedging costs and the inherent uncertainty in forecasting the Fed's next move. This reduction in market depth makes large trades difficult to execute without significant price concessions, further compounding the downward pressure on valuations. This environment heavily favors short-term instruments, leaving long-dated MBS in the cold.

Furthermore, analysts are beginning to look beyond Agency MBS towards the non-agency space, where credit risk is a looming concern. While delinquency rates remain historically low, rising household debt and inflationary pressures could trigger localized stress in Non-Qualified Mortgage (Non-QM) pools. Any uptick in default activity in the non-agency segment is likely to spill over into the perception of risk across the entire housing credit spectrum.

Many institutional buyers who typically provide a floor for MBS pricing are remaining on the sidelines. Large pension funds and insurance companies are exercising caution, often preferring to allocate capital to higher-rated, shorter-duration corporate debt or simply holding cash equivalents, suggesting that the consensus is that the market has not yet found a stable bottom.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads (FNMA 5.5s) widened by 12-15 basis points (bps) this week due to increased rate volatility.
  • The composite yield on new Ginnie Mae pools reached 6.8%, effectively stalling new origination and heightening duration extension concerns.
  • Trading desk surveys indicate that market liquidity has deteriorated to its lowest point this year, forcing major dealers to tighten bid-ask spreads significantly.

"We are currently operating in an environment where the 'buy the dip' strategy for MBS has proven treacherous. The risk-reward profile is skewed heavily towards duration risk, and until we see a definitive reversal in the Fed's stance or significant economic deceleration, investors should expect further erosion of capital."

Dr. Silas Vance, Head of Fixed Income Strategy at Meridian Capital Group
