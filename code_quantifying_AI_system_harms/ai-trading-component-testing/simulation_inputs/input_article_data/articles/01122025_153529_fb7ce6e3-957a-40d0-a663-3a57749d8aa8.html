Mortgage-Backed Securities Face Steepest Sell-Off in 18 Months as Fed Signals Extended Higher Rates

Mortgage-backed securities markets tumbled sharply on Wednesday, with agency MBS spreads widening to their most distressed levels since March 2023, as Federal Reserve officials signaled interest rates will remain elevated longer than previously anticipated. The Bloomberg Barclays MBS Index fell 1.8%, while prepayment speeds plummeted to decade lows, raising concerns about diminished liquidity and mounting duration risk for institutional holders of the $7.2 trillion asset class.

Michael Chen and Sarah Patterson in New York 14/03/2024 16:42:33

The mortgage-backed securities market experienced a brutal trading session Wednesday as investor sentiment soured dramatically following hawkish commentary from Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller, who indicated the central bank sees no urgency to cut rates despite cooling inflation. Agency MBS spreads widened by 12 basis points to Treasuries, the sharpest single-day deterioration since the banking crisis of March 2023, with Fannie Mae 30-year 6% coupons trading at 101-08, down nearly two full points from Friday's close. Trading volumes surged to $287 billion, the highest level in seven months, as institutional investors rushed to reduce exposure amid growing concerns about negative convexity and extension risk.

The sell-off intensified as market participants digested new data showing mortgage prepayment speeds falling to just 4.2 CPR (Constant Prepayment Rate) in February, the slowest pace since 2008 and well below the 6.8 CPR forecast by most Wall Street dealers. The dramatic slowdown reflects homeowners' reluctance to refinance mortgages originated during the pandemic era when rates hovered near 3%, compared to current mortgage rates exceeding 7.2%. This extension risk has forced portfolio managers to dramatically reassess duration exposure, with effective durations on current-coupon MBS now exceeding 7.5 years, up from 5.2 years just six months ago. Goldman Sachs Asset Management reported liquidating $4.3 billion in agency MBS holdings over the past two weeks to manage duration risk.

Technical factors are compounding fundamental headwinds as the Federal Reserve continues its quantitative tightening program, allowing up to $35 billion in agency MBS to roll off its balance sheet monthly. The central bank's MBS holdings have declined to $2.38 trillion from a peak of $2.74 trillion in April 2022, removing a crucial source of price support from the market. Meanwhile, commercial banks—traditionally significant MBS buyers—have pulled back sharply, with aggregate bank holdings declining by $89 billion year-over-year according to Federal Reserve data released Tuesday. Regional banks in particular have reduced MBS allocations by 23% since the March 2023 banking turmoil, prioritizing liquidity over yield as deposit costs remain elevated.

Credit concerns are beginning to emerge in the non-agency MBS sector, where spreads on prime jumbo securities widened by 18 basis points to 145 basis points over swaps, reflecting growing anxiety about housing market fundamentals. Home prices in major metropolitan areas showed their first month-over-month decline in eleven months according to CoreLogic data, while mortgage delinquency rates ticked up to 3.7% in January from 3.4% in October. Fitch Ratings placed 47 non-agency MBS tranches on negative watch Tuesday, citing deteriorating collateral performance and weakening borrower credit profiles. The iShares MBS ETF (MBB) has declined 4.2% year-to-date, underperforming the broader investment-grade corporate bond market by 310 basis points.

Dealer inventories of MBS have swelled to $18.7 billion, the highest level since 2020, suggesting insufficient buyer demand to absorb new issuance and secondary market supply. The elevated inventory positions have forced primary dealers to widen bid-ask spreads to as much as 8/32nds on benchmark securities, up from typical spreads of 2/32nds, effectively reducing market liquidity precisely when investors most need it. Several hedge funds specializing in MBS arbitrage strategies have reported significant losses this quarter, with Bridgewater Capital MBS Fund down 6.8% through February according to investor letters obtained by sources familiar with the matter.

Looking ahead, market participants expect continued pressure on MBS valuations as the technical backdrop remains unfavorable and macroeconomic uncertainty persists. JPMorgan's fixed income strategy team downgraded agency MBS to underweight in their latest allocation guidance, projecting spreads could widen another 15-20 basis points before finding support. Options markets reflect growing hedging demand, with put option volumes on MBS-focused ETFs reaching their highest levels since 2022. Analysts at Barclays estimate that for every 25 basis point increase in mortgage rates from current levels, MBS investors face mark-to-market losses of approximately 2.3%, presenting significant challenges for leveraged portfolios and insurance companies with regulatory capital constraints.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads widened 12 basis points to Treasuries, reaching most distressed levels since March 2023 banking crisis
  • Mortgage prepayment speeds collapsed to 4.2 CPR in February, slowest pace since 2008, dramatically increasing extension risk and duration exposure
  • Federal Reserve's continued quantitative tightening removes $35 billion monthly in price support while commercial bank MBS holdings declined $89 billion year-over-year
  • Non-agency MBS credit spreads widened 18 basis points as home prices decline and delinquency rates rise to 3.7%, prompting Fitch to place 47 tranches on negative watch

"The combination of Fed hawkishness, collapsing prepayment speeds, and vanishing bank demand has created a perfect storm for mortgage-backed securities. We're advising clients to significantly reduce MBS exposure and shift toward shorter-duration fixed income alternatives until technical conditions stabilize."

Rebecca Thornton, Head of Securitized Products Strategy at Morgan Stanley
