Mortgage-Backed Securities Face Steep Selloff as Fed Signals Extended Higher Rates, Spreads Widen to 18-Month Highs

Mortgage-backed securities suffered their worst weekly decline since March, with agency MBS spreads widening 12 basis points as investors flee the sector amid rising prepayment risks and deteriorating housing market fundamentals. The benchmark Fannie Mae 30-year 6% coupon fell 2.3 points to 95-08, pushing yields to levels not seen since late 2022, while non-agency RMBS prices tumbled as delinquency forecasts rose across subprime and Alt-A tranches.

Sarah Mitchell and David Thornburg in New York 15/11/2024 16:42:33

The mortgage-backed securities market experienced a brutal selloff this week as investors reassessed their positions following unexpectedly hawkish commentary from Federal Reserve officials and mounting evidence of stress in the housing finance sector. Agency MBS spreads to Treasury yields widened dramatically, with the current coupon spread hitting 175 basis points—the widest level since May 2023. Trading volumes surged 34% above the monthly average as portfolio managers rushed to reduce duration exposure, while the TBA dollar roll market showed signs of severe dislocation with negative implied financing rates across multiple coupons.

The deterioration accelerated Wednesday after Fed Chair Jerome Powell indicated that recent inflation data warranted maintaining restrictive monetary policy longer than previously anticipated, dashing market hopes for rate cuts in early 2025. Higher-for-longer rate expectations triggered immediate repricing across the MBS universe, with specified pool premiums collapsing as hedging costs spiked. Fannie Mae 5.5% coupons, which represent a significant portion of outstanding agency MBS, fell 1.8 points to 92-16, while Ginnie Mae II securities underperformed amid concerns about FHA loan performance. The Bloomberg U.S. MBS Index posted a negative 2.1% total return for the week, its worst performance since the regional banking crisis.

Non-agency residential mortgage-backed securities fared even worse, with subordinate tranches of recent vintage deals trading down 4-7 points as credit concerns intensified. Investors pointed to rising delinquency rates in the non-QM space, where 60-day delinquencies jumped to 4.7% in October from 3.2% three months earlier, according to data from Recursion Co. Analytics. Legacy Alt-A deals from pre-crisis vintages saw renewed selling pressure as housing price appreciation slowed markedly in key markets including Phoenix, Austin, and Boise, where year-over-year price gains decelerated below 2%. Credit default swap spreads on non-agency RMBS indices widened 45 basis points, reflecting growing anxiety about potential losses in lower-rated tranches.

The technical backdrop for MBS has deteriorated sharply in recent months as the Federal Reserve continues to allow its $2.4 trillion agency MBS portfolio to run off through paydowns and maturities without reinvestment. The central bank's holdings declined by $38 billion in October alone, removing crucial price support from the market. With the Fed now a net seller and commercial banks reducing MBS allocations due to balance sheet constraints and adverse duration characteristics, the sector has lost two of its most consistent bid sources. Money managers estimate that the market needs to attract between $80-120 billion in additional private capital over the next 12 months to absorb supply, requiring spreads to widen substantially further to clear.

Prepayment dynamics have also turned increasingly unfavorable for MBS investors. With mortgage rates hovering near 7.5% for conventional 30-year loans, refinancing activity has ground to a halt, extending the duration of MBS portfolios beyond what many investors modeled. The Mortgage Bankers Association reported that the refinance share of applications fell to just 28.4% of total volume, near historic lows. This duration extension is particularly painful in a rising rate environment, amplifying mark-to-market losses. Meanwhile, faster-than-expected prepayments on older, lower-coupon securities continue as homeowners relocate or default, creating negative convexity that compounds investor losses.

Looking ahead, strategists see little relief for the beleaguered sector. Goldman Sachs Asset Management downgraded agency MBS to underweight in its fixed income strategy, projecting spreads could widen another 15-25 basis points before stabilizing. The housing market fundamentals continue to weaken, with existing home sales down 18% year-over-year and mortgage purchase applications at levels last seen during the 2008 financial crisis. Credit rating agencies have begun placing negative outlooks on certain non-agency shelf programs, citing deteriorating underwriting standards and weakening borrower profiles in the expanding non-QM market. With technical and fundamental factors aligned negatively, institutional investors are bracing for further weakness through year-end and into early 2025.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads widened 12 basis points this week to 175 bps over Treasuries, reaching 18-month highs as investors fled duration risk following hawkish Fed commentary
  • Non-agency RMBS subordinate tranches declined 4-7 points with non-QM delinquencies surging to 4.7% in October from 3.2% in July, raising credit loss concerns
  • Federal Reserve MBS holdings fell $38 billion in October as quantitative tightening continues, removing crucial price support while commercial banks reduce allocations
  • Mortgage rates near 7.5% have caused duration extension as refinancing activity collapses to historic lows, amplifying negative convexity losses for investors
  • Goldman Sachs downgrades agency MBS to underweight, projecting spreads to widen another 15-25 basis points amid deteriorating housing market fundamentals

"The technical setup for MBS has rarely looked worse. You have the Fed in full runoff mode, banks stepping away due to capital constraints, and no natural buyer willing to step in at current spread levels. We need to see 200 basis points or wider before this market finds a sustainable clearing level."

Jennifer Albrecht, Head of Securitized Products Strategy at Westbridge Capital Management
