Global Government Bonds Sell-Off Intensifies as Yields Spike on Hawkish Central Banks and Weak Demand

A broad-based sell-off in government bonds accelerated on Monday as hawkish central bank comments, larger-than-expected issuance plans and softer auction demand pushed yields sharply higher. The move has heightened volatility across core markets, dented safe-haven flows and forced investors to reassess duration exposure amid rising term premia and sticky inflation expectations.

Evelyn Carter, Senior Markets Correspondent in London 01/12/2025 10:23:45

Government bond markets delivered a coordinated repricing early in the week as benchmark yields climbed across the U.S., UK, Germany and Japan. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield jumped 22 basis points to 4.48% in London trade, while the UK 10-year gilt rose 20 basis points to 4.12%. German 10-year Bunds were up 16 basis points at 2.18%, reflecting a broader reassessment of the path for rates and an expanding term premium. Traders cited a mix of hawkish central bank comments, larger sovereign supply and disappointing primary market demand as drivers of the sell-off.

Auction dynamics amplified the move. The U.S. Treasury's latest 7-year note auction attracted a bid-to-cover of 2.30, down from 2.58 at the prior comparable issue, and dealers were seen stepping in more aggressively to absorb stock. Primary market dealers and foreign official buyers reduced participation, with estimated foreign allotments falling to roughly 16% versus a recent average above 22%. Market participants interpreted the weaker demand as a signal that the abundant calendar of issuance will keep upward pressure on yields in coming months.

Central bank signals hardened risk sentiment. Fed minutes released overnight underscored lingering upside risks to inflation and a bias among officials toward keeping policy restrictive longer than markets had priced. Comments from a senior policymaker in the Bank of England suggested tolerance for a period of higher yields to anchor inflation expectations, curbing hopes of an imminent easing cycle. The combined messaging pushed real yields higher and widened breakeven inflation measures across several tenors, indicating that investors are demanding more premium for duration risk.

Technically, the move has been driven by rising term premia and deteriorating liquidity in the long-end of the curve. Hedge funds and systematic strategies have aggressively cut duration exposure, exacerbating price moves as algorithmic sellers hit the market. The 30-year sector underperformed, with duration-sensitive portfolios recording significant mark-to-market losses. Portfolio managers described an environment in which convexity costs are rising and traditional hedges such as long-dated swaps are becoming more expensive and less effective.

Market strategists warned that the confluence of persistent fiscal deficits, a heavy calendar of sovereign issuance and tighter global policy stances creates a challenging backdrop for fixed income. Analysts at boutique shop Harborne Analytics raised their 12-month U.S. 10-year yield forecast to 4.75% from 4.25% and flagged downside risks for price-centric bond funds. Investors are said to be rotating into shorter-duration instruments, higher-coupon corporates and inflation-linked securities where liquidity and carry characteristics offer partial protection against further yield spikes.

Key developments

  • U.S. 10-year yield surged ~22 bps to 4.48% after weak 7-year auction and hawkish Fed minutes; bid-to-cover at Treasury auction fell to 2.30.
  • Sovereign supply outlook tightened: fiscal plans indicate an increase in issuance next quarter, pressuring long-end liquidity and pushing term premia higher.
  • Foreign official demand for core sovereigns softened (estimated allocations down to ~16%), amplifying selling pressure and widening spreads in secondary markets.

"We think the market is re-pricing a structurally higher term premium — this is not a temporary blip. Higher-for-longer central bank expectations combined with chunky issuance means clients should prepare for more sustained volatility and higher yields than they have assumed for much of this year."

Dr. Aisha Rahman, Head of Fixed Income Strategy, Meridian Capital Markets
