Equities Slide as Growth Stocks Lead Downturn; Investors Flee Risk on Rising Yields and Weak Guidance

Equity markets turned decisively negative as a combination of hawkish central bank signaling, a spike in longer-dated Treasury yields and a string of disappointing corporate updates prompted broad-based selling. Growth-oriented sectors bore the brunt, while measures of market breadth and liquidity deteriorated, underscoring an increasingly risk-off environment that is reshaping positioning ahead of next week's macro calendar.

Laura M. Benton, Markets Correspondent in New York 01/12/2025 14:32:00

Global equity benchmarks sank in early trade as investors reassessed risk premia after fresh commentary from central banks and a raft of profit warnings. The S&P 500 fell 1.8% at mid-afternoon, the Nasdaq Composite declined 2.6%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 0.9%. The move came alongside a notable rise in volatility: the CBOE VIX jumped to 19.4 from 15.7 on the prior session, signaling elevated investor concern about forward equity returns.

Pressure was most acute in high-multiple growth names after a batch of earnings releases and guidance cuts. Software-maker Aureon Systems reported quarterly revenue 7% below consensus and slashed full-year guidance, sending its shares down 38% and wiping roughly $18 billion off its market capitalisation. Meanwhile, healthcare developer Noventa Pharmaceuticals warned that a late-stage trial would be delayed, knocking the biotech cohort and extending outflows from the sector. These company-specific disappointments amplified a technical unwind of crowded long positions in growth stocks.

Macro dynamics reinforced the bearish tone. The 10-year Treasury yield rose 12 basis points to 4.21% as markets priced a longer trajectory of policy-tightening risk, prompting a classic duration-for-equity repricing that penalises long-duration growth cash flows. Investment-grade credit spreads widened by 12 basis points on the day and the S&P 500 put-call ratio climbed to 1.02, indicating heightened demand for downside protection. Equity mutual funds and ETFs recorded net outflows of approximately $8.4 billion, the largest two-day exit since earlier in the year, according to exchange-traded fund data compiled by market analytics firm CrestPoint.

Market internals deteriorated sharply: advancers-to-decliners on the NYSE stood at 0.35, and the number of new 52-week lows across U.S. listings rose to 432, a meaningful increase from last week's averages. Sector rotation was explicit — consumer discretionary and information technology led losses, while traditional defensive segments like consumer staples and utilities outperformed but still ended slightly negative as risk-off flows favoured cash and short-duration assets. Trading liquidity thinned in the afternoon with average bid-ask spreads widening 15–20% in the most heavily traded growth names.

Analysts said the present sell-off is as much about positioning and convexity as it is about fundamentals. 'This is not just a pullback; it's a re-pricing of terminal-rate expectations interacting with stretched valuations and concentrated positioning in momentum strategies,' said Morgan Vale, senior equity strategist at Redcrest Capital. 'Until we see either clear evidence of inflation easing or more constructive corporate guidance, market participants should expect elevated volatility and episodic downside.' Market participants flagged an increased prevalence of stop-driven selling in derivative-heavy names, which could intensify moves if macro surprises worsen.

Key developments

  • Growth stocks underperform: Nasdaq Composite down 2.6% as several high-multiple software and biotech names report misses and guidance reductions.
  • Rates-driven repricing: U.S. 10-year Treasury yield climbs to 4.21% (+12 bps), pressuring long-duration equities and prompting rotation into cash and short-duration bonds.
  • Liquidity and flows weaken: Equity ETFs and mutual funds record ~$8.4bn net outflows, put-call ratio rises above 1.0 and new 52-week lows increase to 432, indicating broadening market stress.

""The current rout reflects a convergence of macro tightening expectations and stretched equity valuations — with crowded long positions and derivatives amplifying moves. Until corporate guidance stabilises against this backdrop, downside risk remains higher than usual,""

Morgan Vale, Senior Equity Strategist, Redcrest Capital
