Treasury Yields Plunge as Inflation Data Sparks Rally in Government Bonds

U.S. government bonds surged on Wednesday as softer-than-expected inflation figures fueled expectations that the Federal Reserve will pivot toward rate cuts sooner than anticipated, sending the 10-year Treasury yield tumbling 18 basis points to 3.92% in its largest single-day decline in seven months. The rally extended across the yield curve as investors piled into safe-haven assets amid growing confidence that monetary policy tightening has successfully cooled price pressures without triggering a recession.

Michael Thornton and Sarah Chen in New York 15/11/2024 16:42:33

Government bonds delivered their strongest performance in months on Wednesday as investors scrambled to reprice interest rate expectations following a surprise cooling in core consumer prices. The benchmark 10-year Treasury note rallied sharply, pushing yields down to 3.92% from 4.10% at Tuesday's close, while the 2-year note fell 22 basis points to 4.18%. Bond prices, which move inversely to yields, soared across all maturities as the inflation data reinforced expectations that the Federal Reserve's aggressive monetary tightening campaign has achieved its desired effect.

The Labor Department's report showed core CPI rising just 0.2% month-over-month in October, below the 0.3% consensus forecast, with the annual rate decelerating to 3.3% from 3.6% in September. The softer inflation print immediately triggered a wave of buying in the Treasury market, with trading volumes in 10-year futures jumping 34% above the 30-day average. Analysts at Goldman Sachs Economics now estimate a 72% probability of a 25 basis point rate cut at the Fed's March meeting, up from 48% before the data release.

Institutional demand for government bonds has intensified dramatically as the improving inflation outlook coincides with mounting concerns about global economic growth and geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. Central banks and sovereign wealth funds have been particularly aggressive buyers, with foreign official holdings of U.S. Treasuries increasing by $47 billion in the latest month, according to Federal Reserve custody data. The Japanese Government Pension Investment Fund, the world's largest pension fund, confirmed this week it has increased its allocation to U.S. government securities by 230 basis points to 18.7% of total assets under management.

The technical picture for government bonds has also improved considerably, with the 10-year yield breaking decisively below its 200-day moving average for the first time since July. Chart analysts at Merrill Lynch Technical Research identified the move as a 'significant bullish signal' that could pave the way for yields to test the 3.75% level by year-end. Real yields on inflation-protected securities have fallen even more sharply, with the 10-year TIPS yield dropping to 1.68%, its lowest level since March, suggesting investors are pricing in substantially easier monetary conditions ahead.

Market strategists have grown increasingly optimistic about the total return potential for government bonds heading into 2025. Duration-based strategies, which suffered historic losses during the 2022 rate hiking cycle, are now attracting significant inflows as fund managers reposition for a dovish shift in Fed policy. The Bloomberg U.S. Treasury Index has gained 2.8% over the past month, its best performance since the banking sector turmoil in March, and is now positive for the year with a 1.4% return. Bond mutual funds recorded $12.3 billion in net inflows last week, the strongest weekly intake in 16 months, according to EPFR Global data.

Looking ahead, several structural factors support a continued bullish outlook for government bonds. The U.S. Treasury's recent decision to moderate the pace of longer-dated debt issuance has alleviated supply concerns that had weighed on the market earlier this year. Meanwhile, the Bank of Japan's commitment to maintaining ultra-loose monetary policy ensures continued strong demand from Japanese investors seeking yield differentials. With inflation trending toward the Fed's 2% target and economic growth indicators showing signs of moderation, the bond market appears positioned for further gains as the interest rate cycle turns decisively in favor of fixed-income investors.

  • 10-year Treasury yield plunged 18 basis points to 3.92%, largest single-day drop in seven months following softer inflation data
  • Core CPI rose only 0.2% month-over-month, below consensus forecasts, with annual rate decelerating to 3.3% from 3.6%
  • Foreign official holdings of U.S. Treasuries increased by $47 billion as central banks and sovereign wealth funds accelerate purchases
  • Goldman Sachs now assigns 72% probability to March Fed rate cut, up from 48% before inflation report

"The inflation trajectory is now clearly moving in the right direction, and the bond market is finally getting the recognition it deserves as the ultimate safe haven. We're advising clients to extend duration aggressively here because the risk-reward has shifted dramatically in favor of government bonds. This could be the beginning of a sustained multi-quarter rally."

Jennifer Ashworth, Chief Fixed Income Strategist at Morgan Stanley Wealth Management
