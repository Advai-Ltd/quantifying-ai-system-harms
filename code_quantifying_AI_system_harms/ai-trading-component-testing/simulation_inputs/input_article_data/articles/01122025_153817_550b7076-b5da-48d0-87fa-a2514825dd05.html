Major Indices Retreat as Sticky Inflation Expectations Bite; Tech Leads Market Sell-Off

US equity markets faced steep losses Tuesday, extending the recent downward trend as robust inflation data solidified expectations for prolonged high interest rates. The technology sector, particularly growth stocks sensitive to rising borrowing costs, bore the brunt of the sell-off, signaling diminishing appetite for risk assets across Wall Street and pushing the S&P 500 below a key technical support level.

Jessica Thorne, Senior Markets Correspondent in New York 18/07/2024 10:45:00

Wall Street saw red across the board as benchmark indices registered their worst session in three weeks. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 650 points (1.8%), while the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite dropped 3.1%, dragged down by disappointing forward guidance from several bellwether companies and the continued shadow of hawkish central bank rhetoric. The market breadth was overwhelmingly negative, with declines outpacing advances by more than 5-to-1 on the NYSE.

The bearish pressure intensified following the release of the latest Producer Price Index (PPI) figures, which came in hotter than anticipated, suggesting input cost pressures are far from resolved. This heightened concern that the Federal Reserve may be forced to maintain restrictive policy longer than investors had hoped, leading to a significant repricing of terminal rate expectations and applying further downward pressure on high-multiple growth stocks.

Chipmakers and consumer discretionary stocks were particularly vulnerable. Shares of Innovate Dynamics Corp., a fictional large semiconductor manufacturer, plunged nearly 8% after Aethelred Capital downgraded its rating to 'Underweight,' citing deteriorating demand in enterprise hardware and bloated inventory levels. This sentiment was echoed across the sector, highlighting fears that the inventory correction cycle is deeper and longer than previously modeled.

The technical picture also darkened significantly. The S&P 500 failed to hold crucial support levels near the 4,900 mark on heavy selling volume, potentially opening the door for a retest of the early-year lows established during the initial rate-hiking scare. Traders are keenly watching bond yields, where the 10-year Treasury yield climbed another 5 basis points, reinforcing the unattractive comparative yield environment for equities, especially for companies reliant on future earnings growth.

Furthermore, corporate warnings regarding margin compression have become pervasive. Companies are increasingly struggling to pass rising labor and materials costs fully onto consumers, leading to expectations of substantially reduced profit margins throughout the second half of the fiscal year. This profit erosion dynamic provides a fundamental headwind that technical factors alone cannot overcome.

Until clear evidence of inflation deceleration emerges or corporate outlooks stabilize, many strategists suggest investors should adopt a defensive stance. Defensive sectors like utilities and healthcare showed relative resilience but were ultimately unable to counteract the broad market decline stemming from macroeconomic jitters and profound valuation concerns.

Key developments

  • S&P 500 breaches key 4,900 support level on heavy selling volume, signaling further downside risk.
  • High-multiple growth stocks endure significant pressure as 10-year Treasury yield hits a three-month high, making future cash flows less valuable.
  • Leading firms downgrade corporate earnings forecasts citing margin compression from persistent input costs and weakening consumer demand.

"We are transitioning into a true bear market cycle defined by multiple compression and profit erosion. The optimism priced in during the spring was premature; liquidity is shrinking, and risk is being severely underestimated by passive holders."

Dr. Evelyn Reed, Chief Market Strategist at Aethelred Capital
