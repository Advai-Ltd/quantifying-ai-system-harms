MBS Market Holds Steady as Fed Policy Outlook and Housing Data Balance Risk Factors

Mortgage-backed securities prices remained relatively stable through mid-week trading as investors weighed mixed housing market data against expectations for Federal Reserve policy direction. Agency MBS spreads held within recent ranges while prepayment speeds showed modest seasonal adjustments, reflecting a market in equilibrium as participants await clearer signals on interest rate trajectory and housing supply dynamics.

Rebecca Chen and Thomas Whitmore in New York 15/03/2024 14:23:17

The mortgage-backed securities market exhibited stable trading patterns this week as investors digested a confluence of economic indicators that provided no clear directional catalyst. Agency MBS spreads to comparable Treasury securities remained within the 165-175 basis point range that has characterized trading over the past month, with Fannie Mae 30-year 5.5% coupons settling at approximately 101-08, roughly unchanged from the prior week's close.

Housing data released this week presented a mixed picture that reinforced the current neutral stance among MBS investors. Existing home sales increased 1.4% month-over-month in February, slightly above consensus estimates, while new home starts declined 3.2% during the same period. Mortgage application volumes remained flat week-over-week, with refinancing activity accounting for approximately 31% of total applications, consistent with recent trends as homeowners remain locked into lower rates from previous years.

Prepayment speeds on agency MBS pools showed modest seasonal variation, with the Mortgage Bankers Association reporting Constant Prepayment Rates (CPR) averaging 5.8% across conventional 30-year pools, up slightly from 5.4% the previous month but well below the long-term average of 12-15%. The persistent low prepayment environment continues to provide stable cash flow profiles for MBS investors, though it also reflects the limited refinancing opportunities available given current rate levels relative to the outstanding loan pool.

Market participants reported steady institutional demand for agency MBS from traditional buyers including banks, insurance companies, and asset managers seeking high-quality fixed income exposure. Issuance of new agency MBS totaled approximately $185 billion in February, in line with seasonal expectations and consistent with purchase activity in the housing market. The Federal Reserve's continued holdings of approximately $2.3 trillion in MBS remain a significant factor in market structure, though the passive runoff has become a well-established baseline condition.

Credit performance metrics across agency and non-agency MBS sectors remained stable, with serious delinquency rates holding at 1.8% for conventional loans and 3.2% for FHA-insured mortgages, both figures representing modest improvements from year-ago levels. Non-agency MBS spreads in the investment-grade sector tightened marginally, with AAA-rated securities trading at approximately 95-105 basis points over swaps depending on vintage and collateral characteristics.

Looking ahead, analysts anticipate the MBS market will continue to trade within established ranges absent significant shifts in Federal Reserve policy signals or unexpected deterioration in housing fundamentals. The current rate environment has created relative stability in prepayment assumptions and credit performance, while spread levels reflect balanced supply and demand dynamics. Investors remain focused on upcoming inflation data and Fed communications as key inputs for reassessing duration and convexity positioning in their MBS portfolios.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads maintained 165-175 basis point range over Treasuries with Fannie Mae 5.5% coupons settling near 101-08
  • Prepayment speeds averaged 5.8% CPR across conventional pools, up slightly from prior month but below historical averages
  • Housing data showed mixed signals with existing sales up 1.4% while new starts declined 3.2% month-over-month
  • Agency MBS issuance totaled $185 billion in February, consistent with seasonal patterns and market expectations
  • Serious delinquency rates held steady at 1.8% for conventional loans with credit metrics showing continued stability

"The MBS market is currently reflecting a wait-and-see posture among investors. With prepayment risk contained and credit fundamentals stable, we're seeing consistent bid-side interest at current spread levels, but there's limited conviction to drive meaningful tightening or widening without a clearer catalyst from either Fed policy or housing market dynamics."

Jennifer Caldwell, Head of Securitized Products Strategy at Thornbridge Capital Management
