Treasury Yields Dive Below 4.0% Threshold as Cooling Inflation Fuels Aggressive Rate Cut Bets

Global sovereign bond markets are witnessing robust demand, driving yields significantly lower across the curve, as new inflation data strengthens expectations for imminent and deeper interest rate reductions by major central banks. The benchmark 10-year Treasury yield dipped below the critical 4.0% psychological threshold for the first time this quarter, signaling renewed confidence in the fixed income sector.

Elias Vance in New York 20/07/2024 10:35:00

The relentless rally in government bonds gained fresh momentum today following the release of softer-than-expected core consumer price index (CPI) figures, prompting institutional investors to aggressively price in a more dovish pivot from the Federal Reserve. The move solidifies the fixed income market's bullish stance, which had been tentative earlier in the week amidst lingering concerns about persistent wage growth.

US Treasury yields saw broad declines, with the 10-year note dropping 15 basis points (bps) to settle at 3.98%. The long end of the curve outperformed, indicating strong conviction among investors that inflation risks are receding and that locking in current yields provides significant value ahead of a predicted decline in the risk-free rate. The 30-year bond yield fell by 18 bps, further unwinding the recent curve steepening observed during periods of high economic uncertainty.

Demand metrics underscore the prevailing bullish sentiment. Yesterday’s auction of $24 billion in 20-year bonds drew exceptionally strong coverage ratios, fueled primarily by pension funds and international sovereign wealth portfolios eager to meet long-term duration matching requirements. This institutional appetite demonstrates deep-pocketed conviction that the current high-yield environment is temporary.

Analysts at Zenith Securities noted that momentum indicators suggest the bond market may still have room to run. They cite technical analysis pointing to support levels around the 3.85% mark for the 10-year yield, arguing that if forthcoming employment data confirms a slowdown, yields could breach that level swiftly. The current market narrative is shifting decisively from 'higher for longer' to 'cuts are imminent'.

Furthermore, the bullish trend is global. German Bund yields are mirroring the move, falling to their lowest levels in two months, reacting both to domestic deceleration and spillover effects from US market optimism. This synchronized global rally suggests a broad-based recalibration of recessionary risk and monetary policy expectations across developed markets, bolstering the appeal of high-quality sovereign debt.

Key developments

  • 10-year US Treasury yield drops 15 basis points to 3.98%, breaching key technical support following softer inflation data.
  • Market expectations now fully price in 75 basis points of Federal Reserve rate cuts before year-end, driven by decelerating Core CPI.
  • Strong auction results for 20-year Treasuries signal institutional appetite for long-duration debt remains robust, confirming flight-to-quality dynamics.

"The combination of decelerating price pressures and cautious economic forecasts provides the perfect tailwind for fixed income. We believe the secular bull market in bonds is resuming, favoring duration exposure heavily through Q4."

Dana Sterling, Head of Global Fixed Income Strategy at Nexus Capital Management
