Global Government Bond Markets Reprice Aggressively as Yields Surge on Sticky Inflation, Weak Auction Demand

Government bond markets turned decisively bearish on Monday as stronger-than-expected inflation signals, hawkish central bank guidance and a heavy supply calendar combined to push yields sharply higher and trigger widespread selling across maturities. The move increased borrowing costs for sovereigns, widened swap spreads and prompted significant outflows from sovereign bond ETFs, underscoring heightened market stress in the fixed-income complex.

Eleanor Park in London 01/12/2025 14:35:00

Global sovereign yields climbed across the curve in a broad-based selloff that market participants described as the most pronounced move in months. The US 10-year Treasury yield jumped about 25 basis points to trade near 4.25% intraday, while the 30-year yield rose roughly 28 basis points to 4.65%. Shorter-dated maturities were not spared: the 2-year yield moved up to 5.10%, reflecting renewed conviction that central banks will maintain restrictive policy settings for longer than previously priced.

Traders cited a mix of persistent inflation readings, hawkish forward guidance from major central banks and a heavier-than-anticipated government issuance schedule as the catalysts for the rout. UK gilt yields mirrored the US move, with the 10-year gilt up more than 30 basis points after a weak long-dated auction saw a notable tail versus pre-auction indications. European sovereigns also repriced higher, driven in part by a widening of term premia and deteriorating technicals as dealer inventories filled quickly.

Market structure dynamics amplified the decline in bond prices. ETF providers reported estimated outflows of $2.3 billion from government bond ETFs during the session, while futures positioning showed net short accumulation by macro funds. Swap spreads widened as dealers struggled to hedge the rapid move—US 10-year swap spreads blew out by about 8 basis points—adding to funding pressures for leveraged players and increasing hedging costs for institutional investors with duration-neutral mandates.

Fundamental indicators reinforced the bearish narrative. Breakeven inflation rates were little changed or slightly higher in nominal terms, but real yields climbed markedly, suggesting that market participants increasingly price a higher-for-longer real policy rate. At the same time, sovereign supply expectations rose after an updated issuance calendar from a major eurozone sovereign signalled larger long-term auctions through H1 2026, tightening the technical backdrop and reducing the bid-to-cover ratios investors were willing to accept.

Analysts warned that if macro data continues to surprise to the upside and central banks remain reluctant to pivot, the repricing could persist and increase volatility across credit and rates markets. Pension funds and liability-driven investors face mark-to-market losses that may trigger de-risking or rebalancing flows, further pressuring long-duration assets. For sovereign borrowers, the higher yield environment translates into materially greater debt servicing costs across the curve over the coming quarters.

Key developments

  • US 10-year Treasury yield jumped ~25 bps intraday to ~4.25%; 2-year rose to ~5.10%, intensifying curve bear flattening
  • Estimated $2.3bn outflows from government bond ETFs and widening swap spreads (~8 bps) amplified market stress and hedging costs
  • Weak long-dated auction demand in the UK and a heavier issuance calendar in the euro area raised term premium and pressured sovereign bond prices

"We're seeing a classic supply-and-demand squeeze: sticky inflation prints and explicit central bank hawkishness have drained the marginal buyer pool just as issuance steps up. That combination is pushing term premia higher and setting the stage for a sustained reassessment of sovereign risk-free rates."

Marco D'Angelo, Head of Rates Strategy, Meridian Fixed Income Research
