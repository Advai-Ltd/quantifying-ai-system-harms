Mortgage-Backed Securities Rally to Six-Month Highs as Fed Rate Cut Expectations Fuel Demand Surge

Mortgage-backed securities are experiencing their strongest performance in over half a year as investors pile into agency MBS amid growing confidence in Federal Reserve rate cuts and improving housing market fundamentals. Spreads have tightened by 15 basis points over the past month, with trading volumes surging 32% as institutional buyers return to the sector, signaling a decisive shift in market sentiment that could support sustained momentum through year-end.

Michael Chen and Sarah Patterson in New York 14/03/2024 09:45:32

The mortgage-backed securities market is experiencing a powerful rally as investor appetite for agency MBS reaches levels not seen since early September, with Fannie Mae 30-year 5.5% coupons climbing 1.2 points to trade at 101.15 this morning. The surge comes amid a confluence of favorable factors including declining Treasury yields, robust demand from asset managers, and reduced prepayment risk that has transformed sector sentiment from cautious to decidedly optimistic. Market participants report that bid-ask spreads have compressed significantly, reflecting the depth of buying interest across the coupon stack.

Trading desks across Wall Street are reporting exceptional liquidity conditions, with daily MBS trading volumes averaging $285 billion over the past two weeks—a 32% increase from the previous month. Bank of America's mortgage trading desk recorded its busiest session in seven months yesterday, processing over $12 billion in agency MBS transactions. The heightened activity reflects not just tactical positioning but a fundamental reassessment of value in the sector, particularly as option-adjusted spreads have tightened to 92 basis points over Treasuries, down from 107 basis points at the beginning of February.

The Federal Reserve's recent commentary has proven particularly catalytic for MBS performance, with market pricing now reflecting a 78% probability of rate cuts beginning in June. This shift has dramatically altered the prepayment calculus that had previously weighed on valuations. Current coupon MBS are benefiting from a 'sweet spot' dynamic where rates remain high enough to suppress refinancing activity—keeping prepayment speeds at a manageable 6.2 CPR—while the securities themselves appreciate on expectations of lower rates ahead. Goldman Sachs upgraded its outlook on agency MBS to overweight last week, citing total return potential of 7-9% over the next twelve months.

Institutional investors who had been underweight MBS are now aggressively rebuilding positions, with money managers adding $14.3 billion in agency MBS exposure during February alone according to data from EPFR Global. Real estate investment trusts specializing in mortgage securities have seen their share prices surge, with the VanEck Mortgage REIT ETF climbing 11.4% year-to-date. Japanese banks and insurance companies, traditional pillars of MBS demand, have returned as active buyers after sitting on the sidelines for much of 2023, with purchases accelerating to $8.7 billion in the past six weeks. Asset liability managers at regional banks are also rotating back into MBS as attractive spread levels and reduced interest rate volatility make the risk-reward proposition increasingly compelling.

The technical backdrop has turned decidedly favorable as well, with net supply dynamics working in investors' favor. The Federal Reserve's ongoing balance sheet runoff has slowed to approximately $35 billion per month in MBS, well below the $60 billion monthly pace that had pressured valuations last year. Meanwhile, new issuance of mortgage debt securities has remained tepid at just $42 billion in February, creating a supply-demand imbalance that continues to support prices. Dealer inventories have fallen to $1.8 trillion, the lowest level since 2021, forcing buyers to bid more aggressively to secure positions.

Housing market fundamentals are providing additional tailwinds, with mortgage delinquency rates dropping to 2.4%, the lowest reading since 2019, while home price appreciation remains positive across 87% of metropolitan markets. Credit performance on underlying loan pools continues to exceed expectations, with serious delinquency rates on Fannie Mae-backed mortgages falling to just 0.52% in the most recent reporting period. This exceptional credit quality has attracted crossover buyers from investment-grade corporate bond funds seeking higher yields with comparable risk profiles. With convexity risks diminishing and carry attractive at current spread levels, the technical and fundamental case for agency MBS has rarely been stronger, positioning the sector for what many strategists believe could be a multi-quarter period of outperformance relative to other fixed income alternatives.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads tightened 15 basis points to 92 bps over Treasuries, reaching the tightest levels since September with Fannie Mae 30-year 5.5% coupons gaining 1.2 points
  • Daily trading volumes surged 32% to average $285 billion over two weeks, reflecting renewed institutional demand and exceptional market liquidity conditions
  • Institutional investors added $14.3 billion in agency MBS exposure during February, with money managers and REITs aggressively rebuilding positions
  • Federal Reserve MBS runoff has moderated to $35 billion monthly while new issuance remains subdued at $42 billion, creating favorable supply-demand dynamics
  • Goldman Sachs upgraded agency MBS to overweight with projected total returns of 7-9% over twelve months amid improving prepayment dynamics

"The alignment of technical, fundamental, and macroeconomic factors supporting agency MBS is the most compelling we've seen in years. With prepayment risk contained, spreads still attractive, and the Fed poised to ease, we're looking at a rare moment where risk-reward decisively favors meaningful overweight positions in the sector."

David Rothstein, Head of Securitized Products Strategy at JP Morgan Securities
