MBS Market Holds Steady as Fed Officials Signal Data-Dependent Approach on Policy

Mortgage-backed securities trading remained within established ranges this week as investors digest mixed economic signals and await clearer direction from Federal Reserve policymakers. Agency MBS spreads held relatively stable while prepayment speeds showed seasonal patterns consistent with historical norms, leaving market participants in a wait-and-see posture ahead of upcoming housing data releases.

Sarah Whitmore and Michael Chen in New York 15/03/2024 14:32:17

The mortgage-backed securities market maintained equilibrium this week as investors navigated a complex landscape of economic indicators and policy signals. Agency MBS spreads to Treasury benchmarks remained within their recent 5-10 basis point trading range, with 30-year current coupon Fannie Mae securities last quoted at 162 basis points over comparable duration Treasuries. Trading volumes came in at approximately $185 billion for the week, slightly below the three-month average of $192 billion.

Prepayment dynamics continued to reflect typical seasonal patterns, with the Mortgage Bankers Association reporting constant prepayment rates (CPR) averaging 6.8% across agency pools, largely in line with analyst expectations. The stable prepayment environment has provided portfolio managers with predictable cash flow patterns, reducing the need for significant hedging adjustments. Lower-coupon MBS pools, particularly those in the 3.5% to 4.5% range, saw minimal refinancing activity as current mortgage rates hover between 6.75% and 7.25% for conventional 30-year loans.

Federal Reserve purchasing activity through its balance sheet reinvestment program remained consistent with recent patterns, with the central bank absorbing approximately $18 billion in principal payments from its MBS holdings during the month. The Fed's measured approach to balance sheet normalization has provided a predictable technical backdrop for the sector, though market participants continue to monitor policy statements for any shifts in the reinvestment framework.

Credit performance metrics across agency MBS pools remained solid, with delinquency rates holding at 2.3% for conventional loans, unchanged from the previous quarter. Non-agency MBS sectors showed differentiated performance, with prime jumbo securitizations experiencing stable spreads while legacy non-qualified mortgage (non-QM) paper saw modest spread widening of 8-12 basis points on lighter trading volumes. Credit risk transfer securities issued by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac traded within established ranges, with M2 tranches yielding approximately 315 basis points over SOFR.

Housing market fundamentals present a mixed picture for MBS investors. Existing home sales volumes remain constrained at approximately 4.0 million annualized units, well below the long-term average of 5.3 million, as the lock-in effect continues to limit inventory. However, home price appreciation has moderated to a 4.2% year-over-year pace according to the latest CoreLogic index, providing stability for mortgage credit quality. Housing affordability metrics remain challenged, with the median home price to median income ratio at 5.8 times, above the historical average of 4.2 times.

Looking ahead, market participants are focusing on upcoming employment data and inflation readings that could influence Federal Reserve policy trajectory. The technical supply-demand balance for MBS appears neutral, with projected net issuance of agency MBS expected to reach $420 billion for the year, offset by Fed reinvestments and steady demand from banks and money managers. Seasonal patterns suggest prepayment speeds may tick marginally higher into the spring home-buying season, though elevated mortgage rates are expected to keep refinancing activity subdued. Analysts note that spread levels appear fairly valued relative to current rate volatility and credit fundamentals, with most expecting rangebound trading conditions to persist in the near term.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads held within 5-10 basis point range with 30-year current coupon securities at 162 basis points over Treasuries
  • Prepayment speeds averaged 6.8% CPR, consistent with seasonal expectations and historical patterns
  • Federal Reserve absorbed $18 billion in MBS principal payments through reinvestment program, maintaining steady balance sheet approach
  • Non-agency prime jumbo MBS spreads remained stable while non-QM paper widened 8-12 basis points on light volumes

"The MBS market is essentially in a holding pattern as investors await more definitive signals on both monetary policy direction and housing market trajectory. Valuations appear reasonable given current fundamentals, but there's limited conviction to drive meaningful spread movements in either direction until we get more clarity on the Fed's next moves."

Jennifer Holloway, Head of Securitized Products Strategy at Meridian Capital Advisors
