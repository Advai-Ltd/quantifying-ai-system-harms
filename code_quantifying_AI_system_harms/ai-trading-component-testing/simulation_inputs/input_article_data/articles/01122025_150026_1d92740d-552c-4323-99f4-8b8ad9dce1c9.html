MBS Market Holds Steady as TBA Flows Offset Mixed Housing Signals

Mortgage-backed securities traded in a narrow range on Monday as subdued agency issuance and steady TBA flows offset mixed housing data. Traders described activity as orderly, with spreads to Treasuries largely unchanged and prepayment assumptions remaining the central modeling variable for investors.

Evelyn Marlowe in New York 01/12/2025 09:30:00

Mortgage-backed securities (MBS) were largely range-bound in early trading, with the 30-year FNMA 3.5% TBA quoted near 101-16 (101.50) and an indicative yield close to 4.20%. Market participants said liquidity remained adequate as dealers absorbed modest primary issuance and portfolio managers rotated through coupon stacks. The 10-year Treasury yield, a primary benchmark for MBS spread measures, was roughly unchanged at 4.05%, leaving option-adjusted spreads (OAS) for agency 30-year cohorts around the mid-teens in basis points.

Volume in the TBA (to-be-announced) market was described as steady rather than robust, with the 15-year coupon showing slightly firmer demand earlier in the session—15-year FNMA 2.5% TBAs were trading near 98-08 (98.25), reflecting a yield of roughly 3.35%. Prepayment metrics remained a focal point for desk strategists: current speeds for seasoned cohorts tracked near PSA 130–140, consistent with neutral seasonal expectations and a mortgage rate environment that has not prompted large-scale refinancing. Investors cited limited directional rate risk and emphasized that convexity and prepay volatility were driving most relative-value decisions.

Supply-side dynamics were orderly: agency issuance was modest compared with recent weeks, and portfolio flows from dealers, insurers and bank-held mortgage portfolios provided a steady bid for benchmark coupons. Long-duration buyers were selective, focusing on higher-coupon pools where negative convexity is better compensated. Several mortgage real estate investment trusts (mREITs) and liability-driven investors were reported to be rebalancing duration without exerting outsized pressure on front-coupon spreads, a pattern consistent with a neutral market posture.

Analysts pointed to a balanced macro backdrop as a reason for calm: recent housing starts and existing-home sales reports delivered mixed signals—purchase activity showed resilience in some regions while refinance activity stayed muted—leaving little impetus for a sustained breakout move in MBS. Market-implied prepay models have been adjusted incrementally rather than dramatically, with modelers citing stable origination windows and unchanged borrower behavior forecasts. Hedging activity, particularly from structured product desks, was described as routine and driven by relative-value opportunities rather than speculative directional bets.

Looking ahead, traders said catalysts that could push MBS out of the current band would include unexpected moves in inflation prints, a materially different Fed communications tone, or a larger-than-anticipated surge in issuance from agency lenders. For now, portfolio managers told clients they expected range-bound performance and recommended monitoring CPR/PSA updates and monthly supply calendars closely, while keeping convexity hedges lightly engaged to manage prepayment uncertainty.

Key developments

  • 30-year FNMA 3.5% TBA near 101-16 (yield ~4.20%); spreads to 10-year Treasuries largely unchanged.
  • Prepayment speeds holding near PSA 130–140, reflecting muted refinance activity and seasonal norms.
  • Agency issuance modest in the near term; dealer and institutional flows providing steady bid across core coupons.

"Absent a significant macro surprise, we expect MBS to trade in a fairly tight band—liquidity is fine, prepayment models are doing the heavy lifting, and relative-value trades are driving most activity rather than outright rate direction."

Laura Chen, Senior MBS Strategist, Crestline Securities
