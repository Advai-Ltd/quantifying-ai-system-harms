Agency MBS Spreads Hold Steady Amid Flat Treasury Movement; Prepayment Risk Contained

The Agency Mortgages Backed Securities (MBS) market experienced a period of equilibrium this week, as spreads against the benchmark Treasury curve remained tightly range-bound. Investors adopted a watchful stance, characterized by moderate trading volumes and a focus on digesting mixed economic indicators and anticipated Federal Reserve policy updates, resulting in sustained neutral performance.

Anya Volkov, Senior Fixed Income Correspondent in New York 18/07/2024 10:45:00

Agency MBS performance remained largely range-bound throughout the past five trading sessions, mirroring the relatively flat movement observed in the broader Treasury market. The spread on current coupon 30-year Fannie Mae TBA traded within a tight 2 basis point window, closing the period near T+125, reflecting stability after minor volatility witnessed earlier in the month. This price action suggests that the market has efficiently priced in the consensus economic outlook, lacking the impetus for either a significant spread widening or tightening event.

A key factor contributing to this stability is the continuation of subdued prepayment speeds. The latest Conditional Prepayment Rate (CPR) reports for recent pools confirmed an average CPR of 8.1%, consistent with the previous month's reading. While housing inventory remains structurally tight, elevated primary mortgage rates continue to suppress meaningful refinance incentive, effectively mitigating duration extension risk for MBS holders in the near term.

Market participants are currently focused squarely on technical dynamics rather than macroeconomic surprises. Moderate new supply in the TBA market has been easily absorbed by steady institutional demand, particularly from depository institutions and money managers seeking high-quality, government-guaranteed duration. This balanced supply-demand environment helps anchor spreads and prevents material drift.

Analyst commentary suggests that the neutral posture will persist until the market receives clearer direction regarding the Federal Reserve's timeline for Quantitative Tightening (QT) adjustments or significant shifts in core inflation data. Until such a catalyst emerges, trading strategies emphasize relative value within the specified pool market rather than broad directional bets on the benchmark TBA curve.

While the near-term outlook appears stable, potential risks, such as unexpected spikes in volatility in short-term rates or a sharp decline in housing affordability impacting purchase volumes, remain on the radar. However, strong underlying credit quality and explicit government guarantees on Agency MBS continue to provide a floor, maintaining the overall neutral risk profile favored by conservative fixed-income investors.

Key developments

  • Current coupon 30-year Fannie Mae TBA spread stabilized, settling near T+125 against the benchmark Treasury.
  • Prepayment speeds (CPR) held steady at 8.1% for the recent reporting cycle, confirming controlled duration risk.
  • Supply and demand dynamics remain balanced, allowing institutional investors to absorb new issue flow without significant spread impact.

"The MBS market is currently in a state of digestion. We are seeing decent demand offsetting moderate supply, but there isn't enough catalyst on either the rate front or the housing front to justify a major directional move. It's a game of prudent yield harvesting, focusing on specific pool characteristics."

Sarah Chen, Head of Fixed Income Strategy at Atlas Capital Management
