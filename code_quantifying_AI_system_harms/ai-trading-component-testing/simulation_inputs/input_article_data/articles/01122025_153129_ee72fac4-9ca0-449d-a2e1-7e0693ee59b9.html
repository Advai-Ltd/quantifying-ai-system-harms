MBS Market Shows Steady Trading as Investors Navigate Mixed Housing Data and Fed Policy Signals

Mortgage-backed securities displayed stable performance this week as market participants digested a combination of moderating home price growth and steady prepayment speeds, while awaiting further clarity on Federal Reserve monetary policy direction. Agency MBS spreads held within recent trading ranges, reflecting balanced supply-demand dynamics.

Rachel Chen and Michael Torres in New York 15/03/2024 14:23:17

The mortgage-backed securities market maintained an even keel through midweek trading, with Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac 30-year current coupon securities hovering around 102-14, largely unchanged from last week's levels. Trading volumes remained consistent with recent patterns as institutional investors continued their methodical approach to portfolio allocation amid an environment characterized by stable interest rate expectations and moderate housing market activity.

Agency MBS spreads to 10-year Treasury notes held steady at approximately 165 basis points for current coupon securities, staying within the 160-170 basis point range that has defined the market since early February. Market technicians noted that this stability reflects a balanced equation between steady Fed MBS portfolio runoff of approximately $35 billion monthly and consistent demand from banks, insurance companies, and asset managers seeking yield in government-backed securities.

Housing market fundamentals continue to present a mixed picture that has contributed to the measured tone in MBS trading. The S&P CoreLogic Case-Shiller National Home Price Index showed a 5.2% year-over-year increase in January, decelerating from the 5.6% pace recorded in December. Meanwhile, existing home sales volumes remain constrained at approximately 4.0 million units on an annualized basis, well below the long-term average of 5.3 million units, as the 'lock-in effect' continues to limit housing turnover.

Prepayment speeds on current production MBS have stabilized near 6-7 CPR (constant prepayment rate), reflecting limited refinancing activity given that roughly 85% of outstanding mortgages carry rates below 6%. This predictable prepayment environment has been viewed favorably by investors seeking duration stability in their fixed income portfolios. Analysts at Thornton Securities noted that the combination of moderate prepayment risk and attractive relative value compared to corporate bonds has maintained steady institutional demand.

The non-agency MBS sector showed similar equilibrium, with prime jumbo securities and credit risk transfer (CRT) notes trading on stable spreads. Freddie Mac's STACR 2023-DNA3 M2 notes traded at spreads of approximately 210 basis points over SOFR, consistent with levels seen over the past month. Credit performance metrics remained solid, with serious delinquency rates on prime mortgages holding near historic lows of 0.8%, while expanded credit loans showed stable but elevated delinquency rates around 3.2%.

Looking ahead, market participants are monitoring several factors that could influence MBS performance in coming months, including potential shifts in Fed balance sheet policy, seasonal patterns in housing activity as spring approaches, and ongoing developments in housing affordability that could impact mortgage origination volumes. For now, the consensus view suggests continued range-bound trading as investors await more definitive catalysts that could drive meaningful spread movement in either direction.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS spreads holding steady at 165 basis points to 10-year Treasuries, within recent 160-170 bps range
  • Prepayment speeds stabilizing at 6-7 CPR as refinancing activity remains limited with 85% of mortgages below 6% rates
  • Federal Reserve MBS portfolio runoff continuing at approximately $35 billion monthly pace with balanced market absorption
  • Home price appreciation moderating to 5.2% year-over-year while existing home sales remain constrained at 4.0 million annual units

"The MBS market is finding its footing in an environment where neither bullish nor bearish factors are dominating. We're seeing consistent two-way flow and healthy market functioning, which suggests investors are comfortable with current valuation levels while remaining watchful for any shifts in the macro backdrop."

Jennifer Walsh, Head of MBS Strategy at Thornton Securities
