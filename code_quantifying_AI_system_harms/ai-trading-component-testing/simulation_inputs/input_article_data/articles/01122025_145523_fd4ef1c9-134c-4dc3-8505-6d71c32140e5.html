Global Government Bond Sell-Off Intensifies as Yields Spike on Persistent Inflation and Heavy Supply

Government bond markets turned decisively bearish Friday as a wave of hawkish central bank signals, stickier-than-expected inflation metrics and heavier-than-anticipated sovereign supply drove yields sharply higher. The rout pushed benchmark yields to multi-month highs, lifted borrowing costs across the economy and prompted broad outflows from fixed-income funds, leaving investors scrambling to reposition for a higher-rate regime.

Eleanor H. Price in London 01/12/2025 14:32:10

A pronounced sell-off in government bonds accelerated across major markets on Monday, with benchmark yields climbing to levels not seen since mid-2024. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield jumped about 32 basis points to 4.15%, the UK 10-year gilt rose roughly 38 bps to 4.05%, and the German 10-year Bund was up 24 bps at 2.25%. Prices fell steeply as investors reassessed duration exposure amid a flurry of data and policy signals suggesting that central banks may keep rates higher for longer.

Market participants attributed the move to a combination of stickier inflation indicators and firmer central bank rhetoric. Friday's release of regional CPI proxies and a higher-than-forecast core services inflation print in the U.S. reignited concerns that underlying price pressures remain persistent. Traders also digested minutes and speeches from several central banks that leaned hawkish, prompting a material repricing of terminal rate expectations — the market now places a higher probability on further policy tightening or delayed cuts in 2026.

Technical factors amplified the sell-off. Primary dealers reported weak demand in recent sovereign auctions: the U.S. Treasury's 30-year syndication showed a tail of about 12 bps versus the when-issued curve and a bid-to-cover near 2.15, signaling strained primary demand. Treasury officials signaled an increase in Q1 issuance plans, with an additional $125 billion of long-term supply penciled into the calendar, while the UK Debt Management Office surprised markets with a larger gilt auction schedule. Core bond ETFs recorded net outflows of about $5.7 billion Friday, and repo and secured funding rates widened, adding pressure to the interdealer market and reducing liquidity at the long end.

The sell-off's transmission to broader financial conditions was swift. Investment-grade credit spreads widened roughly 12 bps on the day, bringing yields on high-quality corporates to levels that pushed benchmark mortgage rates up by an estimated 20–30 bps, lifting average quoted 30-year fixed mortgage pricing toward the high-6% area in many jurisdictions. The U.S. dollar strengthened on the repricing, while equities pared earlier gains as higher yields increased discount rates and hurt valuation-sensitive sectors.

Analysts said further volatility is likely in the near term as markets digest next week's slate of macro releases and central bank commentary. Several sell-side desks have turned constructively bearish on duration, recommending shorter-duration positioning, floating-rate structures and selective use of inflation-linked securities for investors seeking to hedge against further real-rate appreciation. Still, strategists cautioned that a disorderly move could attract opportunistic buyers at technical levels, and much will depend on incoming payrolls, CPI components and the tone of official guidance.

Key developments

  • U.S. 10-year Treasury yield jumps ~32 bps to 4.15% amid global repricing of rate expectations.
  • Treasury signals an incremental $125bn of long-term issuance for Q1; UK increases gilt auction sizes, pressuring primary demand.
  • Core bond ETFs record approximately $5.7bn of outflows; primary dealers report weakened bid-to-cover and larger auction tails.

"The market is finally waking up to the persistence of real rates — investors have under-allocated for a world where central banks delay easing. That combination of higher term premia and swelling supply is a toxic mix for bond holders."

Marcus Lin, Rates Strategist, Northbridge Capital
