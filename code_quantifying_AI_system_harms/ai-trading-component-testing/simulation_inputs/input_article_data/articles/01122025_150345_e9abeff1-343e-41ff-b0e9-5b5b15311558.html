Broad MBS Sell-Off Accelerates as Duration and Credit Risks Force Forced Sellers

Mortgage-backed securities markets slid sharply as a rapid rise in long-term yields, a surge in bank-originated supply and signs of rising delinquencies in non-agency pools combined to widen spreads and drive a wave of mark-to-market losses. The rout has amplified duration extension and negative convexity fears, leaving investors reassessing allocations to both agency and non-agency MBS.

Claire D. Mercer, Senior Markets Correspondent in New York 01/12/2025 09:15:00

Mortgage-backed securities (MBS) across both agency and non-agency sectors came under heavy pressure Tuesday as benchmark Treasury yields jumped and dealer inventories ballooned. Agency TBAs fell roughly 1.9% on the day, while benchmark 30-year fixed-rate coupon MBS saw yields spike by about 85 basis points to the mid-4% area. Traders said the combination of rising real rates and persistent inflation expectations has forced rapid repricing in MBS, with 30-year FNMA 3.5% coupons widening to roughly 160 basis points over Treasuries, compared with 90–100 bps a month ago.

Market participants pointed to an acceleration in duration extension as prepayment speeds evaporate. With mortgage rates remaining elevated, refinance activity has collapsed and prepayment assumptions have been revised materially lower; servicers and portfolio managers are now marking models to a slower-paydown curve, which increases effective duration and magnifies losses when rates rise. The negative convexity profile of many agency coupons means that in a rising-rate environment investors are absorbing outsized price declines relative to parallel-move expectations.

Supply-side dynamics compounded the stress. Several regional banks and mortgage originators, facing margin compression and liquidity pressures, dumped seasoned mortgage pools into the secondary market, swelling dealer balance sheets. Brokers reported a flood of bank-originated GNMA and FHLMC pools for sale, forcing dealers to hedge aggressively into the Treasury complex and driving cross-market selling. TBA basis weakness was particularly acute in lower-coupon buckets, where liquidity thinned and basis moves amplified mark-to-market volatility for leveraged holders.

Non-agency RMBS and credit-sensitive tranches felt the brunt of the credit repricing. Traders noted downgrades in selected mezzanine pieces and a notable uptick in 60+ day delinquencies in higher-LTV vintage cohorts from the 2018–2021 origination windows. Several boutique servicers issued cautionary statements about elevated advance funding needs, and stress in subprime and option-ARM legacy pools drove spread widening of 180–250 bps in lower-rated slices. Institutional investors said the risk of rising loss severity and servicing friction is prompting valuation haircuts across credit stacks.

Analysts said the near-term outlook remains firmly bearish until there is clear evidence of easing inflation or a re-acceleration of prepayments. Strategy desks are recommending underweight positions in 15- and 30-year nominal coupon MBS and favoring shorter-duration, higher-coupon agency paper or Treasury bills as temporary liquidity preserves. Several market-makers flagged potential technical bottoms only if 30-year coupon spreads trade back beyond 200 bps over Treasuries, a level that they said would begin to compensate investors for extension and credit risk in the current macro backdrop.

Key developments

  • Agency TBA prices fell ~1.9% intraday as 30-year MBS yields jumped ~85 bps; FNMA 30-year 3.5% coupon spreads widened to ~160 bps over Treasuries.
  • Non-agency RMBS spreads widened 180–250 bps on select mezzanine tranches amid a pick-up in 60+ day delinquencies for 2018–2021 vintages and servicer funding warnings.
  • Surge in bank-originated pool supply and dealer inventory accumulation forced aggressive hedging flows, amplifying mark-to-market losses for leveraged MBS holders.

"We're seeing a classic extension shock — duration risk is biting hard and the market is repricing MBS for a longer, higher-rate environment. Until prepayments meaningfully pick up or supply technicals normalize, expect additional spread widening and volatility."

Eleanor Marks, Head of MBS Strategy, Riverton Securities
