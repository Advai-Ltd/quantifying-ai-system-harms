Agency MBS Hold Ground as Yields Stabilize; Prepayment Signals Show Modest Uptick

Agency mortgage-backed securities traded with little directional bias on Monday as Treasury yields steadied and investor attention focused on evolving prepayment dynamics and steady GSE issuance. Market participants described valuations as broadly range-bound, with option-adjusted spreads and effective durations reflecting a neutral stance ahead of key economic releases later in the week.

Evelyn Martinez in New York 01/12/2025 09:30:00

Agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS) were largely unchanged in early trading as benchmark U.S. Treasury yields held near recent levels. 30-year FNMA coupons in the 3.5%–4.0% range traded within a 2–4 basis point band, with price action described by dealers as “quiet and two-sided.” The 10-year Treasury yield was quoted around 3.72%, leaving 30-year MBS option-adjusted spreads (OAS) near the mid-80s on a basis-point basis relative to comparable Treasuries, a profile market participants characterized as neutral.

Market drivers remained mixed but contained: mortgage applications slipped marginally in the latest week while refinance activity showed a modest improvement for borrowers with lower coupon stacks. Servicer-reported conditional prepayment rates (CPR) for seasoned 30-year 3.5% pools ticked up to roughly 16% from 14% month-over-month, a move that market analysts termed a modest acceleration but not a structural shift. Seasoning and geographic composition continued to influence relative performance across coupons and vintages.

On the supply side, GSE issuance weighed in as expected; Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac placed a steady flow of passthroughs that dealers absorbed without dislocation. Dealer balance sheets remained light, and hedge flows from real-money accounts — pension funds and insurance buyers — provided a steady bid, offsetting stop-start demand from hedge funds. The Federal Reserve’s MBS reinvestment program remained neutral in tone, with no new directional signals announced, leaving the market reliant on private sector demand for incremental clearing.

Valuation metrics painted a balanced picture. The average effective duration for the 30-year coupon cohort sat around 3.1 years, while 15-year cohorts exhibited durations closer to 1.6 years. Option-adjusted spreads held in the low-to-mid 80s bps for agency 30-year pools; convexity profiles and negative convexity risks were priced in but not dominating trader commentary. Relative value between coupon stacks favored modestly lower-coupon pools given current CPR assumptions, but pricing gaps remained within historical ranges.

Analysts and portfolio managers framed near-term prospects as range-bound, citing an event calendar that includes employment data and a consumer price update that could reintroduce volatility. Tactical calls emphasized selective buying on shallow spread wideners and defensive positioning in shorter-duration MBS for accounts sensitive to extension risk. Market participants reiterated that absent a clear move in Treasuries or a surprise in housing fundamentals, agency MBS were set to trade with limited conviction.

Key developments

  • Option-adjusted spreads for 30-year agency MBS remained near mid-80s bps relative to Treasuries, largely unchanged on the session.
  • Servicer-reported CPRs for seasoned 30-year 3.5% pools rose to ~16% from ~14% month-over-month, signaling a modest uptick in prepayment speeds without materially altering duration profiles.
  • GSE issuance continued at steady weekly volumes; Federal Reserve reinvestment policy unchanged, leaving private-sector demand to absorb new supply.

""At current levels, agency MBS look fairly valued — spreads are compact but not stretched, and prepayment metrics are edging higher in a measured way. Investors are treating the market as range-bound until clearer macro direction emerges,""

Sarah Kim, Senior MBS Strategist, Harbor Capital Markets
