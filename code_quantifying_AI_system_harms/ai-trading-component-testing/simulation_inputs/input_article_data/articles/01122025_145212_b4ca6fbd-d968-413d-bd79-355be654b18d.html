Global Government Bonds Rally as Demand Surges, Sending Yields Sharply Lower

Government bonds across major markets rallied strongly as softer macro data and a dovish central bank tilt triggered a wave of safe‑haven buying. Benchmark yields fell across the curve, primary auction demand remained robust and ETF inflows accelerated, signalling growing investor confidence in long-duration assets and underscoring a bullish backdrop for sovereign debt.

Emma R. Caldwell, Senior Markets Correspondent in London 01/12/2025 09:15:00

Government bond markets staged a broad-based rally on Monday, with benchmark yields plunging as investors digested a constellation of weaker-than-expected activity data and conciliatory central bank commentary. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield fell about 20 basis points to 3.40%, while the 30-year slipped 28 basis points to 3.88%. In Europe, the UK 10-year gilt dropped roughly 22 basis points to 3.15% and the German 10-year bund eased 15 basis points to 2.05%. The move reflected a rapid rotation into duration as traders priced a higher probability of rate cuts and an extended period of easier monetary policy.

Technical and flow dynamics amplified the rally. Primary auctions showed resilient demand: a $58bn U.S. reopening of 10-year notes attracted a bid-to-cover of 2.9x and saw strong direct participation, while the UK sold £8bn of 30-year gilts with a 3.1x cover ratio. Exchange-traded funds specialising in government debt reported net inflows of $4.2bn globally on the session, with long-duration funds seeing the lion’s share. Pension funds and insurers were cited by dealers as notable buyers, rebalancing portfolios toward higher duration after last quarter’s volatility.

Macro developments reinforced the bullish narrative. Headline inflation readings cooled more than expected in several regions, with U.S. 10-year breakevens slipping to 2.05% and core monthly CPI prints running softer than consensus. At the same time, recent PMI and retail sales surprises signalled slowing momentum in the real economy. Traders interpreted commentary from a number of central bankers — emphasising data dependence and openness to easing if growth softened — as tilting policy expectations toward earlier and more sizeable rate cuts than previously priced, supporting further yield compression.

Market structure changes also favoured bonds. Swap spreads narrowed by 6–10 basis points on improved funding conditions and strong primary dealer distribution, and real-money accounts increased duration exposure amid attractive convexity profiles. Curve dynamics were notable: the long end outperformed short maturities, producing a modest flattening in the U.S. curve as 30-year yields fell more sharply than 2-year notes. Volatility metrics declined; the MOVE index fell roughly 12% on the session, reflecting reduced near-term rate uncertainty.

Analysts say the current setup supports a constructive outlook for government bonds, though they warn of potential volatility around upcoming macro prints and fiscal supply calendars. 'The combination of cooling inflation expectations, supportive central bank language and renewed real-money demand makes the case for owning duration across the curve,' said Jonathan Meyers, head of fixed income strategy at Riverview Asset Management. Strategists at Oxford Fixed Income Research raised their 12-month target for the U.S. 10-year to 3.10%, citing a higher probability of three Fed cuts next year and persistent demand from liability-driven investors. For investors, the rally has reignited conversations about duration allocation, hedging strategies and the relative appeal of nominal versus real yields.

Key developments

  • Benchmark yields fell sharply: U.S. 10-year down ~20 bps to 3.40%, U.K. 10-year down ~22 bps to 3.15%, German 10-year down ~15 bps to 2.05%, signaling broad-based demand for sovereign debt.
  • Primary market resilience: $58bn U.S. 10-year reopening bid-to-cover 2.9x; UK 30-year gilt issuance covered 3.1x — dealers report strong direct and real-money participation.
  • Flows and positioning: Global government bond ETFs recorded $4.2bn in inflows; swap spreads narrowed and the MOVE volatility index fell ~12%, supporting a bullish duration environment.

"'We're seeing a classic duration rotation — real-money buyers stepping in as inflation signals cool and central bank rhetoric softens. That dynamic should support further gains for long-dated sovereigns over the next several quarters,'"

Jonathan Meyers, Head of Fixed Income Strategy, Riverview Asset Management
