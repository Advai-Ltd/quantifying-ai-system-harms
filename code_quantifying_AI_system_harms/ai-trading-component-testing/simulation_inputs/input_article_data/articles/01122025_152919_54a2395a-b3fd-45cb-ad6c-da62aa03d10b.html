Mortgage-Backed Securities Rally as Fed Rate Cut Bets Fuel Strongest Quarter Since 2020

Agency mortgage-backed securities posted their strongest quarterly gains in over three years as declining Treasury yields and optimistic Fed rate cut expectations drove spreads to multi-year tights. Institutional investors are flooding back into the MBS market, with analysts predicting continued outperformance as housing fundamentals stabilize and prepayment risks diminish amid elevated mortgage rates.

Michael Chen and Sarah Patterson in New York 15/11/2024 14:32:18

Mortgage-backed securities are experiencing a remarkable resurgence, with agency MBS outperforming Treasuries by 127 basis points in the third quarter as investors embrace the asset class with renewed conviction. The Bloomberg MBS Index gained 3.8% over the period, marking the strongest quarterly performance since the fourth quarter of 2020, as option-adjusted spreads tightened to 142 basis points over Treasuries—the narrowest level in 18 months.

The rally has been fueled by a confluence of positive factors including substantial Federal Reserve balance sheet stabilization, reduced supply pressures, and improving technical dynamics. Fed holdings of agency MBS have declined at a slower pace than anticipated, falling by just $18 billion in October compared to $35 billion monthly runoff seen earlier in the year. Meanwhile, net issuance of agency MBS dropped 23% year-over-year to $890 billion, creating a favorable supply-demand imbalance that has supported valuations across the capital structure.

Institutional investors are significantly increasing allocations to the sector, with money managers adding $47 billion in MBS exposure during the third quarter according to Federal Reserve flow of funds data. Real money accounts, including pension funds and insurance companies, have been particularly active buyers of current coupon and higher-coupon securities, attracted by yields exceeding 6.5% on 30-year conventional MBS while prepayment risks remain historically low. Bank portfolios have also shown signs of stabilization after two years of persistent selling pressure.

Housing market fundamentals are providing additional tailwinds for MBS valuations. Home prices have appreciated 4.3% year-over-year according to the latest Case-Shiller Index, while mortgage delinquency rates remain near historic lows at just 3.2% for conventional loans. Credit performance has exceeded expectations across all vintage years, with serious delinquency rates for 2022 and 2023 originations tracking well below pre-pandemic norms despite the rapid rate increase cycle.

The prepayment outlook has also turned decidedly favorable for MBS investors. With the average outstanding mortgage rate sitting at 3.8% while current origination rates hover around 7.25%, refinancing incentives remain minimal for the vast majority of borrowers. Conditional prepayment rates for 30-year conventional MBS have fallen to just 4.8% annually, providing investors with enhanced yield stability and extended duration profiles. This dynamic has made higher-coupon MBS particularly attractive, with specified pool premiums for low loan balance and investor property collateral reaching their highest levels since 2019.

Derivatives markets are signaling continued optimism, with options activity showing significant positioning for further spread tightening. Interest rate volatility has declined sharply, with the MOVE index falling 34% from its March peaks, reducing the negative convexity concerns that have historically weighed on MBS valuations. Swap spread dynamics have also improved materially, with 10-year swap spreads widening to +48 basis points—levels that historically correlate with strong MBS relative value.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS option-adjusted spreads tightened to 142 basis points over Treasuries, the narrowest level in 18 months, as institutional demand surged
  • Net MBS issuance declined 23% year-over-year to $890 billion, while Fed runoff slowed to $18 billion monthly from $35 billion earlier this year
  • Conditional prepayment rates fell to just 4.8% annually as the vast majority of borrowers remain out-of-the-money for refinancing at current rate levels
  • Money managers increased MBS allocations by $47 billion in Q3, with pension funds and insurers leading the buying activity across higher-coupon sectors

"We're seeing the most constructive setup for agency MBS in years, with technical factors, credit quality, and rate dynamics all aligning favorably. The combination of reduced supply, stable Fed policy, and minimal prepayment risk is creating a compelling risk-adjusted return profile that institutional investors simply cannot ignore at current spread levels."

Jennifer Morrison, Head of Securitized Products at Bridgewater Capital Management
