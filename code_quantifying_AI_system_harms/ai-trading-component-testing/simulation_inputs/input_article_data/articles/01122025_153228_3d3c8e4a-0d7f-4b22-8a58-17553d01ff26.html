Agency MBS Spreads Hold Steady as Fed Portfolio Runoff Continues at Measured Pace

Mortgage-backed securities markets are displaying stability this week as trading desks digest the Federal Reserve's ongoing balance sheet normalization while prepayment speeds remain within expected ranges. Market participants report balanced two-way flow as investors assess the interplay between housing market dynamics and monetary policy positioning.

Michael Chen and Sarah Richardson in New York 15/03/2024 14:32:18

The agency mortgage-backed securities market posted modest movements during Thursday's trading session, with the Fannie Mae 30-year 6.0% coupon closing at 101-08, up 4 ticks from Wednesday's levels. Option-adjusted spreads on current coupon MBS widened by 2 basis points to 143 basis points over comparable Treasury securities, reflecting neither particular strength nor weakness in the sector.

Trading volumes across the TBA market registered $247 billion in specified pool trades and $189 billion in to-be-announced transactions, figures that align closely with the three-month average. Primary dealers reported balanced customer activity, with asset managers and banks showing consistent but unremarkable participation across the coupon stack. The Fannie Mae 5.5% and 6.0% coupons attracted the most liquidity, while lower coupons traded with typical spreads to their higher-yielding counterparts.

Prepayment expectations remain anchored near current levels, with market consensus projecting constant prepayment rates in the 5-7% range for conventional 30-year securities. Mortgage servicers have reported refinancing activity consistent with recent months, as current mortgage rates hovering near 6.9% provide limited incentive for borrowers with existing loans below 6% to refinance. The weighted average coupon of outstanding agency MBS stands at approximately 3.8%, suggesting minimal refinancing pressure in the near term.

The Federal Reserve's systematic balance sheet reduction continues to influence market dynamics, with the central bank allowing up to $35 billion in agency MBS to roll off its holdings monthly. This has proceeded without disruption, as private sector demand has absorbed the securities entering the market. Major banks have maintained their MBS allocations largely unchanged, with capital requirements and liquidity coverage ratios supporting steady demand for agency securities.

Fannie Mae reported issuing $42.3 billion in single-family MBS during February, a figure representing a 3% increase from January but remaining within the typical seasonal range. Credit performance across agency MBS pools continues to show stable delinquency rates of 1.84% for conventional loans, unchanged from the previous quarter. Serious delinquency rates for Fannie Mae-backed mortgages held at 0.51%, reflecting the ongoing normalization following pandemic-era forbearance programs.

Market technicals suggest a balanced supply-demand environment, with dealer inventories of $78 billion sitting near the middle of the historical range. The basis between specified pools and TBA contracts remains within normal parameters, indicating neither scarcity nor oversupply of deliverable collateral. Forward trading activity shows typical patterns, with most volume concentrated in the front three settlement months.

Looking ahead, market participants are monitoring upcoming employment data and inflation readings for potential impacts on the Fed's policy trajectory, though expectations remain for a measured approach to both interest rate policy and balance sheet management. The housing market's gradual adjustment to higher rates continues to provide a relatively predictable backdrop for MBS performance, with most analysts projecting continued range-bound trading absent significant macroeconomic surprises.

Key developments

  • Agency MBS option-adjusted spreads widened 2 basis points to 143 bps over Treasuries in balanced trading conditions
  • Federal Reserve's MBS portfolio runoff proceeding at $35 billion monthly pace without market disruption
  • Prepayment speeds holding steady in 5-7% CPR range as refinancing incentives remain limited
  • Fannie Mae February issuance of $42.3 billion represents modest 3% monthly increase within seasonal norms

"We're seeing orderly markets with adequate liquidity across the coupon stack. Investor positioning appears neutral, and there's no particular pressure in either direction. The fundamentals support current spread levels, and we expect this equilibrium to persist barring significant changes to the rate outlook or housing fundamentals."

Tom Weatherford, Head of MBS Trading at Prescott Financial Group
