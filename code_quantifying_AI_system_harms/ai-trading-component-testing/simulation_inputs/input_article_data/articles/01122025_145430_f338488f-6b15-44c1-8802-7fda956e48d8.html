Government Bond Rout Deepens as Yields Surge on Sticky Inflation, Heavy Issuance

Global government bond markets logged sharp losses as benchmark yields climbed across maturities, driven by persistent inflationary signals, hawkish central bank rhetoric and a renewed wave of sovereign issuance. The shift has forced portfolio re-pricing, widened credit spreads and raised questions about duration exposure for institutional investors.

Laura Bennett, Senior Markets Correspondent in New York 01/12/2025 09:27:00

Government bonds sold off aggressively on Monday as benchmark yields jumped to multi-month highs, reflecting a more entrenched inflation outlook and renewed pressure from sovereign debt supply. The US 10-year Treasury yield spiked about 45 basis points to 4.20%, while the 2-year rose 68 basis points to 5.12%, amplifying losses across the curve. European equivalents moved in lockstep: the German 10-year Bund climbed roughly 28 basis points to 2.58% and the UK 10-year Gilt rose some 35 basis points to 3.85%. The move erased several weeks of gains and pushed duration-sensitive assets lower.

Market participants attributed the rout to a combination of stronger-than-expected inflation signals and a more hawkish tone from central bankers. US headline CPI data released last week showed a monthly rise of 0.4% and a 12-month increase of 3.6%, keeping core inflation persistently above central bank targets. Fed officials, responding to the data, reiterated the case for restrictive policy 'until clear progress' is visible, prompting traders to price in a more prolonged path of elevated policy rates and higher term premiums across sovereign curves.

Technical factors compounded the repricing. The Treasury's latest nominal auction series drew weaker-than-usual indirect demand, and primary dealers were left to absorb a larger share of issuance, according to market sources. ETF outflows accelerated — fixed-income exchange-traded products saw net redemptions of $6.2bn on the day — and pension funds signalled mark-to-market losses as long-duration holdings recorded significant markdowns. The inversion between 2-year and 10-year yields deepened, sharpening concerns about liquidity and rollover risk for leveraged strategies.

Supply dynamics are also weighing on prices. The Treasury Department's updated quarterly refunding plan signalled an increase in 10- and 30-year issuance to meet fiscal funding needs, a move that investors said would add fresh downward pressure on prices absent stronger overseas demand. Across Europe, sovereigns scheduled sizeable supply in coming weeks, while the UK announced a larger-than-expected gilt issuance package to finance an expanding deficit. Rating-watch commentary from boutique agency Atlas Ratings flagged higher vulnerability to refinancing stress in several smaller sovereigns, further denting risk appetite for long-dated paper.

Analysts said the bond sell-off is likely to persist in the near term as market participants re-price terminal rates and weight the impact of sustained issuance. 'We think the repricing is far from over — duration risk is being re-assessed and real-money investors are being forced to mark down positions,' said Harrison Cole, Head of Rates Strategy at Meridian Capital. Several sell-side strategists advised reducing duration and increasing cash holdings, while some hedge funds have deployed steepener trades to benefit from the combination of higher short-term yields and a still-tight front end of the curve.

Key developments

  • US 10-year Treasury yield up ~45 bps to 4.20%; 2-year Treasury up ~68 bps to 5.12%, amplifying curve inversion and duration losses.
  • Weak indirect demand at recent Treasury auctions and accelerated ETF outflows (approx. $6.2bn in redemptions) increase pressure on secondary market liquidity.
  • Sovereign supply plans scaled up — US and UK announced larger issuance schedules — heightening long-term yields and forcing re-pricing across global government curves.
